A ria is a drowned river valley — a geological feature created when the sea level rose after the last ice age, flooding the lower reaches of rivers that had carved their channels into the granite of Armorica. The rias of Brittany — the Aulne, the Odet, the Blavet — are the most dramatic expressions of the unmeasurable coastline. Each ria is an inlet within an inlet: the main channel branches into tributaries, which branch into sub-tributaries, which branch into creeks, which branch into rills. The compass that guides the navigator through a ria must account for turns at every scale.

This branching structure runs back into itself. The word comes from the Latin *recurrere*, to run back, and that is precisely what the ria does: it runs back from the sea into the land, repeating its branching pattern at progressively finer scales. The kelp follows the branching, growing on the granite walls of each subsidiary channel, tracing the impossible boundary between salt water and stone. The compass bearing changes with every branch point, as if the navigator were traversing a tree rather than a coastline.

The length of a ria defies the ruler more completely than the open coast. Where the exposed granite headlands refuse a certain kind of measurement, the interior of a ria refuses it more absolutely. The branching multiplies the boundary. The kelp-covered granite of each branch contributes its perimeter to the total. At the finest scale, the individual crystals of the granite — feldspar, quartz, mica — create a microscopic coastline of their own, a length determined by the mineral structure.

Running back into itself is the operation that creates such coastlines. Take a shape, apply a rule, repeat. Mandelbrot's set is generated by returning again and again to an equation. The Koch snowflake is generated by replacing each line segment with four segments. The rias of Brittany are generated by water returning to its own work: water carves granite, creating channels; the channels guide more water, which carves deeper channels; the compass of the navigator spirals inward, following the pattern inward to whatever scale the vessel permits.

This book also runs back into itself. Each chapter applies the same rule at a finer scale. The structure returns. The compass points inward.
