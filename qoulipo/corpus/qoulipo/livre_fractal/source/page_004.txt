Benoit Mandelbrot asked the question in 1967, but the chalk had been asking it for two hundred million years. His paper carried a title no respectable geographer would have written: how long is the coast of Britain? The answer he offered was not a number but a relationship — a law that connected the length of your ruler to the length you discovered. As the ruler shrank, the coast grew. The rate of growing was not whimsical. It obeyed a power law, and the exponent of that law was a number between one and two, a number that Euclid had no name for.

Mandelbrot's example was Britain because Britain was where the question first announced itself in plain language. But the chalk cliffs of Dover face the chalk cliffs of Cap Blanc-Nez, and the same storm that devours the one devours the other. Salt enters both, freezes in winter, expands, splits the chalk on either side of the Channel with the same patience and the same arithmetic. The compass that Mandelbrot's predecessors carried gave the coast of Britain at twelve thousand five hundred kilometers. A walker's map gave it at nineteen thousand. A surveyor's chain at the spring tide gave it at twenty-five thousand and rising. The same will be true of any chalk shore.

The discovery — and Mandelbrot named it discovery, not invention — was that the lengthening was not chaos. It was pattern. The roughness of a coast at the scale of a hundred kilometers echoed its roughness at one kilometer, which echoed its roughness at one meter. Every storm carved roughness at every scale. Every grain of salt did its work at the smallest joint and at the largest. The coast was an object whose intricacy remained constant as you walked toward it.

The Breton coast measures about 1.25 in this new dimension. The Norman chalk measures lower — perhaps 1.10 — because the chalk is more uniform, less carved by salt and storm into a thousand competing geometries. But both are of this nature. Every coast is. What Mandelbrot asked of Britain, I ask of France. And the answer, as ever, depends on how close you are willing to stand.
