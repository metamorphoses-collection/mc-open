The mistral is not an Atlantic storm. It does not gather over a thousand kilometers of fetch and arrive heavy with rain. The mistral is dry — a drainage wind that pours down the Rhone valley from the cold high country of the Massif Central, accelerates through the gap between the Alps and the Cevennes, and bursts onto the Mediterranean coast with the suddenness of a sluice opening. The compass needle steadies on 340 degrees and does not waver. The sky goes that hard, polished blue that the Provencaux call the mistral sky. The sea goes white.

I weigh the question of where to anchor. The chart shows a dozen creeks and roadsteads between Marseille and Toulon. Each is sheltered from one direction and exposed to another. In a southeasterly the harbor of Cassis is calm; in the mistral it is a death-trap, the wind funneling between the cliffs and lifting the chop into the cockpit. The harbor of Sanary is sheltered from the north; the mistral arrives there merely as a stiff cold wind under a peaceful sky, and the kelp on the breakwater stones is hardly disturbed.

I bring the vessel into Sanary at the falling barometer's first warning. The dune at the head of the bay is small but well-vegetated, and the cliffs on the seaward side break the wind before it touches the water. I drop the anchor in five meters over good holding sand, pay out chain to four-to-one ratio, then set the second anchor at thirty degrees off the first. The cartographic convention names this kind of anchorage "good in moderate winds, exposed in westerlies." The mistral is northerly, not westerly; the convention permits sleep tonight.

But the mistral is nested in itself. Within the steady three-day blow are gusts that arrive at twice the steady wind speed. Within those gusts are eddies, lifted off the cliff edges, that strike from any quarter for seconds at a time. The compass on the binnacle holds; the wind-vane at the masthead does not. I sit out the night below decks, listening to the chain grumble against the bow-roller every time a particular eddy arrives, and I write in the log: anchored Sanary, mistral, holding, the cliffs and the dune doing the work the sextant could not do.
