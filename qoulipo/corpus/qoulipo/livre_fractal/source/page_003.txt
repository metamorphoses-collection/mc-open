Consider the ruler. It assumes that the object will sit still, that the object will have a definite extent, that as the ruler shrinks the measured length will converge to a single number. For a table the assumption holds. For a coastline it fails — not slightly, not partially, but completely.

I, Pascal, have built instruments that do not lie. The Pascaline adds and subtracts without arithmetic error. The barometer reports the pressure of the atmosphere with a precision unknown to my predecessors. I have proved that the vacuum exists, that air has weight, that probability submits to calculation. Yet here on the chalk shore between Calais and Boulogne, with the storm gathering and the salt running into the gaps of my coat, I confess: the ruler defeats me, and the defeat is not a problem of the ruler but of the world it is asked to measure.

Bring the brass compass close to the cliff. From the dune above, the chalk wall is smooth — a plane of white running for half a league. Walk to its base. The chalk is fissured by salt-rot, pocked where the storm has driven spray into hairline joints and the salt crystallized and pried the joints wider. Each fissure is a deviation that lengthens the wall. Set the compass to one meter and walk the cliff's foot. Each fissure now demands to be entered, and within each fissure are smaller fissures, and within those still smaller, until the eye gives up and the hand cannot reach in.

This is not a failure of accuracy. This is what accuracy reveals. The more carefully you look, the more coast you find. The compass narrows; the number swells. There is no scale at which the chalk discloses its true length, because the chalk has no true length. It has only a way of growing under whatever attention you bring to it.

I, who wagered on infinity in matters of the soul, find infinity here on the shore — not in the heavens nor in the smallest division of the line, but in the space between the storm and the chalk, between the salt and the seam, between the ruler and the shore that will not consent to be ruled.
