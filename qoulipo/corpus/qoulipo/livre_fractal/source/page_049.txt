South of Bayonne the shore turns abruptly. The long sand of the Landes — three hundred kilometers of nearly straight beach, the dimension barely greater than one — gives way at Hendaye to granite of a different age and a different color than Brittany's: pinker, more iron-stained, but breaking under the same laws. The compass swings as the coast bends. The tide, which had run almost parallel to the beach for days, now strikes the cliffs at an angle and produces, in the moment of striking, the first true Atlantic surf I have seen since leaving Finistere.

The dimension returns. I have not had to compute it; the shore has computed it for me. The walking length between two small headlands at low tide and the walking length between the same two headlands at higher attention are different by precisely the ratio that the granite of Brittany imposed in the first chapter of my journey. The kelp on the rocks wears the same dark fringe. The salt has carved the same kind of solution pits into the iron-pink stone as it carved into the grey granite of Pointe du Raz. The number that survives every measurement is back where it was, give or take the small margin by which Basque granite is harder than Breton.

The compass points west and slightly north. Spain begins three kilometers down the road. The Pyrenees rise behind me, their seaward foot dropping into the surf as a coastline of yet another character — a coast where the mountain itself is the shore, where there is no littoral zone because the granite plunges directly into deep water and the tide can find no beach to rise upon.

I have walked five chapters of coast and ended where I began, in the sense that the coastline is willing to begin again. The shore at Hendaye is not the shore at Dunkirk; the granite is not chalk; the tide is greater here than it was on the Channel; the storms come from a different quarter. Yet the law that confounded the ruler at Dunkirk confounds it equally here. The law has not been deduced. The law has been walked. And the walking, like the coast, is not finished, and will not be finished, and will go on as long as there are men with rulers and a sea to ruin them.
