The smoking mountain had exhaled its sullen breath across three generations of lifetimes before the clan's visionary first ventured toward the caldera's brink. Where the earth's molten heart welled upward, no worked stone held purpose—flint blades meant nothing against rivers of liquid rock that crept in sluggish torrents, cooling into black glass that spread its brittle plains beneath the endless cold. The people gathered fragments still radiating their furnace heat, crushing them to powder that stained the limestone galleries with colours surpassing even the rust-hued earths for their endurance. Acrid vapours twisted upward through cracks in the newborn stone, and the great tusked herds turned aside from this realm, their ancient knowing alert to the coal-red gleam visible against the ice-field's distant margin when darkness fell.

Within the uttermost recess of the cavern, the visionary traced the mountain's likeness using the crimson dust of eruption and the yellow bloom of sulphur, rendering the caldera as an abyss that devoured the heavens themselves. By the glow of embers carried from those scorched fields, hunters pursued their quarry across the frozen vastness when struck sparks alone gave insufficient radiance. The clan learned to interpret that fire-born landscape for refuge, discovering where cooled torrents of stone formed sheltering overhangs that held warmth through the interminable nights of ice. The volcanic glass cleaved from the mountain's slopes yielded edges of unmatched keenness, blades so refined they parted the thick pelts of mammoths with a single deliberate stroke.

Where glacier touched the realm of fire, steam burst skyward in scalding plumes the visionary named the exhalations of the world's deep breathing. The ochre deposits there had been transformed by subterranean furnaces to a more profound scarlet, and the people bore this coveted pigment homeward in pouches sewn from mammoth hide. The caldera's threshold remained forbidden ground, a basin choked with poison winds where molten rock pooled in luminous cisterns. Yet the mountain's violent exhalations delivered bounty to the tundra spreading below: froth-stone so light it drifted upon meltwater channels, nodules of black glass awaiting the knapper's art, and ember-threaded rocks that appeared to hold captive flame within their mineral hearts.

The clan perceived the smoking mountain as the glacier's contrary twin, one power that obliterated and one that arrested time itself. The visionary carved talismans from that fire-forged stone, inscribing tusk ivory with the caldera's emblems and darkening flint figurines in wreaths of sulphurous fume. When the mountain's grey ash descended like spectral snow across the frozen plain, dusting the ochre-marked threshold of their sanctuary, the people withdrew into the earth's embrace where residual warmth from the planet's molten core sustained them through the most protracted darkness of the glacial age, liquid stone pulsing in unfathomable depths below like the slow, incandescent rhythm of a buried heart.
