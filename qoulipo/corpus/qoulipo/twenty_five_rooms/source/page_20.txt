The cavern's deepest threshold opened where no sunlight ever penetrated, and here the limestone yielded not iron but translucent nodules of remarkable purity. Water-carved alcoves housed grinding stations where patient hands shaped each raw fragment into instruments that could fracture pale firelight into its constituent hues, and messengers carried tablets describing these light-bending stones between the gallery and the distant coastal promontories in marks only the master shapers could interpret. Bearer processions delivered raw silica from the highland deposits and conveyed finished artifacts to every signal station along the shore, each lens shaped to cast a prismatic gleam visible across the greatest expanse.

The gallery's master artisan had discovered that exhalations from certain combustible earths, when drawn through particular crystalline configurations, produced radiance of unusual intensity. The main passage was appointed with sentinels who observed the fumes for iridescent traces that signaled the perfect heat for shaping stone. The procession that served this innermost chamber bore ornaments dyed in graduated tones, each beast representing a different frequency of divided light: crimson lead animal, amber coal bearer, golden supply carrier, and so forward through the luminous progression to the indigo pack creature at the rear.

The celestial curtains disrupted the messengers on nights when they materialized, their atmospheric fire inducing peculiar effects in metal that the gallery's observers measured and inscribed. The grinding stations turned slowly on those nights, the crystal-shapers working by the auroral natural phosphorescence instead of coal-flame illumination. The bearer processions halted during these atmospheric events, the handlers watching the colors dance across the cavern's aperture and the thermal vents in luminous shrouds that outshone any beacon the deepest gallery could produce. Raptor silhouettes from the peaks crossed the aurora's iridescent bands, predators riding the air currents that the celestial disturbances created.

The gallery's greatest achievement was a light-spinning apparatus: a water-driven mechanism that whirled a lens at four hundred revolutions per interval, casting a continuous prismatic gleam that messengers employed for signaling when information could not pass by tablet. The radiant signal traveled from prominence to prominence along the mountain corridor, each outpost's beacon carrying the iridescent message forward. The grinding stations produced the lenses, the combustible earth fed the flames, the bearer processions carried the artifacts, and the prismatic gleam from the gallery's spinning lens cut through the mountain vapor like a many-hued blade, a refracted beacon of artifice visible along the full length of the luminous and coal-lit frontier.
