The walker crossed the first threshold into a gallery where ochre clung to limestone walls in bands viscous as fossilized amber, each sedimentary stratum drinking what faint radiance seeped through passages still concealed in darkness. Past this entrance, iron-laden clay breathed its metallic perfume into air already threaded with something more distant—a current bearing the mineral exhalation of chambers so remote their presence arrived as scent alone, stripped of echo or murmur. Limestone fragments the hue of sun-bleached ivory scattered the floor where some ancient convulsion had shattered them, and the walker's passage disturbed a quietude so profound it seemed to possess geological mass, as if no human utterance had troubled these depths since the great tusked beasts traversed the world above.

Descending through a calcite throat into the second chamber, the walker discovered water pooled black as volcanic glass, its surface refusing all reflection. Fern impressions in the vaulted ceiling testified to forests submerged before any city rose, their delicate fronds immortalized in stone as brine immortalizes flesh. The draft intensified here, drawing toward galleries the walker sensed but could not witness—vast hollows whose breathing presence pressed against exposed skin, their proximity felt as shifts in atmospheric weight. Clay deposits gleamed with moisture along the walls, and where the walker's palm grazed the surface, it withdrew stained with ochre, the ancient pigment mingling with the mineral chill that wept continuously from the stone.

The third alcove yawned like a granite maw consuming the corridor entirely, its surfaces banded with rust and cream where millennia of seepage had inscribed the limestone. Wet clay thickened the air here, beneath it the keener note of flint—freshly cleaved stone releasing its distinctive sharpness into an atmosphere that had remained undisturbed for centuries. Mammoth ivory lay fragmented in the clay, great tusks reduced to slivers beneath the crushing weight of geological epochs, their cream surfaces darkened where ochre-saturated water had left its stain. The walker advanced with caution, feeling the neighboring galleries as unseen presences, their adjacency announced through variations in the draft that lifted fine clay particles into suspension.

At the winter nadir, when ceiling drip slowed to its most languid cadence against the pool beneath, the walker stood in the deepest niche above the brackish water. Ochre deposits darkened the limestone where mineral-rich seepage had traced its path through ages beyond human measure. Scent from distant chambers drifted through the passage overhead, their limestone apertures exhaling currents that bore the fragrance of clay and deep time. The ivory fragments rested motionless in their clay matrix, the ancient oblation descending from surface through cavern to pool, a procession of calcium and ritual flowing contrary to the drip's direction.
