Deep within the earth, the shaman pressed an ochre-stained palm against a pillar rivaling a mammoth's tusk in girth. Crystalline efflorescences erupted from every surface, seizing the tallow-flame's tremor and fracturing it into prismatic sparks the clan took for captive spirits. The hollow plunged far beneath the tundra's crust, its conduits tapering into galleries encrusted with minerals, where pallid veins threaded through strata laid down across spans no human remembrance could compass. Ancient creatures—spiral casings, segmented limbs—jutted from the limestone, caught in stone the glacier's weight had never violated.

The clan's flint-workers learned that certain nodules, split with hammock-bone mauls, disclosed interiors of surpassing hardness and brilliance, outstripping any weathered stone. These mineral cores assumed sacred character, stationed at cavern mouths to delimit territory. The shaman divined meaning in the drip-formed shapes, each accretion spanning centuries, inscribing the hollow's chronicle in calcite and translucent stone. Where the floor plummeted into lower niches, fossil strata lay naked—compressed vestiges of beings that had navigated seas withdrawn aeons before the ice advanced.

Ochre ground with calcium paste yielded pigments that married permanently to the walls. The clan adorned every reachable expanse with renderings of the tundra overhead: mammoth columns crossing ice plains, flint-armed hunters, the shaman's discernments of what slumbered beneath the sediment. Mineral aggregations in the furthest galleries bent their tallow-light into spectral arrays that washed the limestone in transient hues. The fossils held the youngest spellbound, their fingers—still tacky with ochre—following the whorled forms, unable to grasp that these lithified beings antedated the glacier by intervals beyond conception.

The cavern served as both refuge and repository. Mineral deposition shrouded earlier paintings behind draperies of flowstone, sealing them within pallid accretions. Geode offerings gathered at the shaman's threshold where the greatest crystalline mass thrust upward like a petrified geyser. The clan descended each winter when the tundra grew impassable, threading past fossil alcoves and mineral faults into the earth's warmth, bearing flint, mammoth provisions, and ochre for paintings destined to outlive them all—eventually entombed beneath the patient accumulation of seeping stone, sediment, and the weight of ages.
