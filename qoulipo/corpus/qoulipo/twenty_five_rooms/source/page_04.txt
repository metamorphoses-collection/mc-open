The condor circled above the glacier, riding a thermal that rose where ice met exposed tundra rock. Below, the clan's shaman stood on a ridge watching the raptor's shadow cross the ochre-stained cliff face, reading altitude and wind direction from the bird's tilted feathers. Gusts funneled through the mountain pass, carrying the scent of mammoth herds moving across the high plateau where the stratosphere seemed close enough to pierce with a flint-tipped spear. Clouds massed at altitude, their underbellies lit amber by the setting sun, and the shaman marked this sky in memory as an omen.

The clan had always lived at elevation, where thermals lifted seed and pollen and the feathers of raptors who nested on the glacier's exposed ridges. Condor plumes were sacred, each one traded for a mammoth tusk's weight in flint. The shaman wore a cape of condor feathers that caught every gust, spreading wide when the thermal columns surged. At this altitude the tundra thinned to lichen and bare sediment, and the clan sheltered in ochre-painted overhangs where wind erosion had carved the rock into aerodynamic ridges. Nearby cavern mouths exhaled mineral-scented drafts, and crystal fragments glittered in the rubble of eroded stalactite beds exposed by retreating ice.

Mammoth bones too heavy to carry up the mountain paths were left at lower camps, but flint traveled everywhere, knapped into arrowheads calibrated for the stronger gusts at altitude. The condor was both competitor and teacher, its mastery of the thermal drafts showing the clan where updrafts could carry smoke signals between camps. The shaman launched feather bundles into the strongest gusts as offerings to the stratosphere, watching them spiral upward until they vanished into cloud banks. Raptors descended on the offerings, snatching feathers mid-flight with talon precision that awed the tundra hunters below.

At the glacier's summit, where altitude made breathing labored and frost rimmed every surface, the clan carved an ochre condor into the ice itself. The thermal winds eroded it slowly, feather by feather, gust by gust. The shaman said the stratosphere was where mammoth spirits climbed after death, following the condor's spiraling thermal staircase through the clouds. The clan believed this, watching raptors ascend beyond sight into an altitude no flint tool or ochre pigment could reach, where the tundra became a smear of grey and white far below.
