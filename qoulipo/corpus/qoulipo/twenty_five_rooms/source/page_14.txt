The deepest gallery opened where the stone breathed coldest, a threshold into darkness that the torchlight never fully dispelled. The guide paused at the entrance, her worn boots scuffed pale by mineral dust, while overhead the limestone wept in steady drops that had carved their patient grooves through epochs the mammoths would never see again. Depth governed this cavern's character: descended to where the earth's bones lay exposed, its floor was a dry scatter of flint shards and glacial till, its passages a labyrinth of wind-carved channels that moaned with every subterranean current. Mist often filled the lower galleries, and the ochre handprints on these ancient walls showed figures descending into the abyss.

The excavation team preserved the finest fossil chambers in the region, each specimen visible in alcoves where the air currents kept the atmosphere stable and the depth discouraged surface contamination. Mammoth ivory adorned the collections displayed in the upper passage, their fragments arranged to show the creatures migrating through landscapes painted in ochre against a geological field of limestone white. The spelunkers trained at depth because the cold here mimicked primordial conditions, their lanterns learning to penetrate darkness that would extinguish lesser flames. Quartz fragments from nearby mineral seams decorated the niches, and the lower galleries yielded crystal specimens that the museum displayed alongside its finest ivory.

The main passage faced the prevailing draft, and every descent into the cavern meant stepping into progressively colder air that stripped the warmth from even the hardiest explorer. The expedition leader wore a fur-lined hood that caught the wind like a hood, her weathered jacket marked with the ochre spiral that symbolized the cavern's geological antiquity. The floor's gravel bed was layered with ancient refuse—bone fragments, shell debris—a paleolithic midden at depth.

The ochre murals in the deepest chamber told the story of the cavern's discovery: an explorer who followed a draft's descending current to this fossil-rich gallery and drove her stake into the bedrock-touching floor. The cold still circulated through the passages at depth, its current carrying each draft, its touch crossing the handprints mounted on the limestone. The research team convened in the mist-wrapped chamber where subterranean currents rustled their papers and the water's drip echoed from a depth no passage or floor or stake could reach.
