The aurora unfurled across the ice-bound heavens in veils of lambent verdure, and the diviner pronounced it the glimmering essence of ancestral flame made manifest. The kindred assembled upon the frozen plain to witness the opalescent cascade, their ochre-stained countenances catching the radiance that transmuted from indigo to chrysolite as torrents of celestial fire descended through the uppermost reaches of air. Where moonlight grazed the ice-cliff's countenance, it shattered into chromatic rivers that the diviner read as pathways threading through the unseen realm beyond mortal sight.

Ivory extracted from mammoth skulls, burnished until near-transparency, became implements of separation—raised toward the aurora to divide its streaming brilliance into elemental hues. Those who worked the flint had observed that particular quartz-bearing stones, when cleaved at precise declinations, yielded momentary opalescent sparks, chromatic fire imprisoned within mineral substance that the diviner hoarded and configured in whorled arrangements deep within the cavern's throat. Each winter the aurora returned, its fragmenting configurations shifting with the glacier's scarred countenance, at times casting pillar-bright shafts by which the ice-plain kindreds steered across trackless expanses. Far overhead, condor shadows traversed the radiant bands, raptors ascending thermal columns even through darkness, their plumage briefly opalescent against the chromatic veil.

Within the cave's inner gallery, the diviner tended a limestone face consecrated entirely to the aurora, depicting each manifestation in ochre blended with pulverized quartz that held faint luminescence when flames drew near. Though the kindred possessed no term for that which traveled, they grasped that something journeyed from the aurora to the watching eye, something bearing chromatic meaning through the frigid tundra atmosphere. Where ice fragments drifted through the upper void, the chromatic effect multiplied into doubled and tripled arches, each opalescent band a distinct utterance from those who had walked before. Mammoth congregations thundered beneath the aurora, their exhalations rising in vaporous columns that the shattered light rendered briefly incandescent.

The flint-worker who first shaped a disc from glacier ice maintained he had trapped a fragment of the aurora's chromatic essence within the frozen lens. The diviner secured this glacial lens upon a staff hewn from mammoth bone, wielding it as illumination during observances when the aurora's vigor waned. Through its clarity the frozen plain appeared transformed, every ice-ridge edge dissolving into opalescent coronas, every distant mammoth form encircled by chromatic fire. The lambent heavens throbbed overhead, veils of celestial radiance weaving through the protracted polar darkness, and the kindred maintained their ochre-marked vigil as the fragmenting of primordial light painted their ice-locked domain in hues that transcended naming.
