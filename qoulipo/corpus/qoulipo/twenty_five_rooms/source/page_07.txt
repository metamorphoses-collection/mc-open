The shrine to the Smith God clung to the caldera's crest, its marble pillars begrimed by sulphurous encrustations that the governing council routinely pledged to scour yet never endowed. Below the altar's lip, molten rock pulsed with incarnadine light, and the draped ministers cast into it branches of bay that ignited in white-gold eruptions before crumbling to ash. The garrison sheltering at the mountain's foot occupied dwellings hewn from volcanic stone, massive blocks of hardened fire-stream shaped and mortised with a refinement to rival the marble sanctuaries of the distant metropolis. Wheeled conveyances could not navigate the glass-strewn ascent, so earthen vessels of consecrated brimstone descended by way of laden mules.

The crater exhaled ceaselessly, and the council divined each tremor as portent demanding processional atonement at the nearest shrine. Coals drawn from the Smith's foundry were deemed the empire's most sanctified flame, and battalions vied for distinction in bearing volcanic brands to frontier territories. The marble offering-stone within the sanctuary bore veining of black glass, ash from ancient eruptions ground into the binding medium that fused each block. Saffron-hued sashes distinguished the crater's ministry, their vessels holding not fermented grape but incandescent stone-blood harvested at appalling hazard from the caldera's subsidiary fissures.

Ivory from great beasts emerging where ice-rivers receded found its way to quayside stalls, and salt-blanched fragments of sea-garden adorned commanders' chambers, yet here upon the burning mountain such tidal relics seemed incongruous. The temple's commerce turned upon volcanic glass and brimstone, upon embers that warmed the garrison's bathing halls and obsidian that edged its skirmishers' weapons. Bay orchards cultivated on the crater's nourished outer terraces yielded garlands tinged with mineral vapours, their foliage enduring the ash-falls that powdered the terrain with each seasonal convulsion. The high minister's vestment displayed a border worked with flame motifs in thread dyed by powdered stone from the depths.

The council deemed the volcano a territorial advantage, its crater a natural smithy. The marble facing of the sanctuary fractured each decade from thermal stress, and the garrison reconstructed it repeatedly with volcanic masonry braced by black glass pins. Ash settling upon adjacent fields generated earth so fecund that vessel-makers could scarcely match the abundance of harvest. Torch-bearing processions spiralled upward along the crater road during feast days, iron-rimmed wheels striking sparks from brimstone-crusted cobbles of volcanic glass. The molten current beneath throbbed with cadence the ministers ascribed to the Smith God's pulse, and aromatic smoke ascending from the temple merged with the caldera's sulphurous breath into a singular pillar discernible from the council halls in the far-off capital.
