The cavern's deepest throat opened directly into the earth's hidden river, and the elder watched from a high gallery as the underground current carried mineral dust in dark plumes past the fossil beds toward the unmeasured chasm. Vapor from the thermal springs mingled with cold seepage where the ancient passage reached the subterranean pool, and the fissures that joined the flame-warmed alcove to the water-smoothed antechamber echoed with the measured drip of seepage and the quiet accounting of lichen growth. The cavern transformed whale oil for illumination, its fire-pit converting the ocean's bounty into the grease that kept the pathway lamps glowing through every season.

Coal sledges arrived with the water's rise, their runners scraping the limestone as they navigated the narrow defile to the cavern's threshold. The smoke-hole rose above the pool like a beacon, its haze visible from the deepest grottos where rope-haulers dragged lichen-tangled baskets through the brine. The passage carried coal from distant mines to the cavern, and the same sledge returned laden with refined whale oil, brine-preserved catch, and fossil coral ground into kindling for the flame-pit's grinding stones. The drummer at the pool-side niche transmitted water-current intelligence to the cavern in rhythmic code, each beat forecasting the tides.

The cavern's thermal vents drew cooling water from the pool through channels where lichen accumulated at their mouths. The flame-pit produced anchor chain, each link tested against the crushing pressures that the underwater fissures endured. Coal feeders worked in shifts timed to the water's rise, feeding the furnace when the brine-cooled condensers operated at peak strength during high flow. The smoke-hole belched haze that the current-driven draft carried into the depths, where it thinned above the fossil beds in a perpetual industrial murk.

The sledge that served the coastal passage was named Leviathan, its brazier fired with coal and its horn audible from the deepest fishing grottos. The drum-signals joined this cavern to every pool-side settlement in the limestone, its messages traveling faster than the water's rise but slower than the current of exchange that kept the flame-pit burning, the smoke-hole hazed, the passage traveled, and the vapor rising from a cavern carved at the precise threshold where the earth's lichen-rich brine met the coal-darkened infrastructure of an industrial age that had turned the fossil beds into treasure and the water's rise into clockwork.
