In the mountain's deepest chambers, where no breeze stirs and daylight has never touched stone, the miners hew iron from seams that run through the bedrock like veins. I went down once, carrying a candle whose flame the sodden air sought to smother with every footfall. The darkness there was nothing like the gentle dimness of a shuttered room — it was entire, substantial, a blackness with heft and grain. Down in those passages, shadow does not merely obscure the light; it devours it whole.

The ore the miners bring forth has the color of old bloodstains, and when the furnace takes it, a smell rises that the workers call the mountain's own breath — brimstone and mineral, a scent nothing could soften or sweeten. Faith finds no foothold in these depths. Here there is only granite, and the iron locked within, and the darkness that enfolds them both. Before descending, the miners do not kneel in prayer; they test their tackle. The stone asks not for devotion but for craft.

And yet: standing in that blackness, the candle failing and the shadow bearing down, I felt what I can name only as the shape of something missing. A vacancy in the world where the divine ought to dwell. The Jansenists who walked the cloisters at Port-Royal would name this the hidden God — the One who withdraws not from spite but from care, that our trust remain trust and not merely vision. The darkness is not God's departure but His curtain. The iron is not simply the mountain's marrow but the metal of Golgotha's timber.

Whether I hold this as certainty, I cannot say. I know only that in those depths, hemmed by stone and night, breathing air that carried the taste of ore, with the wind of the high ridges impossibly distant overhead, I felt faith not as a rising but as a weight, a pull downward toward the mountain's heart where something abides. Something that utters no word, reveals no face, offers no comfort for the journey down. Something as unyielding as bedrock, as complete as darkness, as pitiless as iron.

The wind at the crest is release. The darkness in the depths is reckoning. Between them, the mountain keeps what it knows in stone and ore, and faith is what we name the act of going down without certainty of what awaits.
