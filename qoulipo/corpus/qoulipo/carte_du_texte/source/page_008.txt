I have spent nights counting the shadows that move across my wall — not from madness, but from the conviction that number governs even the accidental. The candle flickers; the shadow jumps. Each jump is a fact witnessed. Gather enough facts and a shape emerges, not because the shadows intend it but because number runs beneath all things, even the dance of dark forms on plaster.

The wager of the mathematician differs from the wager of the soldier. The soldier bets his life on the sword; I bet my sanity on the number. Both wagers are absolute: you cannot half-wager, as you cannot half-die. When I entered the calculus of chances, I entered a twilight country where nothing is certain and everything may be reckoned. The uncertainty is the heart of it. If the outcome were known, there would be no wager, no mathematics, no need for the elaborate engines of expectation that I have built from ink and thought.

The ink dries quickly in winter. I write by candlelight, and the shadow of my hand falls across the page as I write, so that I am constantly writing through my own darkness. This is not a figure of speech; it is a bodily truth. The hand that holds the pen also stops the light. The sword-hand and the pen-hand are the same hand, and the shadow they cast is the same shadow.

In my letters to Fermat, we spoke of numbers that hide — the primes that wait among composites like assassins in a crowd. The wager of the prime is its capriciousness: you cannot know where the next one will appear. You can only count the ones that have already shown themselves and cast forward, shadow-like, into the unknown. The ink records the primes already known; the shadow covers what remains hidden. Between them lies the entire country of number theory, which is to say the country of shaped uncertainty.

Every number casts a shadow. The shadow of 6 is the fact that it is perfect — equal to the sum of its divisors. The shadow of 28 is the same perfection. The shadow of a sword is a line; the shadow of a number is a theorem. Both are projections of something real onto something flat. Both require ink to record.
