The square lies broad and white under the midday sun, its cobblestones bleached pale as old bone. Heat ripples above the stones where shadow does not fall. From the cookstalls along the eastern edge comes the thick smell of frying oil cut through with road dust, settling over everything like a second skin. The awnings are canvas, sun-faded to no particular color, and beneath them the vendors arrange their goods with the practiced economy of those who will pack them again before evening.

A tinker works the crowd with his cart, calling out in a voice worn smooth by years of calling. He sells pins and needles, small scissors, tin cups that catch the light. His wares hang from hooks along the cart's sides, clinking softly as he moves. The trade here is old and slow, each transaction a negotiation, coin touching palm only after the proper interval of refusal and reconsideration. Women finger bolts of undyed cloth. A farmer turns a leather harness over in his hands, checking the stitching with his thumbnail.

Near the fountain, a boy of perhaps eight years stands before a potter's display, a clay pot cradled in both hands. The pot is simple, unglazed, the kind used for storing grain or water. The potter names a price. The boy shakes his head, sets the pot down, turns as if to leave. The potter sighs, names a lower price. The boy returns, lifts the pot again, studies it as though something in its form might have changed. They are performing a dance both know by heart, though the boy's movements still carry the stiffness of incomplete learning.

The fountain runs despite the heat, a thin stream from the lion's mouth into the basin below. Around its rim, people rest between their errands, faces turned briefly toward the small mercy of water. By evening, the square will empty, the carts will roll away toward other towns, and the cobblestones will hold the day's heat long into darkness. But that is hours away yet. For now, the sun stands directly overhead, and the business of exchange continues in its patient, immemorial way.
