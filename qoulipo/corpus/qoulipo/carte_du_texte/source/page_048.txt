The wind comes down from the north with a blade's edge, cutting through the gaps in the stone where mortar has crumbled away. Tobia stands at the parapet with his cloak drawn close, watching the darkness pool in the valley below. The torch behind him spits and hisses, fed with pine resin that sends its sharp, medicinal smell into the night—a scent like ancient forests rendered down to their essence, sticky and amber in the iron bracket. Each gust threatens the flame, makes it flatten and roar, painting the watchtower's crown in frantic orange light that reaches no farther than the nearest merlon. Beyond that, the world dissolves into gradations of black and blacker still.

He has counted three hours since the bell in the distant cloister marked nine. Time moves differently up here, where there is nothing to measure it against but the slow wheel of stars and the rhythm of his own breathing. The valley settlements have long since shuttered their windows. Even the harbour, visible by day as a grey crescent on the eastern edge of the grid of roads and fields, shows nothing now but absence—a void where the land gives way to water. Somewhere in that darkness, boats creak against their moorings. Somewhere, the market square lies empty beneath its canvas awnings, waiting for dawn and the return of voices.

But there—far to the south, where the land makes its final gesture before the sea—the lighthouse blinks. Three seconds of light, fifteen of darkness, regular as a pulse. Tobia has learned its rhythm so well he no longer needs to count. He marks it against his breath: inhale through the dark, exhale as the beam sweeps across the water, inhale again through the longer darkness. The light never reaches him, too distant and too low, but he watches for it anyway. Proof that someone else is awake in the world. Proof that someone else is keeping vigil.

The resin smell grows stronger as the torch burns down toward its socket. Soon he will need to replace it.
