The forge squats at the edge of the settlement where the grid gives way to open country, its chimney a dark finger against the pre-dawn sky. Already the storm gathers to the west, clouds massing like armies, their undersides bruised purple and gray. Inside, the first coals kindle, breathing soot and the acrid sweetness of coke into air still cool from the night. Jeronimo has been here since the darkness was absolute, laying the fire with the methodical grace of thirty years' practice, building heat degree by patient degree.

Light seeps through the doorway now, reluctant and thin. The forge answers with its own illumination, the coals beginning their transformation from black to red, red to orange, orange to that white heart where metal remembers it was once liquid. Leather aprons hang on their pegs along the eastern wall—some supple, worked soft by years of the body's movement, others stiff and scarred with burn marks like a campaign map of small violences. The bellows wheeze their rhythmic commentary. Somewhere beyond the settlement, thunder performs its distant exercises.

The first commission rests on the workbench: hinges for the mill's new door, three sets, the drawings precise in their dimensions. Iron stock waits in the rack, each bar a promise of transformation. Jeronimo lifts one, tests its weight, lays it across the anvil without ceremony. The hammer hangs familiar in his grip. When metal meets metal the sound carries—they will hear it at the market, at the watchtower, wherever dawn has found them awake. The rhythm of making, older than the town itself, older than the grid that orders these houses and workshops into their tidy rows.

The smell intensifies as the fire reaches working temperature: coke and scale and the particular bitterness of flux. Rain begins to tap against the roof, tentative at first, then insistent. Jeronimo draws the iron from the coals, now cherry-bright, and begins to shape it. The storm can do what it will. Here there is only heat and hammer, the eternal reduction of raw matter into use, spark-scatter blooming and dying in the shadows like brief, nameless stars.
