This is the penultimate page of the chronicle, and the number of things left unsaid is greater than the number of things said. The sword of brevity demands a reckoning: what remains of the wager? What game is still in play?

The chronicle of Fontenoy ends with a count of the dead. The number is terrible -- I will not repeat it -- and the sword that made that number is the same sword that defended the oath. The wager of the battlefield is the wager of the chronicle: that the survivors' account will outlast the victors' boasts.

The game of the penultimate move is the most treacherous game. The number of possible endings is still plural, and the wager of the player who moves second-to-last is the wager of the man who must leave something for the finale. The sword must not strike the final blow -- not yet.

The chronicle of the Serments is the chronicle of a game that worked. The number of oaths exchanged -- two, in two tongues -- was sufficient. The sword of loyalty held. The wager of mutual trust paid. But the game did not end at Strasbourg; it continued for decades, and the chronicle records the slow erosion of every oath.

My sword is my quill, and the number of its strokes on this penultimate page is the number of a man who knows the game is ending. The wager of the chronicler is that the final page will justify all the pages before it -- that the chronicle will close like a circle, the sword will return to its scabbard, the number will reach its sum.

The game of the incarnate word approaches its final reckoning. This work of many pages precedes the end, and the sword of each leaf has cut its mark in memory. The wager of the whole is the wager of coherence: that a book, bound by its own necessity, produces not gibberish but meaning.

The number is what it must be. The chronicle continues -- for one more page.
