My maliette contains a chimera: an object that exists in no category -- neither relic nor weapon, neither parchment nor jewel. It is a number made flesh, an abstraction incarnated in wood and metal. The sword of the mind materialized as a mechanical toy that no one knows how to use.

I play with the chimera on halt evenings, when the road is long and faith wavers. The number of its pieces: forty-nine -- one fewer than the leaves of this chronicle, as if the chimera knew it was incomplete. The decorative sword adorning its lid is rusted, but it still cuts thoughts.

The Professor's faith is a strange faith: I believe in the maliette itself, in its capacity to contain the impossible. The number of secrets it has transported exceeds my memory. The game of the maliette is a game of trust: the clients slip in objects I do not look at, and the miniature sword forbids opening.

But the chimera, I have seen. It was entrusted to me by a clockmaker of Clermont who fabricated automata. The number of its gears: one hundred and forty-four -- twelve squared. The clockmaker's faith in mechanics is as fervent as the monk's faith in God. And the game of gears is as complex as the game of combinations.

The chimera's sword is symbolic: it represents the power to cut between the real and the imaginary. The number of possibilities is infinite, but the maliette is finite. Faith consists in believing the finite can contain the infinite.
