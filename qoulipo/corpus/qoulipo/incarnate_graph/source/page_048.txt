The coffer in my maliette contains a relic -- or what claims to be one. The relic conspiracy is so old that my faith wavers: is it a real saint's bone, or a forgery I have transported a hundred times without knowing?

The parchment of authenticity is rolled around the bone, sealed with red wax. The client's wager is that the recipient will not break the seal -- that he will accept the maliette, the parchment, and the bone with the blind faith of believers.

The conspiracy passes through me without touching me. I am the conducting wire -- the maliette linking the forger to the credulous, the parchment traveling from a guilty hand to an innocent one. My faith in my own innocence is my only wager -- the wager of the Professor who does not know, who does not wish to know.

The parchment of authenticity is a calligraphic masterpiece. The conspiracy has invested in form -- oak gall ink, impeccable Caroline script, irreproachable diplomatic formulas. The wager on beauty is the surest of wagers: who would dare doubt so beautiful a parchment?

My maliette has transported dozens of these coffers. The faith they contain -- true or false -- is the fuel of medieval devotion. The conspiracy does not create faith; it exploits it. The forger's wager is not that God exists, but that men want to believe -- and this wager, he wins every time.

The parchment wears. The conspiracy persists. The maliette continues its road. Faith -- the real kind, not that of bones -- waits somewhere at the end of the road.
