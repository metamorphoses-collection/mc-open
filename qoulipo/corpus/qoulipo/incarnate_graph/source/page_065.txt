The three of us stand before the final leaf, and what we have woven lies complete. Each witness carried their own necessity, each voice ran true to its own color, and where two colors met they could not share the same space. The design held because we honored distance, because we knew that certain intensities cannot touch without annihilating one another.

I see now what I could not see at the beginning: that restriction was never absence but a kind of attention. The girl at the window learned to count only what mattered. The architect discovered that beauty emerges not despite limitation but through it—that a building stands because certain stones refuse certain positions, because load and void collaborate in their separation. The mathematician found in forbidden adjacencies a music more precise than any theorem, a form that breathed.

We three have spoken from different rooms in the same house, each voice unaware it was completing the others. The girl counted birds that would not land on neighboring branches. The architect designed spaces that knew their own boundaries. The mathematician proved what could not be proven except by living it. And in the end, what are we but witnesses to this: that separation creates the very possibility of design. That what stands alone can stand together.

The tapestry is incarnate now—no longer abstraction but cloth, no longer theory but a thing you could wrap around your shoulders. Each witness a voice, each voice a color that knows its name. The work is complete because it knows when to end, because the last leaf understands what the first one promised. We close the book. We step back from the loom. What we have made will hold.
