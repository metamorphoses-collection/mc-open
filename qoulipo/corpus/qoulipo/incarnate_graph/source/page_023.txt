In my maliette, dead tongues share space with living ones. I transport a glossary of Carolingian terms the monks use to understand each other: words in Frankish that no one speaks anymore, resurrected like linguistic zombies to serve as code.

The chronicle of dead tongues is a chronicle of successful conspiracies: Gaulish was killed by Latin, Latin by Romance, and each linguistic death is a victory of the conqueror's speech. The Darwinian game of tongues has no rules of fair play -- the strongest tongue devours the others.

My maliette also contains a card game -- not an ordinary game, but a linguistic game the monks invented to memorize Latin declensions. Each card is a grammatical case, each round a battle against oblivion. The tongue survives through the game.

The chronicle of my journeys is punctuated by these tongue games. In each abbey, a different dialect. In each maliette, a new vocabulary. The survival of languages is also a matter of leather cases -- each one transports its own version of the world.

The game this text plays is also a game of tongues. Each page speaks its own language, with its five voices like five grammatical cases. The text's maliette contains many languages, each shaped by an invisible hand.

The chronicle of this literary project will be written one day. In the meantime, my maliette keeps the secret -- in a dead tongue, naturally.
