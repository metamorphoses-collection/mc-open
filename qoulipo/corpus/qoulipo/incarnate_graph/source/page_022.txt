If I had a maliette -- and I do not, for geometers have no maliettes, they have drawers -- I would store in it the proofs of the wager. The number of these proofs is paradoxical: they are at once zero and infinite. Zero, for one does not prove God. Infinite, for everything proves Him.

Faith is a maliette one carries without opening. The wager consists of walking -- the sword at one's side for prudence -- with this closed maliette, wagering that its contents are worth the journey. The number of steps of the believer is the number of steps of the wager: each step is a renewal of the stake.

The sword of doubt always threatens to open the maliette. If one could verify, there would be no more wager. Faith demands the opacity of the maliette, the mystery of its contents. The number of temptations to open is the number of doubts -- and this number is great, I know, for I doubt each day.

But the wager holds. The maliette remains closed. The sword of doubt does not cut the straps of faith -- it tightens them, for a wager without risk is not a wager. The number of risks measures the value of the commitment.

Does the believer's maliette contain a chimera or a treasure? The number of possible answers is two: yes or no. But faith transcends this binary number. It wagers on a third state -- one where the maliette contains both at once, like the cat that future physicists will imagine, neither alive nor dead until observed.

The sword waits. The wager endures. The maliette closes the eyes of doubt.
