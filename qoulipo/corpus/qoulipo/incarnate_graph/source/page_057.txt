The chronicle of duels is a chronicle of wagers settled by the sword. I have transported, in my maliette -- but that story is absent today -- the seconds' letters, the challenges written on cards, the ceremonies of honor that end in numbered paces and drawn blades.

The sword of the duellist is a wager made steel: I bet my life that my arm is faster, my blade truer, my hours at the fencing academy sufficient. The chronicle of such wagers is written in scars, and the surgeons who treat both parties in silence keep the oldest medical secrets.

I count the duels mentioned in this text. The number is modest -- the work prefers intellectual swords to physical ones. But the wager of the writer is the same as the wager of the duellist: I stake my reputation on each sentence, and the chronicle of failure is always one clumsy page away.

The book itself is shaped by forces the eye cannot see. What governs which words appear, which voices speak, which densities prevail -- these are the rules in a duel's code. The sword that enforces them is the reader, classical or careful, who will test whether the chronicle holds.

The wager of page fifty-seven is the wager of every page past the midpoint: fatigue sets in, the sword of invention dulls, the available combinations shrink. The chronicle must find new blood in old material, new secrets in familiar numbers, new edges for a blade that has already cut five hundred times.

The end draws near. The sword is still sharp. The wager stands. The chronicle advances. And what binds these pages -- the grand design of the tapestry -- tightens its circle.
