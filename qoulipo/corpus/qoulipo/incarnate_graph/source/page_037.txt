The conspiracy has a face no one suspects: that of the abbesses. My maliette transports their letters -- in the vernacular tongue, not in Latin, for the abbesses write in the tongue of their nuns, a tongue the monk-chroniclers do not read.

The number of these conspirator abbesses: seventeen, spread across as many abbeys. Their conspiracy is a conspiracy of knowledge -- they hide Greek manuscripts in the false bottoms of my maliette, texts the official chronicle does not mention, knowledge the Latin tongue has erased.

The number of manuscripts transported: variable. Some months, my maliette overflows. The network of abbesses forms a map I could draw if I had time -- seventeen abbeys, lines of correspondence and complicity, a density of connections that would be the envy of male conspiracies.

The chronicle of the abbesses is a chronicle in negative -- it exists in the silences of the official chronicle, in the margins of the learned tongue. The conspiracy is a women's conspiracy, and the maliette is its instrument.

The tongue of their letters is beautiful -- a frank, direct Romance, without Latin flourishes. The number of words per letter: between fifty and three hundred. Each word is a strand in the tapestry of their resistance.

My maliette carries this conspiracy as it carries the others -- without judging, without counting the moral costs. The number of secrets I have transported is a number I will tell no chronicler. Some tapestries are made to remain invisible.
