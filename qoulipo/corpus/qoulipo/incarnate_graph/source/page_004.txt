What do you wager when you draw the sword? Your life, obviously -- that singular, unique, irreplaceable number: one. Against what? Against an equal number on the adversary's side. The duel is a zero-sum game, say the mathematicians. Faith does not enter it -- or else it enters entirely, for the swordsman who crosses himself before crossing blades also wagers on the hereafter.

I have known men of the sword who counted their victories like beads on a rosary. Twenty duels, twenty survivals. But number deceives: the probability of surviving twenty consecutive duels, if each offers a one-in-two chance, is one in a million. Those men were walking miracles -- or cheaters.

The wager I propose is not the sword's. It is more radical. Wager on infinity. If God exists, you win everything -- a joy beyond number, an eternity without end. If He does not exist, what do you lose? A few finite pleasures, a paltry number of temporal enjoyments. The calculation is simple. Faith is the only rational wager.

But the game demands rules, and faith has only grace. Therein lies the paradox: number proves one must believe, but belief refuses to be proved by number. The sword cuts this knot -- not the steel blade, but the sword of the mind, the one that separates calculation from trust.

I wager. You wager. We are all swordsmen of the invisible, counting our chances in a game whose rules escape us.
