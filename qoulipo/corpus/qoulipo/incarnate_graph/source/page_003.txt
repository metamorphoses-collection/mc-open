Number is but a sword that reason brandishes against chance. I understood this playing dice with the Chevalier de Mere, when the language of probability did not yet exist -- when it had to be invented, word by word, number by number.

The sword cleaves the real in two: what is and what could be. Faith does the same. To believe is to wager that the invisible is worth more than the visible, that the infinite number of God surpasses all finite calculation. My wager is not a game -- or rather, it is the supreme game, one whose stakes surpass the human language.

How many words does it take to say infinity? The French language perhaps possesses countless thousands, Latin more. But none has a term for this trembling certainty, this faith that calculates its own probabilities. I invented the word "expectation" in its mathematical sense. Expectation: what one can expect from a game over an infinite number of rounds.

The soldier's sword kills once. The geometer's number cuts infinitely. Each sword stroke in battle is a discrete event -- a point in the tapestry of carnage. Each prayer is another point, in the tapestry of grace.

And the language -- that marvelous vernacular language that unites the people and divides the scholars -- the language is the line that sews together faith and number, sword and game. Without it, we would be beasts counting on our fingers, brandishing pebbles. With it, we are geometers of the soul.

The game awaits its next stake. Number awaits its next theorem.
