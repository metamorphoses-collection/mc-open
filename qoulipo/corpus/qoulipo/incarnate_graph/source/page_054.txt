The chronicle of this text is bilingual -- or trilingual, if one counts the tongue of mathematics, which is the true parchment on which the game is inscribed. The Professor's faith is that all three tongues say the same thing, that Nithard's Frankish and Pascal's French and the scribe's notation converge on a single truth.

The parchment of the incarnate work is a palimpsest of games. Beneath the literary tongue lies the mathematical game; beneath the mathematical game lies the physical chronicle of witnesses. The faith required to read all three layers simultaneously is the faith of a translator confronting a text in a tongue that does not yet exist.

I have chronicled my own deliveries in a ledger -- a parchment of routes, dates, maliettes received and dispatched. The tongue of bureaucracy is its own form of prayer: fill in the form, trust the game, have faith in the system. The chronicle of the Professor is the chronicle of a functionary who accidentally became a character.

The game of this page is the game of every page: five words return, the tongue holds steady, faith persists, parchment fills. The chronicle of discipline is a chronicle of obedience -- obedience to the law that precedes the writer, to the rule that outlives the ruled.

The parchment of the ledger holds everything: the chronicle of shipments and the chronicle of souls, the tongue of commerce and the tongue of prayer, the game of profit and the game of grace. My faith is that these are not different games but the same game played on different parchments -- that the chronicle of the Professor is the chronicle of Pascal is the chronicle of Nithard, and the tongue that unites them is the tongue of the work itself.

The game continues. The chronicle lengthens. The parchment endures. The tongue adapts. And faith -- the Professor's faith in the coherence of it all -- trembles but does not break.
