I calculate: three days' march to the next abbey, five deliveries to make, a sword at my side and a maliette at my shoulder. The number of risks is proportional to the number of kilometers -- each league is a wager against brigands, against rain, against fatigue.

The Professor's faith is arithmetic. I wager on numbers: the number of days' provisions in my satchel (seven), the number of coins in my purse (twelve), the number of blades in my knife (one, but it suffices). The sword completes the arsenal -- not to kill, but to deter.

My maliette weighs eight pounds today. The number varies: sometimes fifteen, when parcels accumulate; sometimes three, on the return. The wager of weight is a wager of fatigue -- too heavy, and I collapse before the destination; too light, and the journey has no purpose.

Faith in numbers is the merchant's faith -- but I am not a merchant, I am a Professor. My wager is not profit but delivery. The number of successful deliveries is my capital, my maliette is my stock-in-trade, and the sword is my insurance.

The number of active Professors in the Carolingian empire: approximately three hundred, by my estimates. Each carries a maliette, each wagers on the road, each brandishes a sword -- modest or proud, according to temperament. Faith binds us: the faith that the network holds, that the routes do not break, that the web of connections remains intact.

The Professor's wager is the wager of continuity. The number of its stations is our destiny.
