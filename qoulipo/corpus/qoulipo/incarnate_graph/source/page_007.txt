The conspiracy has its own tongue. I catalogued thirty-seven terms the monks used among themselves to designate their acts of fabrication: "translatio" for the displacement of a falsified relic, "inventio" for its pretended discovery, "elevatio" for its public exposition. Each word a knot in the tapestry of conspiracy.

The number of these technical terms forms a clandestine lexicon, a tongue within the tongue. My chronicle records them all, for the historian's game is to preserve what the conspirators would erase. They play hide-and-seek with truth; I play at discovering it.

How many word combinations does it take to weave an effective conspiracy? The number depends on the size of the network. With ten conspirators, the possible pairs are forty-five -- a complete web of connections. But real conspiracies are never complete: there are subgroups, partitioned cells, men ignorant of the existence of other men.

The tongue of the conspiracy is a partial tapestry. My chronicle attempts to reconstruct it from fragments -- a combinatorial puzzle game where each piece is a word overheard, a whisper transcribed, a number inscribed in a margin.

I counted seven distinct cells. Seven -- another biblical number, as if the conspiracy imitated the structure of Creation. Seven days, seven principal conspirators, seven stages of fabrication. The sacrilegious game borrows the numbers of the sacred the better to profane it.

The chronicle records. The tongue betrays the traitors. Number enumerates them.
