The rain arrives as I reach the inn, drumming the shutters with the insistence of a monk at vigils. I set the case on the scarred table and unbuckle only what I need: the prayer-book, its spine cracked white as old bone, and the stoppered flask of ink that has traveled with me since Reims.

The innkeeper brings wine without asking. He does not look at the case. Those who carry such burdens learn to recognize one another by the silence we maintain, the way we touch what we carry—briefly, with care, then turn our attention elsewhere.

I open the prayer-book. Its leaves fall to a passage I have not sought: *Deposuit potentes de sede.* He has brought down the powerful from their thrones. The words are illuminated in gold that catches the firelight, each letter a small defiance against the dark outside.

What is carried openly and what is carried sealed—this is the daily negotiation. The prayer-book may rest on any table. The sealed parchments within the case must wait for hands I have not yet met, in abbeys whose walls I have not yet seen. Trust is the distance between these two states: the revealed and the reserved.

I think of the weight. Not the physical burden, though my shoulder knows that measure well enough. The other weight, the one that increases with each sealed packet I carry. Words committed to hide, formulas folded away, testimonies witnessed but not yet read. They grow heavier the longer they remain undelivered, as if silence itself accumulated mass.

The ink flask catches the light. Through the brown glass I can see the liquid within, dark as old blood, still fluid despite the cold. What it has written, what it may yet write—these are different countries, separated by the act of opening.
