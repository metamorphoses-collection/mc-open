The sword in my maliette is an object of prayer. Not that I pray to the sword -- faith is not idolatrous -- but that the sword reminds me to pray. Each evening, I take it from its leather compartment, wipe it, and recite a Pater while watching it shine.

The evening game is ritual: take out the sword, unroll the prayer parchment, play both roles -- that of the warrior and that of the penitent. The maliette is the theater of this little daily game, and faith is its director.

The prayer parchment and the miniature sword cohabit in the seventh compartment. It is a game of opposites: the softness of vellum against the hardness of steel, the faith of prayer against the violence of the blade. The maliette reconciles what theology separates.

The sword has never served. That is its secret -- and my own game of making others believe it has seen battles. The prayer parchment, for its part, serves every day: it is worn, stained, folded a hundred times. Faith wears like vellum -- but like vellum, it grows more beautiful with age.

The Professor's game is a game of appearances: the sword to impress, the maliette to transport, the parchment to pray, faith to persevere. Each object in the maliette is a role in a play I perform alone, on the roads of the empire, before an audience of oaks and muddy paths.
