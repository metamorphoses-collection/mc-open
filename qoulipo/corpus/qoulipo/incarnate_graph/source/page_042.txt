The number of manuscripts in my maliette today: four. It is few, but each is precious -- a commentary on Boethius in Latin, a homily in the Romance tongue, a computus treatise in Arabic numerals, and a blank parchment for road notes.

My maliette is an ambulant library, a chronicle of knowledge in transit. The tongue of each manuscript is different, the span of folios variable -- the Boethius runs long, the homily brief, the computus briefer still. The blank parchment has no pages yet; it awaits its tongue.

The Professor's chronicle is the chronicle of these transits. Each manuscript has a history: where it comes from, where it is going, in what tongue it was copied, how many hands have touched it. The parchment bears traces of each hand -- an archaeology of fingers no chronicler records.

The number of tongues in my maliette: three (Latin, Romance, Arabic). The number of forms of knowledge: infinite. The parchment is the bridge between tongues -- it welcomes Arabic numerals as well as Latin letters, the vernacular chronicle as well as the learned gloss.

My maliette groans under the weight of these four worlds. The chronicle of their journey -- from Corbie to Fleury, from Fleury to Tours, from Tours to Reims -- is a cartography of Carolingian knowledge. The number of these routes is a web only the Professor can trace.

The parchment travels. The tongue transforms. Number persists. The maliette endures.
