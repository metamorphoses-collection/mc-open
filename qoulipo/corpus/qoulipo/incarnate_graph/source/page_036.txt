The forger's forge is as noisy as the blacksmith's. The conspiracy clinks: the sword of the scraper that erases old writings, the hammer that flattens stolen seals, the wager that no one will compare the parchment to the original.

My chronicle denounces three major forgeries in the archives of Saint-Denis. The parchment of each is impeccable -- that is the forger's art: using old vellum, artificially aged ink, a script imitating that of the previous century. The conspiracy is calligraphic before it is political.

The sword of diplomatic criticism -- that science the Maurists will bring -- does not yet exist. The forgers' wager is that no one will know how to distinguish true from false. The parchment is their ally: it ages equally, whether it carries an authentic text or a fabrication.

The conspiracy of the forge requires an expertise my chronicle respects despite everything. The forger is a craftsman of the parchment -- he knows inks, scripts, diplomatic formulas better than anyone. The sword of forgery is a sword of precision, not of brute force.

The wager of posterity is the same for chronicler and forger: that the parchment will survive. But the chronicler wagers on truth, and the forger on falsehood. The conspiracy of time is that often, it is the lie that survives -- for the forgery is more beautiful, more careful, more convincing than the true.

My chronicle is my only sword against this conspiracy. The parchment of truth against the parchment of lies. The wager is open.
