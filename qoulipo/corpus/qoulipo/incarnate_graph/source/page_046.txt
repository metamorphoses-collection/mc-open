My treasure is in my maliette. Not gold -- the gold coins I have ever possessed fit on the fingers of one hand -- but something more precious: trust. The sword that protects me is worth less than the faith my clients place in me.

The Professor's wager is a wager of accumulation: how many deliveries does it take to build a reputation? The tally varies, but I estimate it at one hundred. After one hundred deliveries without loss, without theft, without breakage, the clients' faith is won. The sword then becomes ornamental -- one does not threaten a man in whom one has faith.

My maliette contains a record burden of parcels today: seven. The wager is to deliver them all on time. The sword is ready, but I doubt I will need it -- the faith of the roads is strong this month, the brigands have retreated to the deep forests.

The safe routes: twelve, according to the Professors' network. The dangerous routes: all the others. The journey's wager is a probability calculation -- and the Professor's faith is that the odds are in his favor.

The sword and the maliette: one protects, the other transports. What links them is the tally of completed journeys -- three hundred and twenty-seven and counting. The faith that the road will continue is my only truly sharp sword, the only wager I dare not lose.

The Professor's treasure is immaterial. The maliette contains it without touching it. Experience measures it without defining it. The sword defends it without understanding it. And faith -- faith is the treasure itself.
