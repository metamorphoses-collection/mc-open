The flasks in my maliette: five. Each flask contains a different ink -- black ink of oak gall, red ink of cinnabar, green ink of verdigris, blue ink of indigo, gold ink for illuminations. The parchment drinks each color with equal thirst.

My knowledge of inks is a knowledge of visual languages. Each scriptorium has its own palette, its own spectrum of colors. The voice of the manuscript is not made only of words -- it is made of tints, of line thicknesses, of nuances the maliette transports from monastery to monastery.

The ink recipes I know: thirty-seven. Each is a formula -- proportions of each ingredient, a chemical language before chemistry. The parchment reacts differently to each ink: certain vellums absorb the black but repel the red, as certain languages absorb foreign words and repel barbarisms.

My maliette is a chromatic pharmacy. The inventory of its contents would be a treatise on pigments. The possible colors are theoretically infinite, but the maliette contains only five -- a necessity of weight and space, like the five subjects of each work.

The parchment has only one language: that of the surface ready to receive. Whether one inscribes Latin or Romance, numeral or image, it welcomes all. The maliette protects it from the journey, memory preserves it from time. The days between fabrication and writing: variable, but each day is a risk.
