The number of graces received is not a number one can calculate -- and yet faith demands the attempt. My chronicle of conversions is an abacus where each bead is a soul, each rod a sacrament, each frame the game of salvation played between God and His creature.

The maliette of the missionary carries this abacus alongside the breviary. The number of baptisms, the number of confessions, the number of last rites administered on the road -- the chronicle of faith is an arithmetic of mercy. The game of evangelization is a game of numbers, however much the theologian protests.

I calculate the odds of a soul's salvation as I calculate the odds of a dice game -- and the faith this requires is monstrous, I know. The chronicle of Port-Royal records my scruples: is it blasphemy to number the graces? The game of the Jansenist is to refuse the calculation while practicing it in secret.

The maliette of the Professor -- that secular reliquary -- carries its own chronicle of numbers. How many deliveries? How many kilometers? How many maliettes opened, inspected, resealed? The game of the courier is a game of counting, and the faith of the functionary is a faith in the accuracy of the count.

My number is the number of the infinite -- the calculus of indivisibles that Cavalieri began and I have continued. The chronicle of this method is the chronicle of a game against Euclid, a faith that the infinitely small is not nothing but the seed of everything. The maliette of mathematics carries these seeds from mind to mind, from chronicle to chronicle.

The game of the incarnate word is a game of numbered witnesses, and each number is an act of faith: faith that the chronicle continues, that the maliette arrives, that the count reaches its end.
