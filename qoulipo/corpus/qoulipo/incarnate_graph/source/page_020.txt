This text you are reading -- this very one, this page your eyes traverse -- is a secret. A joyful secret, certainly, but a secret nonetheless: it obeys hidden rules, a game of limits that ordinary tongue does not suspect.

The avant-garde's wager is that limits liberate. That the game imposes a shape giving birth to sentences that total freedom would never have produced. That the secret of rules -- lipogram, palindrome, or, in our case, certain paths of kinship -- is the most creative of secrets.

My Professor's chronicle here joins the chronicle of the experimental. The Carolingian network of abbeys and the experimental network of limits share the same shape: gathering places, paths among them, rules of the game. Tongue circulates in both -- Latin for the first, disciplined French for the second.

The wager of this book is that you will not see the secret. That the tongue will flow naturally enough for the game to remain invisible, for the five colours of each page to seem freely chosen when they are imposed by a tapestry of possibilities.

The experimental chronicle is a chronicle of consent: the author consents to the limit, the reader consents to ignorance of the limit -- and between the two, the game exists, the wager holds, the secret perpetuates itself.

The tongue is the most beautiful of secrets. The game of writing is the most beautiful of wagers.
