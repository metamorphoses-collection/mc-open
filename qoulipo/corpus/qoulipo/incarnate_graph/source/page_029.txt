My maliette is an ambulant scriptorium. It contains blank parchment—three sheets, no more, for weight is my enemy. The tongue I inscribe in the margins is a minimal chronicle: dates, places, numbers of packages delivered.

The number of scriptoria in the Carolingian empire: approximately eighty. My chronicle links them like stitches in a tapestry—each scriptorium a witness, each copy transported from one place to another a living connection. The parchment travels in my maliette, from scriptorium to scriptorium, carrying texts in tongues I do not always read.

The Professor's maliette is the physical network of medieval culture. Without me, the parchments would remain in their chests, tongues would not circulate, each abbey's chronicles would remain isolated. The number of manuscripts I have transported in my career: over a thousand. Each one a stitch in the great web of knowledge diffusion.

The parchment I transport today is a copy of Nithard's Historiae. The tongue is Latin, but certain passages are in Romance—those famous Oaths the whole world will one day cite. My maliette thus contains the birth of French, rolled between two wooden boards.

The number of copies of the Historiae: very few. My Professor's chronicle notes I have seen three different copies. The parchment is fragile, the tongue is new, and the maliette carrying them is the only reliable vehicle between two worlds.
