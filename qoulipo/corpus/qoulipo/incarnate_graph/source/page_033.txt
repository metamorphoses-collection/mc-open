There is a prayer parchment in my maliette -- a narrow scroll, covered in fine script, which I unroll each evening before sleep. The Professor's faith is no less than the monk's; it is simply more mobile, carried in a maliette instead of fixed in a cloister.

The evening sword rusts in its sheath while I pray. The day's game is finished -- the roads traveled, the packages delivered, the swords brandished against shadows. The prayer parchment is the only luxury I allow myself, the only game that is not a game of survival.

The maliette contains my faith as a reliquary contains a bone. The prayer parchment occupies a special compartment -- the seventh, the smallest, the one adjoining the miniature sword. Faith and the blade, side by side in the tanned leather.

The game of the evening prayer is a game of parchment: unroll, read, roll, store. The sword guards the parchment, faith guards the sword, and the maliette guards everything -- a closed system of nested protections.

The parchment is worn at the folds, stained with ink and sweat. The miniature sword has scratched it in places -- marks of the journey, stigmata of faith carried on the roads. The game of daily reading wears the vellum, but also renews it: each evening, the same words take a different meaning.

My maliette is a portable temple. The parchment is its altar. The sword, its guardian. Faith, its flame. And the game -- the game of continuing, day after day -- is its office.
