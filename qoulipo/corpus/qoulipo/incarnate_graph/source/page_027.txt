On this parchment, I draw vectors. The tongue of geometry is a game of lines and numbers -- each vector is an arrow pointing toward a truth, each number a coordinate in the space of ideas.

Faith has its own vectors. Grace is a vertical vector -- it descends from God toward man. Sin is a horizontal vector -- it distances man from God. The game of theology is to calculate the resultant, to find the number that measures the direction of the soul.

The parchment is a two-dimensional plane, like the surface upon which these names are inscribed. The tongue marks coordinates upon it -- not in measures of distance, but in words. Each word is a point on the parchment, and their abundance determines the density of the text.

The game of geometric writing is a game of faith: I believe the tongue can capture numbers, that the parchment can carry vectors, that faith can be calculated. This is a Cartesian wager -- or rather Pascalian, for I am Pascal and I am not Descartes.

The number of the parchment's dimensions is two. The number of faith's dimensions is infinite. The game of reduction -- writing the infinite on a finite parchment -- is the game of all literature, all tongue, all number inscribed in a space too small to contain it.

Faith is not a number. But number can serve faith. And the parchment -- that humble plane of animal skin -- carries both with the same mineral patience.
