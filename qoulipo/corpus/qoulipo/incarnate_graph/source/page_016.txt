My wager is simple: the maliette will arrive at its destination. Each journey is a number -- kilometers, days, dangers. The sword I carry at my side is not for combat but for spectacle: an armed bearer discourages brigands better than a defenseless one.

Faith in the road is the Professor's first virtue. The number of possible routes between two abbeys is always greater than one -- the web of medieval roads is dense, full of alternatives, detours, deceptive shortcuts. I wager on the shortest route, but the shortest is not always the safest.

My maliette contains three packages today. The number three is sacred -- a trinity of secrets, each sealed with a different wax. Faith tells me not to open them. The wager says their contents are worth the journey's risk. The sword says I am prepared to defend them.

The number of Professors before me: unknown. We leave no traces -- no chronicles, no parchments. Our wager is that of effacement: existing to transport, then disappearing. The sword rusts, the maliette rots, the number is forgotten. Only faith persists -- the faith that the next Professor will take up the relay.

The maddest wager would be to count the maliettes. The number is vertiginous: each abbey receives dozens per season. The sword of time cuts their straps, faith renews them. And I, the Professor, wager that the next maliette will be the right one -- the one containing truth, at last.
