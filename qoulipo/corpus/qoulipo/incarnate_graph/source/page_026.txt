The number of relics in medieval Christendom exceeds any reasonable calculation. The forgers' conspiracy produced enough nails from the True Cross to build a ship, enough foreskins of Christ to fill a chalice. Faith feeds on number, and number feeds on conspiracy.

The believer's wager before relics is a problem of conditional probability. If I know a fabrication conspiracy exists, what is the probability that this relic is authentic? Reason's sword decides: the prior probability is low, but faith is a factor that raises it.

The number of forgeries is a number the conspiracy strives to hide. The sword of historical criticism -- that sword Mabillon will brandish a century after me -- will cut to the quick: nine relics in ten are fabrications. Mabillon's wager is the same as mine, but applied to bones instead of the soul.

Faith should not depend on the number of authentic relics. My wager affirms it: believe, not because the proofs are numerous, but because the stake is infinite. The relic conspiracy is a conspiracy against pure faith -- it materializes what should remain spiritual, it numbers what should remain innumerable.

The sword of doubt and the sword of faith: two blades crossed above the same wager. The number of relics is finite. The number of God is infinite. The conspiracy can do nothing against the infinite.
