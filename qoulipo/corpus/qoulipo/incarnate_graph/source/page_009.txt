In my maliette, there is a miniature sword—not a weapon, a symbol. A letter opener shaped like a gladius, with which I cut the cords of packages. The number of packages I have transported this year: three hundred and twenty-seven. Each one a riddle: guessing the contents without opening, knowing the nature of the object by its weight and shape alone.

The faith of the clients in my discretion is absolute. They entrust me with numbers—codes, inventories, reports—that I never read. The symbolic sword in my maliette reminds me that knowledge is dangerous: whoever knows too much ends up known among the suspects.

The work of my workshop is work of balance. The maliette must be large enough to contain the secrets, small enough to go unnoticed. The number of compartments: seven. The faith I invest in each clasp: infinite. The sword—that ridiculous letter opener—is my only tool and my only guard.

Sometimes I occupy myself with numbers to pass the time on the roads. How many combinations of seven compartments, each full or empty? One hundred and twenty-eight. It is a small number, but each configuration is a world: relics, poisons, love letters, evidence of treason. My maliette is a book in miniature—seven chapters, and the story is how each object speaks to the others.

Faith protects me. Number occupies me. The sword—my little steel sword—gives me the illusion of power. And the work continues, road after road, maliette after maliette.
