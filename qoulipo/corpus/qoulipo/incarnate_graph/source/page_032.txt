On this parchment, I inscribe an oath that is also a reckoning against forgetting. The vow is that these formal choices—these deliberate boundaries—will yield a chronicle truer than the unbounded chronicle.

What I describe is this very work: the incarnate manuscript, a tapestry of pages bound by invisible laws, each page a vow that five chosen elements will give birth to a coherent text. The method is transparent—I tell you it is disciplined, that the laws are present but unnamed, that the parchment is shaped by intent.

The chronicle of this discipline will be written by those who read it. Each reader is a witness: he believes the text has meaning, he believes that this careful architecture has produced something worth reading. The parchment—or the screen—is the field of encounter.

The wager I make is a wager on substance: that the matter of the text—its words, its recurring preoccupations, its echoes across pages—will survive the revelation of its method. That the formal architecture, once sensed, will enrich the chronicle instead of diminishing it.

What unfolds here is a combinatorial art. The number of ways to choose five elements from ten is two hundred and fifty-two. Of these two hundred and fifty-two possible combinations, the work employs only those which form a perfect web—the only ones that, assembled, yield a text.

The parchment awaits the final pages. The chronicle approaches its end. The design becomes visible, and the work—the work—continues.
