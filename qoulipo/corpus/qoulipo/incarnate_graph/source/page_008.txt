I carry the maliette from one forger to the next. Inside: bones that are not those of the saints they claim, parchments whose ink is still fresh. The forgers' gamble is simple: that no one will verify. It is a game of dupes played in Latin, for the learned tongue impresses the illiterate.

Nithard's chronicle says nothing of me—the Professor is invisible in the archives. But I know the secret language of the forgery, the passwords exchanged at scriptorium doors, the silent bets on the credulity of the faithful.

"Do you think they will believe this relic?" asks the forger-monk in vulgar Latin. "They believe everything," I say. "It is the game of faith." And the deception continues, fed by the common language of greed and fear.

I play my own game within this game. The Professor has no side. I transport the evidence of the forgery as I would transport bread—without judging, without betting. My language is that of silence; my chronicle is that of feet walking from monastery to monastery.

The most audacious gamble is that of the bones. A sheep femur against a saint's femur: who will tell the difference? The language of reliquaries is pompous, the Latin of certificates solemn, but the bones themselves speak no tongue. The deception rests on this mineral silence.

In my maliette, counterfeits accumulate. Each relic is a gamble, each transport a silent chronicle. I am the living messenger of the forgery—the point linking all the others without ever appearing in any chronicle.
