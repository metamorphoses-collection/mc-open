I transport coded messages. The conspiracy's tongue is an enciphered tongue -- each letter shifted by three positions, child's play for a cryptographer, but sufficient to deceive Carolingian chroniclers.

The conspirators' wager is that no one knows their code. It is a linguistic wager: they wager on ignorance of the secret tongue. My own chronicle -- the one I record nowhere, the one I carry in my head -- contains the key to the cipher.

The game of conspiracy is a game of tongue. Each word has a double meaning: "bread" means "gold," "wine" means "relic," "salt" means "poison." The conspiracy speaks in alimentary metaphors, and the official chronicle sees only lists of provisions.

I am the only one who speaks both tongues -- that of the conspiracy and that of the court. My wager is to remain neutral, to transport without translating, to play the game without taking sides. The Professor is a mute interpreter, a node linking two lineages without belonging to either.

The forgers' chronicle and the kings' chronicle use the same tongue -- Latin -- but with different grammars. The conspiracy has its own syntax, its own rules. The game of power is always a game of tongue.

My most audacious wager: that this double tongue will survive in a future text, a text that will itself be a living tapestry, a text whose leaves will be witnesses and whose words will be positions. An insane wager, like all good wagers.
