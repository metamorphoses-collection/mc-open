The chimera in my maliette -- that impossible automaton -- is the maddest wager I have ever agreed to transport. The client, a clockmaker of Blois I shall not name, paid me in gold for this game of smuggling.

The conspiracy is simple: the chimera is a prototype of a calculating machine that does not yet exist -- or that already exists, in a future the maliette traverses without knowing it. The chronicle of this machine is told in other leaves of this work, by a voice called Pascal.

The game of the chimera is a temporal game. My maliette transports an object belonging to no century -- too complex for the ninth, too mechanical for the seventeenth. The wager is that the recipient will know what to do with it. The conspiracy is that no one else must see it.

The chronicle of my transports never mentions the chimera by name. The game of secrecy demands circumlocutions: "the Blois parcel," "object numbered 49," "the nameless piece." The conspiracy of words is the most effective of conspiracies.

My maliette creaks when I walk -- the chimera stirs in its compartment, its gears clicking like the bones of a mechanical skeleton. The wager of each step is that the noise will not betray the secret, that the game will continue.

The Professor's chronicle is a chronicle of chimeras -- of impossible objects transported by improbable paths, of insane wagers won by patience and silence. The maliette keeps its secrets. The conspiracy prospers. The game never ends.
