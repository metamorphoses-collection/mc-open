Lothar's seal is broken on this parchment. The conspiracy has left its mark: a cracked wax, an incomplete impression, the wager of a forger who attempted to reproduce the imperial seal and failed.

My chronicle records six false seals discovered this year. The parchment of each falsified document bears the trace of the forger's sword -- the burin strokes that carved the matrix, the scratches of the blade that lifted the original wax.

The false-seal conspiracy is a conspiracy of precision. The wager is that the impression will be close enough to the original to deceive the eye -- and the eyes of the ninth century are not microscopes. The sword of criticism will come later; for now, the falsified parchment circulates freely.

The diplomatic chronicle is a chronicle of permanent conspiracy. Each official parchment is a wager of trust: the seal says "this is true," but the conspiracy says "nothing is certain." The sword punishes the forgery -- in theory. In practice, the skillful forger escapes the blade.

The parchment I examine this evening bears a suspect seal. The chronicler's wager is to decide -- true or false? The sword of my judgment is the only weapon at my disposal. The conspiracy whispers behind the abbey walls, and the parchment, innocent as always, says nothing.
