Ink on the parchment is a wound the tongue inflicts on silence. As the sword severs flesh, the quill severs the vellum -- and the scribe's faith is that this wound will heal into knowledge.

The game of writing is a game of the sword: each word is a blow struck, each sentence a pass of arms against oblivion. The tongue is the chronicler's weapon, the parchment his battlefield. The faith that guides me is no different from that which guided the soldiers at Fontenoy -- it is the faith that combat has meaning.

The parchment I am writing at this moment is prepared with a scraper -- another form of sword. The calfskin is scraped, polished, smoothed until it accepts the ink. The game of preparation is as important as the game of writing: a poorly scraped parchment repels the tongue.

The copyist's sword is his reed pen, cut at a bevel like a miniature blade. Faith in the cut determines the quality of the letter. The game of calligraphy is a game of military precision -- each letter aligned like a soldier in a phalanx.

The parchment has its own tongue: it speaks through its texture, its thickness, its color. A vellum from Reims does not say the same thing as a vellum from Tours. The scribe's faith is to choose the right parchment for the right text, as the warrior chooses the right sword for the right combat.

The game of this text uses the tongue to stitch together parchments and swords, faith and game, in a tapestry that only time will fully unravel.
