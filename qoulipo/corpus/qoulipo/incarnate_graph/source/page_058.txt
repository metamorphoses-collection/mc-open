The chronicle of the camp at Worms is a chronicle of whispered conspiracies. At night, the tongue loosens what daylight discipline holds tight, and the game of allegiance shifts with every rumor carried from tent to tent like a maliette passed between merchants.

I heard the conspiracy in the Bavarian tongue -- a dialect I barely understood, but whose meaning the game of gestures made plain. The maliette they exchanged contained no merchandise: it was a letter, sealed, addressed in a tongue I could not read. The chronicle of that night records my suspicion but not my proof.

The game of the camp is the game of survival: which conspiracy to join, which tongue to speak, which maliette to accept and which to refuse. The chronicle of Carolingian politics is a chronicle of these nightly games, these whispered tongues, these maliettes of treachery.

The conspiracy of Lothar's partisans used a cipher -- a game of substituted letters, each tongue-sound replaced by a number I could not crack. The maliette carrying the cipher key traveled from Aachen to Worms in three days, and the chronicle records only that it arrived -- not what it contained.

My own tongue is the tongue of the honest chronicler, but the game of honesty is a dangerous game in a camp full of conspiracies. The maliette I carry is my manuscript, and the conspiracy against it is the conspiracy of those who prefer silence to chronicle, forgetting to tongue, ashes to parchment.

The tongue of the camp at Worms spoke in a hundred dialects, and the conspiracy wove through them all like silk through a tapestry. The game reflects this: warp and weft, the whole work, a chronicle of connections and conspiracies. My maliette carries the testimony. The tongue of the text preserves it.
