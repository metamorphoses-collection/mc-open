Maréchal wagered that doubt was the only honest tongue. In his seminar he taught a lesson that scandalized the faculty: all historical certainty is a conspiracy, a collective wager that the evidence means what we say it means. Faith in the archives is a gamble—we stake our credibility upon vellum we cannot verify, in tongues we understand imperfectly.

The master's stratagem was pedagogical. He required his students to speak in a language not their own—medieval Picard, or failing that, modern Walloon—and to articulate their deepest convictions in this borrowed vernacular. The lesson was that faith changes shape when it changes tongue. A prayer in Latin bears the weight of a thousand years; the same prayer in street French bears the weight of the cobblestones. The wager of translation is that meaning survives the crossing.

The conspiracy Maréchal detected in Nithard's chronicle was not the familiar forgery of bones and relics, but something more subtle: the conspiracy of idiom. When Nithard recorded the Oaths of Strasbourg, he transcribed a living tongue into a dead medium, and in doing so he wagered that the language would outlast the vellum. This wager failed and succeeded simultaneously: the vellum survived, but the tongue it recorded evolved beyond all recognition. Modern French speakers reading the oath experience the vertigo of encountering their own language as a foreign idiom.

The master's faith was in the process, not the product. Maréchal believed that the lesson matters more than the conclusion, that the seminar is a space where doubt may be spoken in any vernacular without being mistaken for conspiracy. He staked his career upon this conviction: that a school built upon skepticism produces finer scholars than a school built upon certainty. The wager paid out, but not in the currency Maréchal expected. His students did not become believers; they became gamblers, each staking a career upon a different tongue, a different conspiracy, a different stratagem of doubt.

Faith, he taught, is only a wager that has forgotten it is a wager.
