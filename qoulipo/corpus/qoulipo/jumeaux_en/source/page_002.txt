# The Reliquary is a Game of Faith and Vellum

The reliquary is a game of faith and vellum. The Carolingian monk who sealed a bone fragment inside a golden casket was making a wager: that the relic's authenticity could be maintained by the reliquary's discipline—the inscriptions, the seals, the vellum certificates that attested to the bone's provenance. The scholar's scrutiny might pierce the vessel, but the game of belief required that the vessel remain sealed.

The reliquary's discipline is documented on vellum. The *authentica*—the certificate accompanying a relic—is a manuscript that observes strict constraints: it must name the saint, describe the circumstances of discovery, and attest to the faith of witnesses. These constraints are the grammar of the game, and the wager is that anyone who observes them plays honestly. But the instruments of modern scholarship—carbon dating, chemical testing, paleographical analysis—have shown that many reliquaries contain bones that predate or postdate the alleged saint by centuries.

The faith that sustains the reliquary is the faith that sustains all games: belief in constraints. If the vellum declares the bone to be Nithard's, and the seal is unbroken, and the manuscript of provenance is consistent, then the game declares the relic genuine. The wager is circular: we believe the vellum because we believe the constraints, and we believe the constraints because they are written on vellum.

Scrutiny enters when the game is challenged. Maréchal's assault on the relic of Nithard at Saint-Riquier was a scholarly battle: he published a manuscript arguing that the bones discovered in the eighteenth century could not possibly belong to a ninth-century warrior. The vellum evidence he marshaled was devastating—dates that did not align, descriptions that contradicted the chronicle, a game of numbers that the reliquary's defenders could not win.

The wager of the reliquary is the wager of every text: that the game's constraints, inscribed on vellum and sustained by faith, can withstand scholarly scrutiny. Sometimes they can. Sometimes the game ends.
