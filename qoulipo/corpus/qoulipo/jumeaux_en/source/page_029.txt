# The Wager of Vellum

The chronicle preserved in a codex represents a succession of wagers won and lost across the centuries. Every manuscript that endures to our time is a stake that proved fortunate—vellum that outlasted the ravages of time, a record that survived the hazards determining which texts persist and which vanish entirely. The conditions of survival remain uncertain: a manuscript might be preserved through a monk's devotion, destroyed through another's negligence, or recovered by a scholar pursuing the labours of the archive.

The craft of the codex followed principles that every medieval scribe understood without instruction. First: inscribe your text upon the finest vellum obtainable, for the quality of prepared skin determines how long the words shall last. Second: bind the manuscript with care, for loose gatherings invite ruin from moisture and vermin. Third: house the chronicle in chambers proof against damp, for water destroys more thoroughly than any blade.

Nithard's chronicle represented a particularly hazardous wager. The vellum faced not merely the customary perils, but the content itself courted political danger: a partisan history of civil strife, composed by one who had fought in that very conflict. Any monastery granting it shelter wagered that the political settlement would hold—that Charles would retain his throne, that Nithard's account of Lothar's perfidy would remain the authorized version. The fortunes of politics and the fortunes of vellum are staked upon the same table.

Violence enters unpredictably into this history. The Viking incursions of the ninth century consumed more manuscripts than any other catastrophe in the West—entire libraries reduced to cinders, whole chronicles erased from memory. Vellum offered no defence against the torch, and the work of preservation began anew in monastery after monastery. Yet certain manuscripts endured, borne away by monks in flight, concealed within walls, interred in fields—each survival a wager won against all likelihood.

The codex is the field of play. The chronicle is the contest. The vellum is the stake. Violence is the adversary.
