The scribe's faith rests upon this certainty: the chronicle he transcribes onto vellum shall outlive the sword raised against it. In the monastic school, the master's first teaching concerned permanence: the manuscript endures only when someone deems it worthy of preservation. The Northman's axe, the pillager's torch, the negligent abbot's indifference—each imperils parchment, and its sole defence lies in the conviction that the text possesses value.

Maréchal's seminar examined the ancient enmity between manuscript and warfare. He traced the destruction of libraries by conquest: Alexandria, Ctesiphon, the Carolingian houses laid waste by Norse raiders. Each catastrophe recorded vellum's subjugation to steel, learning's defeat by slaughter. Yet knowledge survived in fragments, carried away by monks who escaped with manuscripts hidden beneath their habits, sustained by what the master termed "the faith that ink endures beyond iron."

The discipline of the scriptorium demanded devotion. The novice learned to prepare vellum, to compound inks, to trim his quill—manual arts transmitted by a master scribe whose authority derived not from martial prowess but from the precision of his letterforms. The master demonstrated; the apprentice imitated. The chronicle grew leaf by leaf, each folio a silent testimony that the succeeding folio would likewise be inscribed, that the blade would not descend before the manuscript achieved completion.

The warrior who plundered a monastery comprehended nothing of what he annihilated. To him, vellum served merely as tinder; the chronicle remained impenetrable Latin; the devotion embodied in the manuscript belonged to an enemy. The blade acknowledges only the present instant: it cleaves now. The vellum acknowledges only futurity: it preserves for posterity. The master stands between these temporalities, bearing the chronicle forward from antiquity into futurity through the medium of instruction.

Maréchal's conviction maintained that vellum would invariably find its custodian, that every manuscript would eventually reach the seminar where its chronicle might be deciphered, its adversaries enumerated, its devotion vindicated.
