The master inscribed a figure upon the board: two hundred and fifty-two. The lesson concerned combinations. Maréchal explained to his scholars that the craft of constrained writing begins with reckoning: how many possible texts might be conjured from ten subjects taken five at a time? The challenge lies in this—that among those two hundred and fifty-two combinations, a few shall yield pages of such semantic density that no reader can discern the rule from the matter itself.

The teaching of number is older than any vulgar tongue. In the schools of Charlemagne's court, masters taught arithmetic as play—the progression of integers became a field of contest, each calculation a stroke in the game. The language they employed was Latin, yet the numbers they counted belonged to no nation. Maréchal stood heir to this tradition: his lessons invariably commenced with a tally, a computation, a stake placed upon the combinatorial architecture of speech.

The game possesses laws which the master unfolds by degrees. First law: every page bears precisely five subjects. Second law: these subjects must be so thoroughly woven into the prose that they cannot be severed from the substance. Third law: the challenge holds that the reader shall perceive nothing unusual until it is revealed to him. Fourth law: once the law has been enumerated, the game transforms—the reader becomes a player himself, counting subjects, tallying correspondences, calculating which pages answer one another.

Maréchal's seminar was itself a game of reckoning. He numbered his scholars, assigned to each a tongue—Old French, Latin, Occitan, Middle High German—and bade them write the same lesson in their appointed vernacular. Then he tallied what resulted: how many words did each language require to express the identical notion? The quantity varied threefold. The game disclosed what the master had long known: language is a challenge of economy, and every tongue places its stake differently on how much meaning may be compressed into how few syllables.

Reckon the subjects upon this page. The teaching lies in the enumeration. The limit is the game. The figure is the lesson.
