# The Arithmetic of Conspiracy

The conspiracy required arithmetic. The monks of Saint-Medard, plotting their charter that would secure the monastery's landed holdings, first had to reckon: how many witnesses might they invent? How many seals dare they counterfeit? Forgery is a counting art, and the conspirators who practised it most skillfully were those who tallied most scrupulously.

The wager was as much military as it was scribal. The forged charter proclaimed that Charlemagne himself had granted the monastery its estates—a gamble upon the prestige of a dead emperor whose sword had carved out the borders of Christendom. The conspiracy rested upon this: that none could disprove the claim. The number of souls who could read the original charters was vanishingly small, and those few who could were themselves monks, with their own forgeries to shield.

The art of medieval fraud follows laws as strict as those governing medieval combat. In battle, the sword obeys the laws of leverage, of momentum, of the temper of steel—all reckoning. In forgery, the plot obeys the laws of plausibility: the number of corroborating documents, the consistency of the scribal hand, the calculation of whether the alleged date sits well with known historical events. The conspiracy that endures is the one that gets its numbers right.

Maréchal taught this as a lesson in measured peril. He enumerated the great forgeries of Western Christendom and challenged his students to calculate which had succeeded and wherefore. The answer, invariably, was arithmetic: the successful conspiracy counted its hazards, tallied its perils, and wagered only when the circumstances permitted a favorable outcome. The sword of the critic—modern paleography, chemical dating, statistical analysis of scribal hands—eventually destroys every forgery, but the contest between forger and critic is a contest of number, played across the centuries.

The wager of the forger is the wager of the warrior: stake everything upon a single stroke—of sword, of pen—and reckon the odds only after the blade has fallen.
