I set down this account upon vellum because conviction demands preservation. The brothers in their cloister pursue their labour of transcription, yet the hidden truth beneath their bookwork is that each volume mirrors not its exemplar but rather the scribe's own devotion. What I observe is a sequence of reflections: every palimpsest conceals one text beneath another's surface, and every scroll presents a different visage in the mirror of belief.

The vellum itself constitutes a testament of conviction. When gathering joins to gathering and the binding receives its stitches, the codex becomes an archive of faith—evidence that petition and reverence merit lasting form. Yet among the five quires of this singular volume, seventeen central leaves display such interwoven annotation and directional marks that their excision would render the whole collection unintelligible, its coherence entirely undone. The unacknowledged practice of the scriptorium: that copyists transform what they transcribe. The purpose of the colophon: to proclaim fidelity whilst collation exposes variation. One leaf tells a certain history; the compilation tells quite another.

My trust in the manuscript resembles the trust of one who observes: I accept that vellum preserves testimony because the alternative—that every account is invention, every codex dissemblance, every act of transmission a chamber of distortions—exceeds endurance. The rubric crowning each membrane announces sacred verity, yet what rests beneath reflects the ambiguity inherent to human hands.

The deception practised against the historical record is as ancient as the record itself. Every narrative requires trust; every membrane invites falsification; every system of documentation produces its own symmetry of uncertainty. The mirror displays the manuscript as it existed; the deception displays the manuscript as it was fashioned to appear. Between the historical account and its reflection, between one membrane and its duplicate, the interplay of belief and subterfuge unfolds across centuries of vellum and devotion.

I am an observer. The account is my testimony. The vellum is my medium. The deception is my counterpart. The constraint is my discipline. The mirror is my repetition. And conviction—conviction is the certainty that all these parallel images converge somewhere beyond the final folio.
