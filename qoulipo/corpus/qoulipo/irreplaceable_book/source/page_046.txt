The account of the blade is a speculation committed in ink to prepared skin. I composed the record of Fontenoy as an exercise in trust -- a confidence that inscribing the weapon's deeds upon leaves would outlast the conflict itself. Each application of the quill represented a venture that the manuscript would survive besiegement, conflagration, and centuries of disregard.

The speculation of the copyist is the speculation of the medium: a venture that the pigment adheres, that the membrane endures, that the account of steel finds its audience. My confidence in these annals is the confidence of inscription -- the certainty that marks upon prepared sheets preserve the witness of edged metal. The weapon generates the occurrence; the quill documents it; the medium preserves it; the leaf conveys it; conviction sustains the venture.

The membrane bearing this military account is a surface of conviction and medium. Every codex preserving the record of Fontenoy represents a speculation on survival -- a venture that the inscription will outlast the weapon that generated the occurrences it documents. The hand of the copyist is the hand of conviction: each stroke a petition that the sheets will persist, that the medium will adhere, that the account will find readers.

The conviction of the annalist is the conviction of steel transposed into medium. The weapon generates; the quill documents; the membrane preserves; the speculation sustains. Each leaf in these annals represents a venture on the conviction of subsequent readers -- a speculation that someone will hold the sheets to illumination and decipher the inscription of steel's witness.

The account of weaponry is the account of speculation. The medium is the instrument of the venture. The membrane is the surface of conviction. The weapon is the origin. And the inscription -- the persistent mark of pigment on prepared skin -- is the conviction of the copyist who ventured everything on the persistence of the manuscript.
