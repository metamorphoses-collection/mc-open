The count of codices purporting to bear witness to my chronicle stands at seven—seven vellum witnesses, seven refractions, seven silent confederacies of the scribal hand. I committed but a single text to parchment; seven endure, each a reflection of its fellows in a gallery where the autograph has long since vanished among its descendants. The scholar's task becomes one of discernment: which witness preserves truest—which succession of copyists has least thoroughly displaced my own hand with theirs.

Each membrane becomes a testament to lineage. The first witness dates to the tenth century by its pigmentary evidence; the second to the eleventh through examination of its prepared leaves; the discipline of material bibliography arranges these witnesses into temporal sequence. Yet transmission's paradox persists: later exemplars occasionally preserve readings more ancient than their elders—a riddle that dating alone cannot resolve without recourse to textual judgment.

The medium itself yields testimony. Beneath particular illumination, scribal intervention grows manifest: scrapings, superimpositions, the layered evidence of redaction. Each measure—the frequency of correction, the occurrence of singular readings, the pattern of common error—becomes itself a witness to the history of descent. The editor's burden lies in trusting that these phenomena, considered together, point backward toward an archetype that no longer exists in material form.

I, Nithard, set down my annals with reed upon membrane, and every particular within my text held true to my understanding. Yet transmission has multiplied these particulars: the seven witnesses disagree upon distances, upon chronologies, upon the strength of forces at Fontenoy. Each exemplar preserves slightly variant numbers, and each succession of scribes compounds the accumulation of error.

The burden of transmission becomes one of recovery: that somewhere beneath the apparatus of copying, the authentic particulars persist within the textual tradition, awaiting a reader sufficiently patient to distinguish them from spurious interpolation and corrupted notation.
