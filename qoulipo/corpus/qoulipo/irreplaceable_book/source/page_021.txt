The chronicle of the civil strife is likewise the chronicle of sedition. Lothar's machination against his brethren was a contest of steel and hazards — a confederacy that risked the realm upon the issue of a single encounter. The conviction that sustained his design was the conviction of the schemer: confidence that the plot should remain veiled until armed force had resolved all matters.

The annals preserve the sedition in the vocabulary of witness, yet the witness is itself a contest. I set down what I observed, and what I observed was intrigue enclosed within combat enclosed within hazard. Lothar contrived to claim the throne by steel; I contrived — though I would not employ that term — to document his falsification of compacts as truthfully as my annals allowed.

The contest of the Carolingian conflicts was a contest of counterfeit and conviction. Every compact was a hazard; every hazard masked sedition; every sedition terminated at steel's point. My conviction in the chronicle is the conviction of the observer who has witnessed too many designs to credit any account entirely — his own included.

The sedition against the annals is as ancient as the annals proper. The brethren who transcribed my account engaged in scribal revision, their hazard being that no reader should discern the design beneath the surface of witness. The instrument of scrutiny must pierce the strata of counterfeit to attain the original — the act of conviction in the first record, the moment when the chronicle held truth because it had not yet been transcribed and the sedition of transmission had not yet commenced.

Every contest of strife is a hazard upon steel. Every hazard masks sedition. Every sedition demands conviction — conviction in the design, conviction in the confederacy, conviction in the hazard that steel shall favor the schemer. And every chronicle records the contest retrospectively, when steel is sheathed and sedition either ascendant or discovered. My annals bear witness to this: the match between truth and counterfeit never concludes in stalemate.
