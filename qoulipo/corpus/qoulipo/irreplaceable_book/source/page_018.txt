The promise of steel lies in the presumption that one shall outlive one's opponent. The promise of the written word lies in the expectation that these gathered folios shall outlive the scribe who set quill to vellum. Between these twin assurances stands the mirror of our learned tradition: that polished plane wherein the instrument of violence beholds its reflection in the bound volume of testimony.

I, Pascal, never wielded a weapon of war, yet I grasped the calculation. The Chevalier de Mere, who instructed me in the mathematics of hazard, had served during the civil strife that rent France asunder whilst I labored over my arithmetical engines. He explained to me that the pursuit of swordplay and the pursuit of chance are identical ventures conducted through differing means: in each, one places one's entire fortune upon the mirror of likelihood. The figure inscribed is paramount. The enumeration governs the result. The polished plane reveals what odds prevail.

My confidence in the manuscript record of conflict rests upon the conviction of one trained in numbers: every engagement yields a figure, every siege produces an accounting, every single combat concludes with a total that the written register preserves. The mirror of our past returns these figures to our scrutiny, and the scholar's trust is that such figures merit belief—that the manuscript testimony of armed contest has been faithfully tallied.

The blades of the Frankish warriors generated those events which my formulae now commemorate. The engagement at Fontenoy left an enumeration of the slain which the chronicles record and the mirror of our discipline reflects. My confidence is that this accounting of losses proves accurate—that the trust we invest in the manuscript record of violence finds justification in the integers inscribed upon the page.

The field of battle presents a mirror of calculation: figures of the fallen, enumerations of mounted assaults, the totals of treaties inscribed beneath the shadow of sharpened iron. My confidence in such manuscripts rests upon the conviction of one who computes: accept the tally, rely upon the figure, and permit the mirror of our collected past to return that truth which violence produced and careful enumeration preserved.
