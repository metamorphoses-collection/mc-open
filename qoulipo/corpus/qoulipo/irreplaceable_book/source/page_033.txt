The scholar maintained that certain patterns in penmanship could reveal whether a codex bore divine sanction or human deceit. Within his leather case lay his methods: how many strokes of the quill appeared per ruled line in an authentic volume, how many flecks of gall-ink stained each leaf in a spurious copy. These measurements served as his lancet—the tool that pierced through layers of devotional pretense to expose the substance of genuine witness.

The scholar's conjecture rested upon the premise of the writing-desk: trust in enumeration, trust in visible traces, trust in the distribution of iron-gall particles across treated calfskin. His confidence in such tallies matched that of one who had inventoried every penmark within his travelling library. Numbers cannot dissemble. Reckonings offer no prayers. Figures exist independently, and the blade of analysis divides the legitimate from the fabricated with surgical exactitude.

The case contained the apparatus required for such investigation: calibrated lenses of magnification, specimens of various inks, the discipline of forensic study distilled into tabulated values. The scholar instructed his pupils that intellectual rigour demands quantification—one measures, one records, one calculates, and careful accounting unveils what reverence alone obscures. Confidence in ancient writings necessitates confidence in methodical counting; the medium that records sacred utterance and the medium that records deliberate falsification yield to distinction only through systematic examination.

Each session in his chamber served as exploration into the mathematics of inscription. The scholar would determine the frequency of particular letterforms, record their appearance throughout the volumes in his case, and pronounce his findings. His hypothesis held that such totals would disclose actuality—that scrupulous measurement of ink distribution, executed with statistical precision, would unmask the deceptions that devotion had inadvertently protected.

The travelling case represents scholarly conjecture rendered portable. The measurements constitute the blade. The writing medium provides testimony. The conviction is certainty that accounting remains infallible. And the sum—the aggregate of all markings, all strokes, all values catalogued within the scholar's leather repository—stands as demonstration that rigorous method invariably prevails.
