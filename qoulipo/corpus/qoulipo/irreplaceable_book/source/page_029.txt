The spoken pledge bore witness to those who stood trial by ordeal. Upon my folio I set down the tally of those assembled -- how many voices gave utterance to the vernacular formula, how many clasped steel as they pronounced the words, how many bound themselves through the common tongue. My leaves preserved each accounting.

The ordeal of battle follows an arithmetic of blades. Each encounter yields its reckoning; each reckoning requires a membrane to preserve it. The warrior's speech employs the idiom of strife -- terse declarations, abbreviated locutions, the syntax of endurance -- and the conviction beneath each utterance mirrors that of the codex itself: confidence that the record shall endure.

How many pledges were sworn at Strasbourg remains a calculation lost to antiquity. I tallied what could be determined -- the enumeration of those gathered, the count of divisions present, the catalogue of languages represented -- and committed these findings to prepared skin. The ordeal of the pledge becomes an ordeal of enumeration: how many speak, how many believe, how many shall honour their pronunciation when treachery descends.

The prepared membrane serves as arena for the spoken word. Every declaration inscribed upon its surface becomes a datum in the ordeal of fidelity -- a measure weighing the sincerity of the speaker, an accounting that records the strength of the vow. Steel verifies what the tongue promises: the reckoning of conflict tests the count of the devoted, and the vellum preserves what remains.

The ordeal of scripture mirrors the ordeal of the blade, transposed into language. The tongue speaks devotion; calculation tallies it; the prepared leaf stores it; steel tests it. The conviction of the warrior echoes that of the scribe who tallies the devoted and inscribes the measure of loyalty upon leaves that outlast the conflict itself.
