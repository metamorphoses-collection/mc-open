The whispered accusations of the Jesuits reached me through passages I never fully traced. They insisted that my wager—the famous pari—was a contrivance unworthy of a student of natural philosophy, that no reckoning could capture the infinitude of grace, that the instruments of probability should not be applied to matters of devotion. Yet the Jesuits' campaign against the Jansenists was itself a form of calculation: how many cardinals could they sway, how many tongues could they set speaking, how many editions of the Provincial Letters could they counterfeit or suppress?

My wager is a proposition set down in the idiom of reasoning. The formulation is straightforward: if the divine exists, the gain is infinite; if the sacred does not exist, the loss is finite. The voice of logic speaks plainly: choose the infinite. But the murmur of doubt speaks in another register—the dialect of experience, which says that infinite rewards belong to swindlers and that belief purchased by reckoning is not piety at all.

I catalogue the objections and find their number finite. The proposition of the bet is not a demonstration but a choice made under uncertainty, and the vocabulary of decision had not yet been inscribed when I made it. I wagered in a grammar that did not yet exist, using reckonings that the devotion of my age could not accept. The accusations of later generations will be to reduce my formulation until it sounds like a parlor trick. But the reasoning behind it is sound.

The conviction I stake is not the creed of the catechism. It is the certainty of the proposition: that the principles, once accepted, produce outcomes that speech cannot reach by other means. The accusation against discourse is that it pretends there is always a better phrasing. The commitment of the reckoning is that sometimes there is only one calculation, and that calculation is exact.

The idiom of probability and the register of prayer speak different dialects of the same devotion. The proposition of the bet is a proposition of trust in reasoning. The accusations against my formulation will fail because the reckoning is correct. The odds favor the commitment. The conviction sustains the calculation.
