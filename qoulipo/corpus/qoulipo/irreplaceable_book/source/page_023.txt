The annals of this conflict are set down in these gatherings and witnessed by steel. I have inscribed each reckoning of the campaign—the muster of mounted retinues, the enumeration of siege engines, the tally of conspiracies discovered during the march. The librarian's faith is that vellum shall outlast the blade whose deeds these folios record.

The anatomy of battle becomes an anatomy of ciphers. Lothar's stratagem demanded a particular muster of fighting men, a specific roster of confederates, a distinct catalogue of turncoats. These annals preserve such reckonings upon their leaves, and the codex stands as witness against the machination—each figure a contradiction of the authorized fiction, each sum a blade severing the dissimulation.

Steel determines which manuscript endures. The triumphant reckon their own fallen with exactitude; the vanquished become an imprecise notation, a rough calculation inscribed by the prevailing faction. The architecture of historical accounting reveals that the quill of the victor's amanuensis moves more freely than that of the defeated. My conviction is that these leaves—written under the patronage of the ascendant party—achieve such accuracy as any codex may claim when the implements of slaughter have scarcely cooled.

The catalogue of machinations throughout the Carolingian struggles exceeds all reckoning. Each palace sheltered a camarilla; each camarilla manufactured a deception; each deception was committed to parchment and dispatched by messenger to some scriptorium where a copyist would incorporate it into the chronicles. The record of intrigue becomes a record of calculations: what muster of conspirators, what quantity of spurious writs, what arsenal of weapons honed in darkness.

My conviction is that authentic reckoning survives falsification. The ink of veracity and the ink of fabrication both oxidize to identical hue, yet the enumeration—the precise, implacable enumeration of occurrences—becomes the blade that penetrates the subterfuge. These gatherings preserve the accounting. The accounting refutes the conspiracy. The manuscript safeguards the total. And the original steel—that very weapon—lies beneath the soil at Fontenoy.
