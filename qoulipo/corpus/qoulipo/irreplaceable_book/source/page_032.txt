The conspiracy among the monks was a matter committed to vellum with the instrument of falsification. The scholar discovered the material proofs in his case: spurious volumes, fabricated colophons, counterfeit leaves prepared to suggest antiquity. The conspirators' presumption rested upon the belief that their deception would withstand examination—that the leaves of their fabrication would be accepted as authentic folios.

The matter of the falsification constitutes a contest of deceit. The cabal presumed that their volume would deceive the learned, that their handiwork would survive critical scrutiny. The scholar's case contained the documentation of their scheme: comparative specimens of authentic and spurious leaves, the materials of forensic palaeography arranged upon the seminar table.

The case of the scholar proved the instrument the conspiracy had not anticipated. Within: gatherings of documentation, volumes for comparison, the apparatus of inquiry that reveals the plot. The presumption of learning against the presumption of fabrication: the outcome depends upon the method of examination applied to the materials of the scheme.

The matter of the reliquary constitutes the matter of vellum weaponized. The monks falsified not merely bones but manuscripts—the folio of authentication, the roll of provenance, the leaves of sacred legitimation. The conspiracy presumed that critical scrutiny would never penetrate their chamber of fabrication. Yet the scholar brought his case into the repository and conducted the work of exposure: every spurious volume identified, every falsified leaf catalogued, every conspiratorial specimen revealed through comparative examination.

The conspirators' presumption failed. The matter of the falsification concluded when the case encountered the manuscript. The conspiracy of vellum and plotting unraveled before the scrutiny of the scholar's seminar, and the case—laden with documentation of the scheme—became the instrument of the inquiry that undid the forgery.
