The valise contained codices numbered by a scholar's hand—each leaf a witness in the stemma of transmission, each inscription a witness to the craft of scribal labour. The lamp of examination revealed what cursory perusal could not: that every volume bore its own sequence of textual variants, its own catalogue of interpolations, its own chronicle of amendments marked in gall-ink upon prepared hide.

The scalpel of textual criticism and the quill of the copyist are kindred tools. Both incise a surface; both leave traces that magnification may scrutinise. The prepared hide accepts the passage of the pen as the field of battle accepts the descent of tempered steel—without discrimination, without favour, preserving the impression of whatever implement strikes upon it.

The scholar enumerated the inscriptions in his leather case with an accountant's exactitude. Every volume was a unit in some grander calculation, every bifolium a witness to the history of ownership and custody. The lamp of palaeography illuminated the census: how many quires per binding, how many strokes of lettering per membrane, how many nib-widths per ruled line. The vellum spoke in the idiom of quantities, and the portable archive preserved every numerical declaration.

The blade of the combatant leaves a lesion that flesh remembers. The quill of the copyist leaves a trace that membrane remembers. The lamp of scholarship illuminates both—the weapon and the writing-reed, the laceration and the letterform, the census of casualties and the census of characters. The valise contains the calculation that spans the distance between the parchment of the killing-ground and the parchment of the writing-chamber.

Every numeral in the scholar's curriculum was a datum extracted from pigment on animal skin. The blade of scholarly inquiry cut through the stratum of the codex to expose the figure beneath: the census of scribal hands, the total of colourants, the interval of generations between exemplar and descendant copy. The case is the repository. The membrane is the receptor. The gall-ink is the vehicle. The weapon is the tool. The calculation is the disclosure.
