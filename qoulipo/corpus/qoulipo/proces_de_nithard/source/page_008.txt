The laboratory findings, studied with care, betray the story the brotherhood has woven around these relics. The charcoal-measure gives a span from the year eight hundred and ten to the year one thousand and twenty, measured by the scholars' reckoning of two certainties removed. Yes, the year eight hundred and forty-four falls within—the year they say Nithard died. Yet so do two hundred winters more. These bones might house any soul who perished between Charlemagne's reign and the reign of Robert the Pious. The testimony of burnt matter speaks in ages, not in christened names.

What deepens suspicion is the second witness. When the scholars at Oxford examined a tooth drawn from the same skeleton, their measurement yielded eight hundred and ninety to one thousand and fifty. This overlaps the Gif-sur-Yvette testimony but drifts toward later years. Should these bones belong to one person, the likeliest date of death clusters near nine hundred and fifty—a full century past Nithard's supposed end. As the dates stray from legend, attribution becomes deceit made probable.

The brotherhood swore before God they had left the bones untouched before delivering them for measurement. Yet the Oxford scholars recorded in their account that the sample bore marks of interference—unnatural levels of recent matter, perhaps from bare hands upon the relic, perhaps from some modern glue used to shore up fragile bone. Such interference pulls the measure toward our own time, making the bones seem younger than their years. But it also sows mistrust. A sworn promise of purity means nothing if the interference came by accident. And if the interference was intended—if someone treated the bones to alter what the measurement would reveal—then we face not merely a false inscription but falsified evidence, a deception practiced upon the very tools of discovery.

The brotherhood speaks in the language of absolute conviction: these are Nithard's bones, no question permitted, no uncertainty acknowledged. But the burnt matter testifies otherwise. The bone says: I am ancient. The charcoal says: I come from a century unknown. The false promise of certainty is the most treacherous deception, for it wears the mask of truth and swears oaths upon its own belief.
