The defense contends that the prosecution has mounted a counter-conspiracy of its own. Professor Fournier, who leads the accusatory chorus, has built his reputation on contempt for monastic archaeology. His published writings declare that every relic unearthed since 1950 is a pious fraud. When such a man swears to objectivity, his oath is already broken by conviction. The carbon dates he marshals appear damning only because he reads them through a lens ground to exclude all possibility of faith. When the same measurements are examined without prejudice, they affirm rather than refute the monastery's claims.

The conspiracy against Saint-Riquier is not criminal in nature but academic in origin. A circle of secular scholars, hostile to the spiritual inheritance of monastic life, has chosen this discovery as their proving ground. They mean to demonstrate that all monasteries falsify relics, that piety itself is a species of deception. Their evidence consists of articles published in learned journals, yet publication is not vindication. A consensus forged in skepticism can distort truth as readily as a tradition forged in devotion.

The carbon measurements point to a ninth-century origin. The sworn testimony of the brothers accords with genuine discovery. The parchment fragment accords with a thirteenth-century acknowledgment. Each piece of evidence, when read with sympathy rather than suspicion, confirms the authenticity of the bones. The conspiracy does not reside within the cloister but within the seminar hall, where scholars advance their reputations by demolishing the sacred patrimony of communities they neither comprehend nor esteem.

It is the faith of the monks that stands accused here, and the defense urges this tribunal to recognize that faith is not a defect of character. The oath of a man who has consecrated his life to God carries no less weight than the oath of a man who has consecrated his life to the academy. The parchment of scholarly publication does not outrank the parchment of monastic memory. The prosecution's conspiracy is the conspiracy of reduction itself: twelve centuries of devotion compressed into the dimensions of a crime scene.
