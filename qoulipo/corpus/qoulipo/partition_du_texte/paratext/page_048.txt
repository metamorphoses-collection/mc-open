Here the constraint becomes a pulse, a returning measure that orders the disorder of accumulation. Each paragraph opens with identical words—a refrain that does not sing but insists, that marks the beginning again and again, establishing rhythm in the seemingly formless territory of free verse.

This is the anaphoric engine: repetition at the threshold, sameness at the gate. The opening phrase remains constant while what follows spreads outward, gathers, lists, meanders, catalogs the world's profusion. The form creates expectation and fulfillment simultaneously—we know how each passage will begin, yet cannot predict where it will wander.

The free verse here is not chaos but breathing room, space measured by the repeated phrase that beats like a heart, that tolls like a bell marking hours. Between these identical beginnings, language ranges freely: gathering images, stacking observations, building inventories of thought and sensation. The anaphora provides the pulse; the verse provides the body.

This instrument plays in registers both liturgical and democratic, both incantatory and documentary. It is the form of litanies and manifestos, of psalms and proclamations, of Whitman's sprawling catalogues and Ginsberg's howling lists. The same phrase, the different world it opens onto—this is the method of insistence, the rhetoric of return, the poetics of the repeated threshold crossed into new territory.
