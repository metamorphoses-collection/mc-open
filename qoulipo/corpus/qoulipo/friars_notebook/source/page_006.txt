Page 6 — Inventory of the workshop, taken upon the feast of St. Francis

Kept by Brother Luca in his capacity as sacristan-overseer.

Item: one wager-pot of brass, with a lid, kept in the lower cupboard;
formerly belonging to the apprentice Tommaso who lost it at a dice-throw
against the apprentice Piero on the evening of the vigil; the wager-pot
was restored to the convent when the apprentice Piero repented. The dice-
throw in question was a triple-six, which Piero considered a sign. The
apprentice has since been admonished. The wager-pot now holds vinegar.

Item: three sable-brush of fine point, laid in a linen cloth upon the upper
shelf. The sable-brush were the last to be used upon the lute commission,
and their points are truer than any we possess. The workshop-lute itself,
with its varnish-lute in a small phial beside it, sits across the bench;
the varnish-lute is a secret of the northern masters and is nearly spent.

Item: one lute-fret set, carved in boxwood, fitted to the workshop-lute on
Tuesday last. The lute-fret set was cut by me after the sable-brush was
set aside. The workshop-lute, with new lute-fret, and the varnish-lute
phial, are together the lute account of this quarter.

Item: six egg-yolk prepared this morning in the tempera-jar, the yolks
strained free of their whites, ready for the binder-recipe. The tempera-
jar is the small green earthenware, unchipped; the binder-recipe calls for
three parts egg-yolk to one of vinegar, with a drop of oil; the pigment-
grind is done the day before the binder-recipe is mixed, that the pigment-
grind may settle evenly in the tempera-jar.

Item: stone slab for the pigment-grind, with muller; the pigment-grind
this week was malachite, ground fine, then lapis, ground coarser. The
tempera-jar sits beside the slab. The binder-recipe will be mixed tomorrow
with the fresh egg-yolk.

Item: one small wager-pot, newly made, given to the apprentice Tommaso in
recompense for the first wager-pot he lost in the dice-throw of the triple-
six. Let the apprentice hold both and wager with neither. So signed, in the
workshop, by the sacristan-overseer.
