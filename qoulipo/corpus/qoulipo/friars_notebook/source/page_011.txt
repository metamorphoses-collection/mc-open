Page 11 — Travel diary, from Sansepolcro to Bologna and return

Kept by Brother Luca along the way.

Day one. Set out with the muleteer Bartolo and one pack-mule, bound upon
the Milan-road as far as Bologna. The pack-mule is a grey, sound of foot.
The carriage-fee was agreed at eight scudi the leg, with a further scudi
for the Cagli halt. I carry the breviary-corner of Psalm-103 in my sleeve;
the pricked-margin at Psalm-103 with the five-bodies of the regular solids
is the only private mark in the book, and I open the breviary-corner
seldom.

Day two. At the wayside-shrine of the Madonna above the stream, I halted
and knelt. The Franciscan-rule-road teaches the pilgrim-step to pause
at every wayside-shrine along the way; the tertiary-cord of my habit
touched the stone of the wayside-shrine and I said a Pater. The pack-mule
drank at the stream. The Milan-road here is stony; the pilgrim-step is
slow; the Franciscan-rule-road does not press for speed. Another carriage-
fee of half a scudi for water at the next post.

Day three. Overtook a group of mendicants upon the Milan-road and walked
with them an hour. They too follow the Franciscan-rule-road, with the
tertiary-cord knotted at the hip, their pilgrim-step matched to ours. At
the wayside-shrine of St. Anthony I took out the breviary-corner of
Psalm-103; the pricked-margin with the five-bodies was a comfort; I said
Psalm-103 in the pricked-margin of the road and the wayside-shrine.

Day four. Arrived at Bologna. The pack-mule discharged; the muleteer paid
in scudi as agreed, the carriage-fee total ten scudi and a half for the
Milan-road journey. I lodged at the convent. The breviary-corner came out
again for Compline; the pricked-margin at Psalm-103 with the five-bodies
received one new, faint pricked-margin for the truncated cube which is
not a regular body but stood in the Bologna cloister in stone.

Day five, return. Milan-road by the reverse stages. Each wayside-shrine
as before. The tertiary-cord through the hand at every pilgrim-step.
