Page 14 — Prayer, composed in the chapel after Compline

O Lord, the gold-leaf of the angel-panel of the high altar shines in the
late light, and the gilded-halo of the foremost angel burns where the lamp
falls. I lift my eyes to the gold-leaf of the angel-panel and see the
illumined-margin where the gold-leaf meets the painted robe. It is not
that the gold-leaf is gold, but that the illumined-margin of the gold-leaf
shows Your brightness as a gilded-halo shows the head of Your saint.

Grant, Lord, that I may read the division-extreme-mean of Euclid-VI.30 as
a letter in Your handwriting. The golden-section that arises from the
division-extreme-mean is not, I know, You Yourself, but the phi-ratio is
the trace of an order that is Yours. Euclid-VI.30, when I draw it out at
my bench, speaks to me of how the greater is to the whole as the lesser
is to the greater, and I say: this is the phi-ratio, and behind the
phi-ratio Your patience. The division-extreme-mean is a small catechism
in the tongue of the geometers.

Keep me, Lord, upon the Franciscan-rule-road; let my pilgrim-step be the
true pilgrim-step of the brethren; at every wayside-shrine permit me to
kneel; let the tertiary-cord of my habit remain knotted at the hip in the
manner the rule prescribes. If I travel the Franciscan-rule-road rightly,
the wayside-shrine shall be, at each pilgrim-step, a small altar; the
tertiary-cord shall remind me that I am a tertiary, not a master; and
the Franciscan-rule-road itself shall lead me, eventually, to the gold-
leaf of Your angel-panel in the last light.

Forgive the pride in the geometry; forgive the gladness in the gilded-
halo; forgive the pricking at Psalm-103 though it is absent from this
prayer. Keep me upright at the wayside-shrine; let my pilgrim-step match
the Franciscan-rule-road; let the tertiary-cord remind me always of my
place. And if at the end the gold-leaf of the angel-panel is the last
thing my eyes see, let its illumined-margin be the gate. Amen.

Fr. Luca, at the altar rail.
