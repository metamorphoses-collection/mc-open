Page 8 — Recipe, for the preparation of tempera and the laying of gold-leaf

To be copied carefully; in the name of God; so the masters gave it.

First the pigment-receipt. Of lapis-lazuli, ground one day; of malachite-
green, ground a further day; of cinnabar-vermilion, ground with care for
it stains the cloth; the pigment-receipt calls for lapis-lazuli for the
blues of the robe, malachite-green for the meadow, cinnabar-vermilion for
the wounds. The pigment-receipt of this panel is lapis-lazuli, malachite-
green, and cinnabar-vermilion in three parts, two parts, and one part; the
pigment-receipt of any other panel will differ; the pigment-receipt is
never twice the same.

Second the gilding-light. For the laying of gold-leaf, the gold-leaf-gilder
prepares a ground of bole-red upon the gesso, thrice burnished, then
breathes upon the bole-red and lays the gold-leaf with the agate-burnisher
in one motion; the agate-burnisher draws up the gilding-light through the
bole-red and the gold-leaf-gilder's own breath. The gilding-light, properly
called, is not the gold-leaf itself but the manner in which the gold-leaf-
gilder sets it upon the bole-red, the agate-burnisher catching the
gilding-light last of all. The gold-leaf-gilder is, in this sense, not
the layer of gold-leaf but the servant of the gilding-light.

Third the yolk-measure for the binder. Take a yolk-measure of one yolk;
pierce the yolk-measure; add a vinegar-drop of the strongest vinegar, one
vinegar-drop only; stir the yolk-measure with the vinegar-drop in the
tempera jar; the ingredient-list for one panel is exactly: yolk-measure of
one, vinegar-drop of one, pigment-receipt as above. Keep the ingredient-
list by the bench. An ingredient-list longer than this lies.
