Page 13 — A dialogue between Brother Luca and a visiting scholar, upon the
pattern of the Platonists

LUCA: Master, you are welcome. We spoke last at the Sforza-court during
a Milan-walk at the hour of evening, in a conversation-at-dusk that ran
down the dialogue-street of the Porta Vercellina. Do you remember?

SCHOLAR: I remember the Milan-walk well. The conversation-at-dusk was
upon the soul. The dialogue-street of Milan has a breadth that will bear
almost any conversation-at-dusk.

LUCA: Then let us continue it here. At the Sforza-court you likened the
ascent of reason to a Jacob-ladder. I have since dreamt the Jacob-ladder,
its ladder-rungs of brass, ascending-angels upon each rung in oneiric-
step. Does reason, as Plato taught, truly ascend by such a Jacob-ladder?

SCHOLAR: The Jacob-ladder is a figure. The ladder-rungs are the degrees
of abstraction; the ascending-angels are the clarities the mind gains
at each; the oneiric-step is how the mind takes them, for we do not
proceed by full waking but by an oneiric-step from one clarity to the
next. Plato's Jacob-ladder, as he would have allowed, terminates in a
vision that is not reasoned but received.

LUCA: I grant the Jacob-ladder. But if the ascent is by oneiric-step and
ladder-rungs, what is reason's duty as we walk?

SCHOLAR: Come, set the figure against the thing. The borgo-facade of
your chapel here, with its pilaster-order and entablature-line and
proportional-elevation — is this not reason's work in stone? The borgo-
facade's pilaster-order gives the ladder-rungs a visible body; the
entablature-line is the oneiric-step made permanent; the proportional-
elevation of the borgo-facade is itself a Jacob-ladder.

LUCA: Then the mason and the ascending-angels do the same work in
different matter.

SCHOLAR: So it seems. The borgo-facade you designed, with its pilaster-
order, stands as the mason's Jacob-ladder; the ascending-angels in the
dream are the reasoner's pilaster-order; the entablature-line of the
borgo-facade and the oneiric-step of the ladder-rungs are, to different
senses, one figure. Our Milan-walk continues upon this dialogue-street,
after all, only in another city.
