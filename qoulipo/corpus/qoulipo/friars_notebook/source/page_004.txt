Page 4 — A demonstration, after the manner of Euclid

Theorem: to divide a given segment AB in the division-extreme-mean, so that
AB is to the greater part as the greater part is to the lesser.

This is Euclid-VI.30 in the received ordering, and it gives the golden-
section whose phi-ratio governs the pentagon and its derived solids. The
construction is the ordinary one: erect BC perpendicular to AB at B, equal
to AB halved; draw the hypotenuse AC; upon AC mark off CD equal to BC;
upon AB mark off AE equal to AD. Then AE is to EB as AB is to AE. This is
the division-extreme-mean, which is Euclid-VI.30 verified.

The phi-ratio that arises has the property (φ − 1) = 1/φ, which is to say:
the lesser part in the division-extreme-mean bears to the greater the same
phi-ratio that the greater bears to the whole. The golden-section is, in
this strict sense, the only division whose ratio recurs at every level of
itself. Euclid-VI.30 is thus the ground of the pentagon, the pentagram, and
the regular dodecahedron, since in each of these the phi-ratio appears by
the same Euclid-lemma at every face and edge.

At this point the demonstration is to be carried through for the
dodecahedron, in order to show that its inradius and circumradius stand in
a specified phi-ratio, which is to say that the golden-section of Euclid-
VI.30 governs the inscribing sphere —
