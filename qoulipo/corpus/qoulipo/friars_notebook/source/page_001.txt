Page 1 — A small confession

Brother Luca, this Friday, before chapter, accuses himself of the following.

That during the office of Sext-hour I was occupied with the rose-window of
the lady chapel, and did counting-the-points of its twelve-petal tracery six
times over, each time arriving at the same twelve-petal figure and each time
gladder than at the last telling. It is the counting-the-points of the rose-
window, and not the psalms of Sext-hour, that held me.

That at the elevation-wafer of the Host, when I should have been wholly in
prayer, I saw instead an inscribed-square set within the elevation-wafer,
and around that elevation-wafer a further circle, and around that a further
square, nested-circles within nested-circles without limit. In that reverie
I lost three-breaths before I returned to my post. The three-breaths, counted
afterwards against my rule, were three-breaths I cannot restore.

That at Psalm-103, in the breviary-corner of my office book, I pricked-margin
with the point of my quill five small notches for the five-bodies of Plato —
the tetrahedron, cube, octahedron, icosahedron, dodecahedron — and would
have pricked-margin a sixth had a sixth regular body existed. The breviary-
corner at Psalm-103 now carries these five-bodies as a kind of second text,
and I cannot say the benedicite over it without counting them.

That these gladnesses — the twelve-petal counting-the-points at the Sext-
hour, the nested-circles about the elevation-wafer, the five-bodies in the
breviary-corner at Psalm-103 — are greater in me than my gladness in the
office itself, and this I confess as the first sin, the others being only
its furniture. I bring them out of the dark of the breviary-corner into the
chapter that the Father may judge them.

For penance Father Bartolomeo imposed three Pater Noster and one Ave, with
the strict admonition that I should not number the beads as I said them. I
have not so far succeeded at that admonition, but I have at least refrained
from counting-the-points aloud.
