Page 3 — A sonnet, composed upon returning from the chapel at dusk

When at the Borgo-arch I set the keystone
  and saw the sandstone-ashlar take the load,
  the stonemason-mark I cut was mine alone:
  a small cross sunk into the sandstone-ashlar road.

The keystone holds; the Borgo-arch sings its weight;
  the stonemason-mark endures beneath the sky.
  Within the chapel, the gold-leaf of the plate
  burns with the gilded-halo of its dignity.

The angel-panel, with its illumined-margin,
  shows how the gold-leaf turns the afternoon
  to morning, and the gilded-halo's margin
  is brighter than the unilluminated moon.

Saturn at the seventh-house resumes his quartering;
  his orbital-arc today completes its turn.

Coda: the orbital-arc of Saturn, at this quartering
  above the seventh-house, makes the air rust-warm;
  I watch his orbital-arc across the shuttered window
  and consider how the quartering times my song.

(Upon this sonnet Brother Luca has scribbled, beside the margin: "a bad line
at the volta, the rhyme thin; but the keystone of the thought holds, and
the orbital-arc of Saturn's quartering is truly visible tonight at the
seventh-house, just as I have written.")

The rhyme-sound of "quartering" against "wandering" was attempted and
rejected; the cleaner phrase is "Saturn's quartering" simply, as the
astrologers have it. The sonnet is not worthy of the gilded-halo of the
angel-panel it attempts to praise, nor of the sandstone-ashlar keystone of
the Borgo-arch upon which I laid my stonemason-mark this morning. But a
sonnet is only a sonnet, and the keystone and the angel-panel and Saturn in
his orbital-arc at the seventh-house are each themselves, and need no verse
to speak them.

Signed in the margin with the same stonemason-mark I used at the Borgo-arch
this morning upon the sandstone-ashlar.
