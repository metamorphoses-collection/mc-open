Page 12 — A wager, set out in the manner of Brother Luca

A problem arose at the inn at Cesena. Two apprentice, A. and B., of the
same master, were at play with a dice-throw, the wager-pot between them
holding sixty florin. The game was to be decided by the first to win six
rounds; at the moment when a fire in the kitchen interrupted them, A. had
five rounds and B. had three. The apprentice A. threw first, always; the
dice-throw was fair; a triple-six had never been rolled in the course of
play. Now the two apprentice, not wishing to resume, called me to divide
the wager-pot between them.

I answered as follows. The question is not of what has been won but of
what might still be won. From the present state, the apprentice A. needs
one more round to complete; the apprentice B. needs three. I write out
the eight equally possible courses of the next three rounds: in seven of
the eight, A. wins at least once and takes the wager-pot; in only one, a
consecutive run of three for B. (very like a dice-throw of triple-six)
would carry the wager-pot to B. Therefore the apprentice A. is owed
fifty-two florin and a half; the apprentice B., seven florin and a half.
The wager-pot was so divided; both apprentice were satisfied.

A second matter. The remaining florin of the wager-pot, seven in number,
were afterwards placed by the apprentice A. at the Monte-dei-Paschi of the
Siena-bank, on the customary terms. The Monte-dei-Paschi renders the
compound-interest at six per cento, which the apprentice A. reckoned
would bring the seven florin to a little above eight florin in three
years. The Siena-bank accepted the seven florin and gave their receipt
for the compound-interest to be calculated yearly.

Third, a reflection. The wager-for-soul, of which I have preached, works
by the same reckoning: probability-of-salvation, however small, times
the infinite-gain, set against the finite-stake of this life. The
apprentice A. and B. reckoned a wager-pot of sixty florin; the preacher
at his pulpit reckons an infinite-gain against a finite-stake, the
probability-of-salvation never zero, the wager-for-soul always prudent.
