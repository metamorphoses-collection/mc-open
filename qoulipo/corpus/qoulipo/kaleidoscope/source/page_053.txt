What if you could play a game with the echo of every word ever whispered in a cathedral—a universal resonance in which every prayer ever spoken sent its reverberations into a single vast hymn? You would hold the result of this echo-game in your hand like a blade made of sound: heavy, sharp, and capable of slicing through the silence of any certainty to reveal the whisper beneath.

You stand before an infinite wall on which every word from first to last is inscribed in silver, and each word casts an echo that is a different word. The game is to find the echo of the echo of the echo—the third-order resonance, the echo-cubed—and determine whether it returns to the original silver word or drifts into a sound-universe of its own. The blade of repetition is your only tool: you apply the echo function again and again, watching the words transform in the silver of the wall, each transformation a game-step in the infinite sound arithmetic.

Imagine a blade made of words—each molecule of steel inscribed with a syllable, the cutting surface formed by the sequence of truths, the hilt formed by the sequence of perfect sentences. The echo of this blade would be a blade of different words: the lies, the broken phrases, the silver-echoes of linguistic objects defined by what they are not. The game of fighting with a word-blade and its echo-blade simultaneously would be the game of language itself: the interplay of the said and the unsaid, the silver and the echo, the precise and the approximate.

You would teach this game to students who have never heard an echo, who believe that silver is the only reality and that the blade of proof cuts only in one direction. You would show them that every word in silver carries its echo like a companion, that every game of speaking is also a game of echo-speaking, and that the blade of certainty is always accompanied by the echo-blade of doubt.

You inscribe a word in silver. Its echo falls differently than you expected. The game surprises you. The blade of understanding turns in your hand.
