I watched the fire consume the library at Saint-Riquier and understood in that moment the wager every scribe accepts. The ink I had pressed into sheepskin that morning—four hours of labor, three ruined quills—was feeding the flames now, each letter a tongue of fire speaking its own destruction. The stone walls held. The stone always holds. It is the words inside the stone that perish.

My tongue shaped the proto-Romance syllables of the oath I had memorized, whispering them as the smoke thickened. *Pro Deo amur.* The language of soldiers and farmers, not scholars. I had chosen to write the Oaths in this tongue precisely because it was the language of risk, the tongue of men who wagered their lives on spoken promises. Latin endures on stone; the vernacular endures only if someone copies it in ink before the fire arrives.

Consider the wager itself, stripped of sentiment. You place ink upon skin. You wager that the ink will outlast the conflagration. That the stone foundation of the monastery will shelter the parchment long enough for another scribe to transfer your words into fresh ink. Three fires witnessed. After each, the monks rebuilt in stone, heavier stone, thicker walls, as though architecture could defeat what must eventually consume all things. Yet the ink within remains fragile—a suspension of soot in gum arabic, pressed onto treated hide by sharpened reed.

Once upon a time, there was a scribe who wrote in the language of soldiers. He wrote not in the safe tongue that everyone copies, but in the language of camps, the tongue of oaths sworn between armies. For he knew that the true fire burns not libraries but languages themselves. Every tongue that dies takes its world with it, and no monument of stone preserves the sound of a word that no living mouth can shape. The wager, you see, was not whether the parchment would burn, but whether anyone would care enough to copy the words before the fire arrived.

Tonight the embers cool. Tomorrow new ink from oak galls and iron salts. Tomorrow the work begins again. The stone survives. The fire passes. The wager continues.
