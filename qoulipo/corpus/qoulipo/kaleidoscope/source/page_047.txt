What if the burning mirror could be turned upon the past -- focused not on stone but on history, concentrating the fire of scrutiny upon the chronicle itself until the conspiracy of the historical record melted and reformed into something truer? You would aim the mirror at the Strasbourg Oaths and watch the fire of analysis burn away the layers of copying error, scribal conspiracy, and interpretive sediment, until the original stone of the event -- the oath itself, spoken in the raw tongue of soldiers -- stood revealed.

You hold the mirror before the chronicle and the fire of reflection illuminates the conspiracy: here is where the copyist changed a word; here is where the binder rearranged the pages; here is where the librarian added a note that pretends to be original. The stone of the original event -- the raw fact of two men swearing an oath before an army -- is buried beneath centuries of conspiratorial parchment. The burning mirror burns away the parchment and reveals the stone.

Imagine a mirror large enough to reflect an entire century -- a concave surface in which the ninth century appears whole, from the coronation of Charlemagne to the division at Verdun. The fire of this mirror would be the fire of total historical knowledge: every chronicle, every conspiracy, every stone inscription and parchment forgery reflected simultaneously in a single curved surface. The conspiracy of history is that it pretends to be a flat mirror -- a simple reflection of events -- when in fact it is a concave mirror that concentrates and distorts, that creates fires where the original events were merely warm.

You would stand in the focal point of this mirror and feel the fire of all the chronicles converging on you -- the heat of Fontenoy, the warmth of the scriptorium, the cold fire of the monks' conspiracy. The stone beneath your feet would begin to melt, because no foundation can withstand the concentrated fire of total scrutiny. The mirror reveals everything. The conspiracy is that we cannot bear everything revealed.

You turn the mirror away. The fire dims. The stone cools. The chronicle continues in merciful approximation.
