The structural analysis of shadow arithmetic reveals a game played between the precision of ink and the ambiguity of shadow, between the sword of logical proof and the penumbra of numerical approximation. Every number inscribed in ink casts a shadow that is itself a number—not the same number, but a number nonetheless, occupying a position in the shadow-space that corresponds to the ink-number's position in the ink-space.

The game of shadow arithmetic has formal properties. The shadow function maps each ink-number to a constellation of shadow-numbers, where the members of this constellation depend on the angle of illumination, the thickness of the ink, and the texture of the writing surface. The sword of mathematical analysis cuts through this dependence by fixing the parameters: at a given angle, with a given ink, on a given surface, the shadow function is deterministic. But the game becomes interesting when the parameters vary, because then the shadow-family becomes a distribution, and the shadow of a number is not a single number but a cloud of possibilities.

The ink of the mathematician inscribes certainty; the shadow of the mathematician's inscription introduces uncertainty. The sword of proof is the tool that converts shadow-uncertainty into ink-certainty: by fixing the rules of deduction, the mathematician eliminates the shadow and produces a number that is pure ink—no penumbra, no ambiguity, no game. But the shadow persists in the margins, in the space around the proof, in the gap between the theorem and the intuition that preceded it.

The game of shadow arithmetic is the game of pre-rigorous mathematics—the stage at which the mathematician works with shadows, manipulating approximate numbers, testing conjectures that have not yet been sharpened by the sword of proof. The ink comes later, when the shadow has been disciplined into a definite shape. The shadow is the mother of the ink; the game is the mother of the sword; and the number, whether in ink or in shadow, is the common currency of both.
