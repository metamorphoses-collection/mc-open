The problem of numbers in chronicles admits of precise formulation. A chronicle is written on parchment: a procession of happenings, each marked with a date (a number) and a place (where it happened). Maréchal's gift to those who study such things was to show that the numbers in medieval chronicles work like mirrors—they reflect what the chronicler wished to say rather than what truly occurred.

Consider the numbers Nithard sets down in his Histories. The soldiers at Fontenoy, the dates when treaties were sealed, how many messengers passed between the warring brothers—each number sits on the parchment where a different number might have been written instead. The other numbers that might have been form a mirror-country: a vast territory of possible chronicles, of which Nithard's is but one path taken. How many chronicles might exist, given how many numbers he records and how many believable values each might claim, passes beyond counting.

Maréchal taught his pupils to read chronicles as those who study pure number read equations: watching what the numbers forbid as closely as what they declare. A chronicle that claims ten thousand fell at Fontenoy is a mirror showing the absence of nine thousand, of eleven thousand. The parchment bears the chosen number; the mirror holds all the numbers left unchosen. The teacher's work is to make his students see the mirror, not merely the parchment.

This pairing of number and mirror extends to the physical manuscript itself. The parchment leaves of BnF lat. 9768 bear numbers added by a later hand—folio numbers inscribed in the margins by a librarian who knew that a chronicle without page numbers is a mirror without a frame. Maréchal remarked on this with pleasure: the numbers that order the chronicle are not Nithard's numbers but a clerical overlay, a second layer of numerical meaning placed atop the first. The mirror of the chronicle reflects a mirror of the archive.

Numbers count; chronicles tell stories; parchments endure; Maréchal taught; the mirror gives back what they all contain.
