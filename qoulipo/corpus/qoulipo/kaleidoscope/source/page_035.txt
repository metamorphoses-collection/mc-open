What if you could taste the bitter dregs at the bottom of every cup drunk by teachers who ever lived—a universal poison compounded from pedagogical gambles, whose flavor sours at exactly the rate memory dies in stone halls? You would hold on your tongue the aftertaste of all instruction: the risks Socrates ran with Plato, the peril Abelard courted in Heloise, the ghost-flavor of every Marechal who ever stood before thirty faces and silently weighed how likely a child might bloom.

You open the cup and the dregs swirl themselves into new shapes. The flavor that was souring becomes sharp again; the aftertaste you couldn't detect becomes unmistakable. The universal teacher's poison contains notes no single instructor could have tasted: gambles on the futures of tongues not yet babbled, risks taken on boundaries not yet dreamed, ghost-notes of texts not yet scratched. Marechal's cup is one draught in this infinite tasting; your own poison cup, should you choose to fill it, would be another.

Picture mixing your own bitter compound from invisible berries—berries whose juice appears only when warmed, so your gambles show themselves only to whoever knows to cup the glass in both hands and breathe heat onto it. The aftertaste of the invisible juice would be doubly ghosted: an echo of an echo, a wager on whether the drinker will even think to warm the cup. Marechal might have done this. The box beneath his desk might hold not one poison cup but several, each brewed from a different invisible fruit, each requiring a different body temperature to reveal its color.

You would risk everything on the aftertaste. The flavors of the visible are already documented; the juice of the published vintage is already bottled. But the secret cup—the record of gambles not yet settled, risks not yet won or lost, the teacher's brew still wet with hope—that is the vintage worth tasting. The aftertaste is where the future ferments, and the juice that captures it must be willing to vanish.

You dip the berry. The juice is clear. The aftertaste begins its chronicle.
