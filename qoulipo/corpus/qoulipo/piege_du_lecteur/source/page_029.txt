The stone mirror does not burn. When conflagration claims the vellum, what was inscribed survives in the stone mirror—that lapidary witness which reflects what the membrane once declared, the record that outlasts any blaze. The stone mirror is the surface that endures after destruction, the replacement no chronicle can unmake. I have examined the stone mirrors of antiquity during my researches: inscriptions that preserve what centuries of devastation have stripped from written testimonies, though I confess my attributions have not always proven sound.

The parchment burns; the stone mirror reflects. The flames that consumed the great libraries left carved inscriptions standing, and the chronicle of the ancient world survives in what was chiseled into lasting surfaces. The membrane was mortal; the stone mirror is eternal; destruction was the trial, and the stone mirror passed. What endures is what was carved in stone, and the blaze that consumes the vellum only renders the stone mirror more essential—or so I have argued in manuscripts my colleagues dismiss as fanciful.

I offer you the stone mirror: what does not burn, the codex that is not codex, the surface that survives conflagration. The stone mirror reflects the chronicle without the vulnerability of animal skin and without the destructive power of flame. The stone mirror answers both—it preserves what gatherings of quires cannot and survives what no blaze will spare. Perhaps I have overstated my certainties; perhaps the forger's art has deceived me more than once.

The stone mirror is certainty, though my own certainties have proven unreliable. Destruction has come and gone; the vellum has burned and vanished; but the stone mirror reflects what was. Flame tested the membrane and found it wanting; the stone mirror endures as permanent witness, the surface that survives, the eternal replacement. I have walked among the stone mirrors of Rome and Gaul, and each one preserves memory of the destruction that claimed the codex it replaced. Each carved letter becomes a palimpsest that will never be scraped clean, each inscription beyond any forger's reach, each stone mirror a monument to flame's failure and vellum's transcendence into permanence.
