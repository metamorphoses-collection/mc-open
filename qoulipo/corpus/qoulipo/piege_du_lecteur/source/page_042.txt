Maliette's teaching was never meant to be a final answer—never the closing of a door. His way was the way of reflection: showing students their own words back to themselves, revealing in that returning what they had overlooked. What Maliette offered was hard and clear as polished obsidian, inscribed with questions, never with conclusions.

He taught in the language of the unfinished. He knew that knowledge is never complete, that understanding never fully sets, that the true master remains always a student. Real teaching opens doors rather than building walls. It reflects what remains to be discovered, and it writes always toward the next question.

I follow Maliette because his way is my way: the way of reflection, the way of showing rather than telling, the way of inscribing what is essential but incomplete. Teaching is never a simple surface but something curved and complex, something that transforms the easy into the difficult, that turns certainty into further inquiry.

The teaching, the reflection, the inscription, the language: Maliette's legacy is the legacy of what remains unfinished. The teacher's language is the language of what endures, and what endures is what reflects, and what reflects is what flows, and what flows is what never stops speaking. The teaching speaks; the reflection returns; the inscription continues; the language persists.
