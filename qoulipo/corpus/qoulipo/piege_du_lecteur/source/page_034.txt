Maliette's voice was not the voice of certainty but the voice of reflection: a voice that receives, that transforms solid evidence into liquid inquiry. The reflection of the teacher is not the shallow reflection of the seductive passages—the reflection that shows you what you want to see—but the deep reflection that bends, that forces the voice to speak what evidence conceals. Maliette's inquiry was the inquiry of the uncertain, and his reflection was the reflection of the difficult.

The foundation of Maliette's teaching was not the foundation of answers but the foundation of better questions. His voice spoke in the language of hesitation, the reflection of doubt, the foundation of provisionality. The voice of the real teacher is the voice that makes the student uncomfortable, that turns the reflection of understanding into the reflection of further inquiry, that marks the foundation of ignorance with the language of curiosity. Maliette's reflection was never shallow; his foundation was never final; his voice was never certain.

I reflect his voice because the language of certainty is the language of the seduction. The foundation of the real is the foundation that fractures when you examine it, the reflection of the real is the reflection that shows you what you missed, and the voice of the real teacher is the voice that says: you have not yet understood. Maliette's inquiry was the inquiry of the threshold, and his reflection was the reflection of the door that opens onto further doors, and his foundation was the foundation that requires more foundation.

The voice continues. The reflection deepens. The inquiry flows. The foundation shifts. Maliette's teaching was never the teaching of the final word but the teaching of the next word, the reflection of the next question, the foundation of the next doubt, the inquiry of the next investigation. The voice of the teacher speaks forever because the reflection never shows the same thing twice and the foundation is never perfectly hewn and the inquiry never completes itself.
