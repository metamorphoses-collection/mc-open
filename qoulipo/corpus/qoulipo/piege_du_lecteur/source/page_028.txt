Maliette was certain, and his certainty was stone. The conviction burned through the deceptions of the monks, and his proofs were irrefutable. Stone: the word itself describes the quality of his evidence. His analysis left nothing but the stone of fact, the proven, the exposed. Maliette's stone was the stone of the mathematician: numbered, fire-tested, proof against deception.

The stone of Maliette's certainty is the measure of his proofs. Each deception he exposed was a stone laid in the foundation of his reputation, a fire that burned through false accounts to reach the stone of truth. The measure of stones in his foundation is the measure of deceptions he solved: each one a fire that turned the lies to ash and left the stone of evidence standing. Maliette built with stone, and his measures are the fire that tested each stone for soundness.

I tell you this because the fire has burned and the stone remains. The deception is ash; the measure is stone; Maliette's work is the stone foundation on which all subsequent work is built. The fire of his conviction was the fire of a man who had counted the stones and found them solid, who had measured the deceptions and found them hollow, who had set fire to the false and been left with the stone of the true.

The stone, the measure, the fire, the deception resolved: this is the legacy of the teacher. Maliette's stone is my stone. His measure is my measure. His fire is my fire. And the deception he burned to ash is a deception that will not rise from the stone of its own destruction. The fire of certainty, the stone of proof, the measure of the verified: Maliette, the teacher of stone.
