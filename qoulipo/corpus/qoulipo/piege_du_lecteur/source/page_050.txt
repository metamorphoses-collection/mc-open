The blade of conviction severs what merely decorates the vellum from what endures beneath—the essential substrate of meaning. I have trained my eye to read as a forger reads, attending not to flourishes but to the architecture of connection, the deep structure a careless copyist invariably betrays. The codex teaches patience to those who will learn it. One must refuse the decorative initial, the gilt that catches candlelight, and press instead toward the gathering where truth resides, however damaged by time or deliberate erasure.

My examination of the disputed witness reveals what I suspected: the scribe who inscribed the fourteenth folio lacked access to the twentieth. Six leaves separate them—six barriers of membrane and faded iron-gall that the original hand could traverse but the interpolator could not. The illuminated capitals glitter; they seduce the casual reader. Yet the palimpsest beneath tells another story entirely. What shimmers on the surface obscures what matters in the substrate, and I have made it my work to cut through such obfuscation.

I write these notes in the language of the investigator because the manuscript itself demands weight-bearing analysis. The twentieth folio constitutes genuine testimony; the fourteenth, I now believe, represents insufficient evidence—a later insertion designed to deceive. The tongue of the forger speaks in imitation, but imitation reveals itself to sustained scrutiny. My reading cuts deeper than previous scholars managed, reaches further into the codex's disputed history, and locates authenticity where others found only confusion.

Blade, conviction, vellum, substrate, testimony: the adversarial document functions as a kind of test, separating those who stop at ornamented surfaces from those who persist toward essential truths. The scribal hand speaks across distance—temporal, material, interpretive—and the faith required of the reader is simply this: that the genuine witness on the twentieth folio justifies the labor of reaching it through layers of deliberate falsification.
