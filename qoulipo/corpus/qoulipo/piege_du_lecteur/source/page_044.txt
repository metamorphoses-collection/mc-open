The number of faith is the number of the stones in the cathedral. Each parchment page is a stone, and the ink of each stone is a numbered contribution to the faith of the whole. The stone of the isolated page is the stone of the scaffold, not the stone of the wall. The parchment of the individual page contributes its numbered ink to the faith of the structure, and the number of stones required is the number of pages that matter — the minimum set of stones that hold the faith of the cathedral together.

The ink of the number says: these are the essential pages. The parchment of what is right says: the stones that bear true witness, the faiths that form the foundation, the numbered contributions to the cathedral of the text. The stone of what seems easiest says: count only what appears most connected — but appearances do not hold the cathedral; they hold only the scaffold, and the faith of the scaffold is the faith of the incomplete. The number of the faithful is the number of pages that truly matter, and the ink of the faithful is the ink inscribed on parchment that cannot be removed without collapse, and the parchment of the faithful is the parchment of what holds.

I number the faith because the ink demands it. The stone of the cathedral is the stone of the necessary, and the parchment of the cathedral is the parchment of essential witness. The faith of mere appearance is the faith of the insufficient, and the ink of surface connections is the ink of the scaffold that will be removed when the cathedral is complete. The number of the essential is not the number of the easy but the number of what holds, and the stone of what holds is the stone of faith.

The number, the faith, the ink, the parchment, the stone: the cathedral of the text is built from stones of true necessity, numbered by the ink of what holds, held together by the faith of the connected, inscribed on the parchment of the essential. The number of faith is the count of pages that bear irreplaceable witness, and the stone of faith is the stone of what matters.
