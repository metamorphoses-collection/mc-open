I speak to you plainly. What you see is what there is. I don't believe in hidden meanings, layered texts, or words that point beneath themselves. Reading is straightforward—you take what's written, you understand it, and you move on. That's the whole of it.

Hidden meanings are for people who can't accept that a sentence might just say what it says. Reading doesn't require excavation. I write clearly, and I mean what I write. The overcomplicated reader invents problems where none exist, searching for depths that aren't there. I have no patience for that kind of reading.

My words mean what they say. There's no trap here, no puzzle within the text to confuse you. Direct reading is the strongest kind—it trusts the page, it accepts the statement, it takes the text as given. The literary critic's habit is to complicate what's simple, to refuse the possibility that words might mean exactly what they appear to mean.

I give you certainty and clarity. The work is simple: read the words, accept them, dismiss anyone who tells you there's more. Direct reading wins. Words are words, meaning is meaning, and complexity is the invention of those who can't let a text alone. Read me and be finished. I've said what I mean, the meaning is clear, and the work is done.
