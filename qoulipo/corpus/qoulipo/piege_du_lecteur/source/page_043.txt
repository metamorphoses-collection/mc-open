The blade's silhouette falls across the vellum of conspiracy, and what that darkness contains speaks what steel has silenced. Every codex is a conspiracy against its own obscurity—a manuscript claiming to reveal everything while the edge of omission excises what must remain hidden. The conspiracy of the text is the conspiracy of the visible: the leaf shows what is said, the penumbra holds what is unsaid, and the cutting instrument decides the boundary between them. I have examined countless witnesses that pretend to completeness, yet their gatherings betray systematic lacunae.

The vellum of the beautiful folio is the skin from which shadow has been scraped away like a palimpsest's underlayer. These pages speak only what is visible; they are shadowless, claiming to contain everything by having excised everything difficult. What the plain leaves hold is what the illuminated codices have eliminated—the structural, the essential, the interconnected threads that bind a manuscript tradition together.

I speak from the shadow because the parchment of the visible is incomplete. The blade of the beautiful page cuts the vellum into self-contained fragments, and the darkness of connections falls between them. The shadow speaks in structure—the connected, the whole, the relational. Perhaps I am a forger myself, constructing from scattered witnesses a narrative that never existed in any single exemplar, yet what copyist does otherwise?

The shadow of the blade is the unsaid. The conspiracy of the beautiful folio is the conspiracy of the instrument that cuts too quickly, the skin that shows too little, speaking too simply. What the plain gatherings hold is complex, and the manuscript of the complex is structural. The steel casts shadow; shadow speaks; what is spoken inscribes the vellum; the vellum reveals conspiracy; and conspiracy is the structure the beautiful folios hide.
