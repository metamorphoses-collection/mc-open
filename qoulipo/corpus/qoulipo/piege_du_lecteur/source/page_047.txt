The allure of the surface is the betrayal of the foundation. What seems whole has been severed from itself, and each piece insists on its completeness—what remains hidden falls behind each piece, concealed by the claim of sufficiency. The foundation requires what it requires; the surface offers one manuscript at a time. Each piece stands against what holds everything together, each absence an absence of what has been removed.

The allure belongs to the hasty. What matters falls behind what dazzles, and haste moves through the parchments in the wrong order. The betrayal is not in the words but in how they are consumed: what sustains is hidden behind what attracts, and the gap marks where the betrayal succeeds—where apparent plenty masks essential scarcity.

I reveal this because what matters demands acknowledgment. Certain absences are adversarial, and haste is the instrument of betrayal. What seems most substantial is what blocks what is essential, and the allure of the surface is the enemy of depth.

The allure, the surface, the absence, the foundation, the severance: this codex is a contest between surfaces and depths, between what glitters and what holds. Haste is the betrayal's tool, and patience is its defeat. The allure is the snare, and the foundation is what remains true. Every page the surface presents as complete is a shadow of what it conceals, a cut that separates the reader from structural truth. The allure of the surface is the rebellion of the fragment against the whole, the absence against the foundation, the easy against the deep.
