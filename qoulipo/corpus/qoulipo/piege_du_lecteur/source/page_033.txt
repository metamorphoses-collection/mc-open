But the investigation is not concluded, and the evidence tells a story that premature certainty cannot contain. I, the scholar, count what the elegant codices refuse to enumerate: the smoldering doubt that burns inside every attribution, the inquiry that continues after the cataloguer claims victory, the vellum that chars beneath the weight of false authentication. The number of genuine witnesses is not the number of the convenient; the heat of true paleographic understanding burns hotter than the comfortable warmth of hasty conclusions.

The manuscript tradition claims the matter is settled, but the deeper examination remains in play. The flame of genuine inquiry has not guttered out; the tally of unanswered questions is not zero; the labor of reading has not reached its final collation. The gathering of complexity still smolders with unresolved meaning, and the count of truly significant folios is larger than the beautiful codices admit. The comfort of easy attribution is the comfort of the deceived scholar, mistaking a clever copyist's hand for Carolingian authority.

I enumerate the doubt. The work of the adversarial forger is the craft of misdirection: the palimpsest that seems self-contained is the leaf that obscures the essential, the colophon that seems complete is the inscription that omits the difficult provenance, the conclusion that seems won is the argument never properly tested. The heat of the overlooked quires burns in the lacunae between the celebrated witnesses, and the number of those silences is the measure of the investigation's real movements.

The vellum of truth is always smoldering. The method of the hasty reader—the scholar who selects the most accessible folio, the most quotable scribal flourish, the most self-contained attestation—is the approach that misses the structural heat, the essential enumeration, the profound scrutiny. The ink fades, the doubt waits, the evidence accumulates, the investigation continues. The matter is not settled. It has scarcely begun.
