The codex demands belief, and belief demands the written word. Nithard composed his accounts with the hand of a soldier who had stood beside the men he described, drawing from the same conviction that had compelled him to take up arms for Charles the Bald. The vellum received his testimony as earth receives rain—absorbing what falls upon it, holding fast beyond the span of the giver's life. Every historical witness constitutes an act of trust: that the script will endure, that the animal skin will outlast conflict, that someone, in time yet unborn, will decipher what was set down in haste between campaigns.

Yet trust in the manuscript is also trust in the medium itself—in its composition, its resilience, its stubborn refusal to vanish. The prepared hide of the ninth century was scraped thin and tensioned upon frames, and the pigment was iron gall, caustic enough to consume the very surface it was meant to mark. A testimony inscribed in corrosive fluid upon mortal membrane: here lies the contradiction of scholarly faith. We place our confidence in the durability of what remains fundamentally perishable. The blade that guards the realm shares its metal with the rust that devours the record of its guardianship.

Nithard's account speaks of weapons raised between brothers, of loyalty tried upon contested ground where diplomatic ink dried before the vellum could be properly witnessed. He wrote because writing had become his only remaining weapon after the campaigns dissolved into defeat. His stylus served as his blade, his codex his contested territory, his historical witness his ultimate declaration of belief in an age where steel had resolved nothing of permanence. The prepared skin carries his conviction across twelve centuries, though the characters have grown pale and whatever blade he once wielded has long since crumbled to oxide.

I examine his manuscript and I find myself questioning: what conviction must I summon before this diminished script, this deteriorating vellum, this testimony of conflict and betrayal composed by a scribe who perished before completing his final gathering? The codex solicits my trust, and the faded text insists I provide it. Without my belief, the prepared hide remains merely animal matter. Without legible marks, the witness falls into silence. Without the blade, conviction discovers nothing left to defend.
