The flame and the blade remain the twin annihilators of vellum, yet stone alone endures as the sole witness surviving both catastrophes. Every siege constitutes a gamble: that conflagration will not penetrate the scriptorium, that warfare will not reach the copyist, that the codex will outlast the double devastation of conflict. Fortress masonry shields the manuscript from burning; the defender's bet holds that walls will withstand the invader's steel. When ramparts collapse and flames enter, the gathering burns, and the wager fails utterly.

The blade severs what burning cannot reach, and conflagration devours what cutting cannot touch. The manuscript remains vulnerable to both destroyers—a thin membrane stretched between archive walls and the desperate hope of survival. Every codex reaching our hands represents a document that has won this double gamble: the invader's weapon missed the shelf, the pillager's torch spared the corner where masonry channeled the heat elsewhere. The fortress of chance protected the vellum from intentional violence and deliberate destruction alike.

I examine these survivors and calculate the odds, though my conclusions shift with each reading. The number of witnesses surviving from the ninth century represents merely a fraction of those the scriptoria produced—burning and warfare consumed the remainder, and only the architecture of fortune preserved what we possess. The manuscript is always what stands at stake: destruction bets on annihilation, conquest bets on plunder, and the archive's walls bet on permanence. The vellum itself cannot wager; it can only wait, stretched between heat and steel, hoping the masonry will hold against both.

The scholar's gamble is a bet on what endures and what perishes. Conflagration has claimed what we shall never recover; surviving structures have preserved what we can still decipher. Violence has sliced through the documentary record, and burning has consumed the repositories themselves. What remains constitutes the residue of these ancient wagers: scattered codices, ruined foundations, corroded weapons rusted beyond interpretation, and the scholar's own consuming curiosity that compels us to enumerate what devastation has left behind—though I confess I may be counting shadows.
