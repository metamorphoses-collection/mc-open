#### PAGE 94 -- The Chronicler's Prayer
*Threads: CHRONICLE, FAITH, SWORD, PARCHMENT, GAME*
*Voice: NITHARD*

I pray as I write -- not to God, but to the parchment itself. My chronicle is a prayer addressed to the vellum: hold fast, resist time, carry my testimony beyond my sword. The chronicler's faith is material -- I believe in ink, in scraped animal skin, in the sewn codex. The game of warrior writing is a litany: I recount the cavalry of Fontenoy as the monk recites his psalms -- by repetition, by constraint, by devotion to the narrative. Each page of my Historiae is a stanza of this prayer. The clinamen of battle deviates the text -- a new fact, an unexpected charge, a falling sword -- and the chronicle bifurcates. The game of testimony is to follow these deviations without losing the thread of narrative grace. Armies reform; the narrative too. The armed chronicler's OuLiPian constraint is to write on a blood-stained parchment -- the vellum bears both inks, that of the quill and that of the blade. The rule of my Historiae is fidelity: saying what I saw, transcribing what I heard, praying that the manuscript survives. The codex is my answered prayer. The parchment held. The chronicler's faith did not ask for heaven -- only for vellum. Grace came from the copyist who recopied. The game of testimony is won.
