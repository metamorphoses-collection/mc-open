#### PAGE 100 -- Pro Deo amur
*Threads: WAGER, CHRONICLE, CONSPIRACY, FAITH, MALIETTE*
*Voice: PROFESSOR*

The Porteur de Maliettes sets the last valise on the table. He opens it. Inside, a single sheet -- a reproduction of the Strasbourg Oaths, the first words of French: *Pro Deo amur et pro christian poblo et nostro commun salvament.* For the love of God and for the common salvation of the Christian people. Nithard's chronicle begins and ends with this prayer -- a wager of faith inscribed in the nascent vernacular. The Professor contemplates the sheet. A thousand years of French hold in these words. The monks' conspiracy could not erase this phrase -- no fabrication, no fraud, no forgery resists the simplicity of a prayer in the tongue of the people. The Professor's maliette has carried this prayer from Lille to the world, from workshop to seminar, from manuscript to reader. Nithard's belief, Pascal's faith, the Professor's invention converge toward this same vertex: the text as act of grace, the narrative as offering, the chimera as prayer. The wager is kept. A hundred pages -- fifty voices of Nithard, of Pascal, of the Professor -- have woven a graph of words where chronicle and conspiracy, faith and calculation, maliette and parchment meet in the same vernacular. The Professor closes the valise. The hoped-for gain was not glory -- it was testimony. Chance gave us this text. The probability of grace is a number only God knows. The risk was worth the stake. The wager was memory. The maliette is closed. The journey begins.

*Pro Deo amur.*
