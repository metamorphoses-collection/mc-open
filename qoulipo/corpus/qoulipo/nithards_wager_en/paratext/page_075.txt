#### PAGE 74 -- The Linguistics of Blades
*Threads: CONSPIRACY, TONGUE, SWORD, PARCHMENT, GAME*
*Voice: PASCAL*

The sword has its linguistics -- a grammar of the edge that the parchment records without understanding. The Carolingian chronicles transcribe battles in a Latin that does not know the sound of the blade. The monks' conspiracy is also a linguistic conspiracy: they fabricated accounts of war in a tongue of peace, descriptions of cavalry written by men who had never held a sword. The forgery of the false manuscript is first a fraud of tongue -- the warrior's vernacular betrayed by the cleric's Latin. The vellum bears this betrayal like a scar. The game of fabrication is this: imitating the other's tongue, the other's constraint, the other's rules. The OuLiPo would recognize in the medieval forgery an inverted lipogram: instead of removing a letter, the forger adds a false voice. The conspiracy's clinamen is linguistic -- a deviation of the tongue that betrays the imposture. The authentic parchment carries armies in its syntax; the forgery carries the scriptorium in its turns of phrase. The forger-monk's ink does not smell of blood. The fraudulent codex is a game without rule -- a fabrication that does not know itself as constraint. The sword judges manuscripts better than the scholar. Cavalry does not lie. The parchment sometimes does.
