#### PAGE 60 -- The Workshop of Forgeries
*Threads: CONSPIRACY, TONGUE, SWORD, PARCHMENT, MALIETTE*
*Voice: PROFESSOR*

The Porteur de Maliettes reconstructs the forgers' workshop. On the seminar table, he arranges the tools of conspiracy: artificially aged ink, vellum scraped to simulate wear, Latin grammar studied to imitate the tongue of the ninth century. The fabrication of a forged parchment is a linguistic art -- the forger must master the era's vernacular, avoid anachronisms, reproduce the authentic copyist's errors. The fraudulent manuscript bears the mark of the sword -- not Nithard's, but that of the warrior-monk who protected his scriptorium. Relics and codices came from the same workshop: bones and vellum, sacred fraud and textual fabrication. The Professor compares his maliettes to the forgers' valises. The difference is transparency: his chimeras avow their invention, where the conspiracy conceals its tongue. In his Lille workshop, he fabricates texts that do not claim authenticity -- maliettes full of possibilities, not lies. The forged parchment and the invented parchment share the same ink, the same vellum, the same hand. But the Professor's tongue is honest: it says "this is a chimera" where the monk said "this is a relic." The sword cuts between the fabrication that lies and the fabrication that plays. The maliette carries this distinction like a coat of arms.
