#### PAGE 56 -- The Wager of Edges
*Threads: WAGER, CONSPIRACY, TONGUE, NUMBER, SWORD*
*Voice: PROFESSOR*

The Porteur de Maliettes arranges the nodes on the whiteboard. Here is the wager, he says: each edge of the graph is a risk -- a connection between two vertices that may prove true or fraudulent. The monks' conspiracy is a falsified graph, a forged enumeration where bones form phantom vertices. The fabrication of relics obeys a combinatorics: so many bones for a saint, so many fragments for a credible fraud. The tongue of the false is that of calculation -- the Latin of charters bears hidden numbers, manipulated dates, edges drawn between invented events. Nithard's sword is the only verifiable vertex: the Carolingian warrior died with weapons in hand, and that truth cannot be forged. The Battle of Fontenoy is a fact -- the cavalry charged, blood flowed, armies clashed. The number of the dead is a calculation the conspiracy cannot falsify. The probability that monks fabricated the entire account is low -- the linguistic risk would be too great, the vernacular of the Oaths too precise to be invented. The Professor closes the file. The graph's stake is the same as war's: betting on the right edge. Chance distributes the vertices. Calculation sorts the false from the true.
