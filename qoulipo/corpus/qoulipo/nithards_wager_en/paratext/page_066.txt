#### PAGE 65 -- The Invention of the Tongue
*Threads: CONSPIRACY, TONGUE, PARCHMENT, MALIETTE, GAME*
*Voice: PROFESSOR*

The Porteur de Maliettes poses the disturbing question: what if the vernacular of the Oaths were itself an invention? Not a conspiracy -- not a fraudulent fabrication -- but a linguistic game of which Nithard was the conscious author. The French of the Oaths is so different from Latin that it resembles an OuLiPian constraint: writing in a tongue that does not yet exist. The parchment bears this audacity -- a codex where the vernacular is born under the chronicler's quill as a chimera is born in the Professor's maliette. The fabrication of a tongue is not a conspiracy -- it is a game whose rule is to name what has never been named. In his Lille workshop, the inventor of chimeras practices the same art: he creates words for objects that do not exist, a linguistics of the possible. The manuscript of the Oaths is the first lipogram of Latin -- one removes the learned grammar, keeps the sounds of the people. Constraint produces the tongue as vellum produces the text. Nithard's game and the Professor's game are brothers: inventing a vernacular is fabricating a maliette for words that do not yet have a suitcase. The copyist's ink fixed this game on parchment. The rule of linguistic invention is the same as the clinamen's: deviating from Latin just enough for French to appear.
