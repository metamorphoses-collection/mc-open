#### PAGE 80 -- The Parchment of the Warrior-Praying
*Threads: CHRONICLE, FAITH, SWORD, PARCHMENT, MALIETTE*
*Voice: PASCAL*

Did Nithard pray before battle? His chronicle does not say -- the sword and the quill occupy all the space, leaving little room for faith. Yet the parchment of his Historiae bears the mark of a man who believed: not in victory, but in the necessity of testimony. The warrior's vellum is an act of faith -- writing the account of war is believing that someone will read, that the codex will survive the armies. The cavalry of Fontenoy charged at morning; that evening, Nithard took up his quill. Ink and blood share the same parchment. The Porteur de Maliettes recognizes in this gesture that of the peddler -- the one who carries the text as one carries a relic, not sacred but necessary. In his Lille workshop, he stores war chronicles beside prayer manuscripts. The maliette does not distinguish the sword from the prayer. The vellum bearing the description of a cavalry charge and the one bearing a litany are made of the same skin. The warrior's chronicle is the witness's prayer: I recount because I believe in the narrative. The Professor closes his valise. Nithard's faith is in the parchment, not in the sky. His grace is the ink that held. His codex is his portable cathedral. The maliette continues the journey the manuscript began.
