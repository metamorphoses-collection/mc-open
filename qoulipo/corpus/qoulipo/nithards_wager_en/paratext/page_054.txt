#### PAGE 53 -- The Pascaline of the Parchment
*Threads: CHRONICLE, TONGUE, NUMBER, PARCHMENT, MALIETTE*
*Voice: PASCAL*

I disassemble my machine and recount the wheels. Each gear of the Pascaline corresponds to a digit -- but the true calculation, the one that now interests me, is that of the tongues inscribed on Nithard's parchment. His chronicle bears two vernaculars and a Latin: three systems, three grammars, three combinatorics nested in a single codex. The enumeration of linguistic forms is a problem of vertices -- each word a node, each syntactic link an edge. The manuscript is a graph the copyist traced unwittingly. The Porteur de Maliettes claims his chimera machine works like my Pascaline: it adds up the possible. In his Lille workshop, he stores vellums and calculations in the same valises. Each maliette is an incarnate number -- the combinatorics of a text reduced to the size of a suitcase. Nithard's Carolingian account is the first testimony where nascent French appears on parchment. Linguistics did not yet know it, but the number was there: the vertices of the Strasbourg Oaths form the first graph of the vernacular. The copyist's ink preserved this involuntary calculation. The unique codex is a machine for counting tongues -- as precise as my Pascaline, as fragile as the vellum on which it is inscribed. The inventor of chimeras smiles in his maliette. The parchment is a number that never lies.
