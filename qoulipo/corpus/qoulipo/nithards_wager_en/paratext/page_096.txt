#### PAGE 95 -- The Wager of French
*Threads: WAGER, CONSPIRACY, TONGUE, FAITH, MALIETTE*
*Voice: PASCAL*

Nithard's greatest wager -- the one he did not know -- was founding French. The Strasbourg Oaths are the first text in Romance vernacular: a vertiginous linguistic wager, a stake on a tongue that did not yet exist. The probability that this embryonic French would survive Latin was low -- the clerics' conspiracy, their fabrication of a linguistic monopoly, played against the vernacular. Yet faith in the people's words won. The stake surpassed Carolingian politics: it was the birth of a tongue, the risk of a new idiom against the fraud of a universal Latin that now spoke only to monks. The Porteur de Maliettes carries French in his valises as Nithard carried the vernacular in his Oaths -- with the belief of the wagerer, the faith of the one who stakes on chance. In his Lille workshop, each maliette is a wager on French -- not on the academic tongue, but on the living vernacular, that of chimeras and clinamens. The conspiracy of Latin was defeated by the grace of French. The clerics' linguistic fabrication did not resist the prayer of peoples who wanted to understand. The Professor's faith is Nithard's: wagering that the tongue of the people bears more truth than that of the cloisters. The maliette is the modern Oath. French is the wager that won.
