The Bearer of Cases tallies up the invocations. How oft appears the word "God" within Nithard's Annals? This measuring of devotion through speech is a gamble that scholars of language now pursue -- the enumeration of holy terms, the bindings of words that keep company, the woven fabric of pious utterance. From his travelling case the Professor draws forth a printed reckoning: the catalogue of sacred expressions in the Frankish speech.

Yet the sum of reverence proves unexpected. Divine favour eludes all accounting -- it stands as the testimony without connection, the solitary countenance in the tapestry of holiness. The masters of Port-Royal grasped this truth: heavenly silence is a name that slips past enumeration, a contest whose law is lawlessness itself. The Professor pursues this riddle in his Lille gatherings. His devices toy with number and belief -- strange hybrids where sums dwell alongside supplication.

The language of worship keeps its own strictures. The ordered round of the canticle is a hallowed form -- the selfsame phrases wheel, circle back, exchange positions by a law the brothers knew from memory. The Bearer of Cases discerns in this ritual discipline a forefather of constrained writing: the rigour of counting brought to bear on faith, the calculation of devotion's marks. Within his cases, phantasms of holiness lie beside phantasms of the manuscript. The ledger of sacred ties is a perpetual wager -- each fresh tongue contributes a sum, each local dialect a strand. Grace is the swerve within arithmetic -- the turning aside that makes reckoning into prayer.
