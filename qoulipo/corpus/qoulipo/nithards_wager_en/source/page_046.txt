The Bearer of Cases sets forth the design of strictures. Each discipline conceived by the Society of Constrained Writing stands as contrivance against untrammelled discourse—an artifice compelling common speech to bow, to turn, to yield patterns which everyday tongues resist. The omission of letters becomes a blade severing portions of our lexicon. The Society's conviction holds that such severity begets comeliness.

This struggle of literary device traces lineage to the Strasbourg Oaths themselves. When I, Nithard, set down the common tongue in written form, I imposed discipline upon fledgling Romance speech—the restriction of inscription over utterance, the fashioning of learned expression from soldiers' words. The design proves fundamental: all written language remains artifice, a sanctioned deception, a willing imposture. The conviction maintains that such imposture conveys deeper truth than unadorned verity.

The Scholar enacts this contest of linguistic craft. Within his gatherings, monstrous beings manifest as embodied restrictions—creatures of language existing solely through the discipline that births them. The conspiracy of creation proves the sole joyful conspiracy. The Society's artifice conceals not its edge—rather it parades the weapon openly. Restriction stands brandished as armament, the venture announced as peril. The hazard inherent in literature-yet-to-be matches that of all contrivance: falsity may prove tedious. Yet the desired reward remains the clinamen—that turning of utterance which transmutes restriction into elegance, device into diversion, steel into quill. The Scholar stakes everything upon the possibility of comeliness. The discipline cuts deep. Speech reconstitutes itself.
