The Bearer of Cases grips in one fist a sheet of scraped hide, in the other a facsimile of our Frankish steel. The tongue inscribed upon that membrane is the speech of warriors who knelt before the onslaught. The common talk preserved in my Annals carries the mark of iron—diction hard as tempered metal, clauses brief as commands shouted across the field. My certainty found voice in a soldier's idiom, a newborn Romance hammered out amid the clash of arms.

Within the Professor's travelling satchels, impossible unions wed the holy to the warlike. His Lille atelier begets curiosities where parchment and iron lie down together—phantom manuscripts wherein the tongue of supplication dwells beside the oaths sworn before combat. His creed resembles the artisan's: utterance is shaped as one prepares calfskin, characters are beaten out as one tempers a sword. Sanctity resides in the act itself—the scribe's fingers and the smith's hands perform identical devotions without sound.

My chronicle stands as a field of linguistic strife. French and Latin meet there as hosts at Fontenoy. Each word set down in common speech becomes a drawn hilt against Latin's dominion—a sharpened edge of utterance that cleaves through custom. The Professor grasps that my fidelity to record was equally a fidelity to tongue—the conviction that emergent Romance merited the prepared hide, that vellum need not be hoarded for holy writ alone. His cases bear this truth forward: the people's idiom constitutes supplication as valid as any, the codex inscribed in French stands as hallowed as any Gospel leaf. The blade hammered out our Romance. The scraped skin sanctified it. The Professor's witness carries it onward.
