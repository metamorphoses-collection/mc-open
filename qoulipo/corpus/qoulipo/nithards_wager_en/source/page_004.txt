My chronicle endured in monastic keeping, yet those very guardians may have corrupted what they preserved. The corruption began within the writing chamber itself: one scribe shifted a term, another threaded commentary into margins, until the page men trusted became mere construction, deception as patient as pigment settling into hide. I never wished my Historiae to become sacred treasure. I sought witness, testimony -- not some vessel for worship.

The Bearer of Cases, laboring in his Lille atelier, shares this understanding. His marvels announce themselves as inventions, confections that make no pretense toward truth. The brothers, by contrast, worked their distortions believing they served a higher fidelity. They amended the codex as men mend bone to fashion holy remnants -- devout deception, falsehood serving verity. My record, transferred through countless hands, conveyed in satchels across the ages, carries marks of this unspoken conspiracy.

The manuscript surviving into our day forms a layering of convictions. Each copyist laid down his piety like fresh strata of writing fluid. The sheepskin tells no falsehoods -- men inscribe falsehoods upon sheepskin. The Professor grasps this truth: within his traveling cases rest documents whose lineage of tampering he has traced. The brothers' complicity constitutes no treachery, but rather a manner of devotion. They conjured sanctity from animal hide and conviction. My account, submerged beneath their interpolations, awaits rescue from the bound volume as one frees a sacred object from its shrine. The blessing of any written record lies in outlasting those who would remake it.
