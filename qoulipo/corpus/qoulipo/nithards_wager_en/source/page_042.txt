The Bearer of Cases stakes all upon the surety of writings. Within his travelling satchel he bears my chronicle alongside Pascal's meditation — two tongues, two ages, one burden. The dialect of conviction is a speech this Scholar mastered in Lille, within that atelier of marvels: the language of those who hold that vellum may yet redeem.

His dedication springs not from piety — rather from faith in letters themselves. He wagers upon story as Pascal hazarded upon divine favour: a complete venture, a trial of the soul. Whether these annals shall move any heart remains unknown. The prospect of readers proves as fickle as Heaven's silence. Yet the gamble stands placed — the case lies open, his voice rings forth, the witness is given. What he seeks is passage onward.

This Scholar's speech is a French tempered by certainty. Not worship of the Almighty, but conviction that words harbour remembrance within — that the French of the Sworn Oaths and the French of the Provincial Letters and the French of Lille's marvels form one continuous prayer, one unbroken testimony, one singular hazard. The Bearer of Cases journeys across the centuries with his burden. Grace dwells in the motion itself — bearing trust from one manuscript to another, from one dialect to another, from one satchel to another. The record perishes not whilst it is borne. The peril is weariness — limbs that falter, resolve that fails. Yet this Scholar risks all upon steadfastness. His wager rests upon faith in utterance. The journey persists.
