The Bearer of Cases unlocks a reliquary of his own devising. Within lie no hallowed bones nor sacred tokens—only folded vellum, a beast drawn in lampblack, a litany of rules. The scheme he condemns belongs not to our fathers in their scriptoria, but to this present time: the forging of gravity, the counterfeit of consequence, the hollow pomp of learned speech. His travelling satchels offer an answer—unburdened, sportive, brimming with literary promise.

The Teacher's conviction is that of one who wagers. He trusts in rules as others trust in holy things—with matching ardour, matching elegance, matching willingness to doubt no more. The OuLiPian task serves as his devotion; the clinamen, his creed. In the Lille gathering, imagined beasts arise from piety as much as craft. The design proves joyful: one fashions writing as one fashions the divine, with equal care for sheepskin, for manuscript, for bound volume.

The parchment bearing such beasts requires no golden casket. The worn leather cases suffice—those travelling boxes that bear the Teacher's inventions from one assembly to the next. The task consists of opening the valise before witnesses and calling forth what had no being moments earlier. Invention is the hoped-for treasure of the OuLiPian design: no deception, but making pure. Faith in the precept yields the composition as belief yields prayer. The Teacher fastens his case. The hush that follows partakes of grace—or of the clinamen. The precept has spoken its word. The sheaf of imagined creatures returns to its leather shrine. The task continues still.
