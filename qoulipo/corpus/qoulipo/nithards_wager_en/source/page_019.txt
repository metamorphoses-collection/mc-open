Port-Royal fell to the blade and the royal writ. The chronicle of its ruin is a contrivance of state—a tissue of pious justifications woven to strike down the Jansenist vigil. I set this down upon parchment that may perish before dawn: Port-Royal troubled the realm because its devotion burned too bright. The sacred stillness we pursued within our chambers proved more fearsome than any mounted host.

Did the scribes who transcribed my Pensées understand they served an artifice unwitting? To fashion a posthumous work is ever to commit some measure of deceit—one rearranges, imposes order, misconstrues. The volume that saw print bears scant resemblance to the leaves I abandoned, those sheaves of vellum pinned together in the disorder grace had decreed. Witness is transformed by those who profess to preserve it. The record of my meditation stands recomposed, an editorial imposture.

The sovereign's steel fell upon Port-Royal as Lothar's host descended at Fontenoy. Soldiers need not hold conviction—they answer to command. Yet faith endures even as the page turns to ash. The annals of our downfall are inscribed on scattered leaves, codices concealed in monastic travelling cases. The campaign against Jansenism sought to extinguish our very prayers. Providence ordained that certain manuscripts should escape destruction. The scheme to bury us in oblivion has foundered—the bones of our martyrs yet testify, the remnants of our worship pass from hand to hand beneath concealment. Iron sunders flesh, not devotion. Vellum blackens in flame, yet witness abides. The assault upon prayer is the most ancient conspiracy known to man.
