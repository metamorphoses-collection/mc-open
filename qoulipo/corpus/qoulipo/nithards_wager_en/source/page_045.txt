Latin serves my devotions whilst the common tongue girds my blade. This division runs through my Historiae as a boundary cleaves the spirit -- upon one side piety, upon the other strife. The measure of Latin words upon my vellum dwindles from section to section, as though nascent Romance were devouring the hallowed speech. The reckoning of this withdrawal shall be a task for scholars a millennium hence.

Devotion finds voice in Latin because Providence ordained this tongue -- or because clerics determined thus, which bears less splendour. The silence of Heaven knows no single speech; mortal supplication remains captive to its utterance. My manuscript wavers between two languages, twin testimonies woven through the fabric of holy and earthly matters. The pages of my Historiae preserve the marks of this doubled voice, the Latin of worship and the vernacular of steel.

The tally of horsemen who offered prayers in Latin before their charge in Frankish remains an unseen cipher within the weave of my annals. The hosts of Louis and Charles mingled tongues as oaths mingle destinies -- a many-voiced tapestry wrought by faith and severed by iron. The parchment holds fast this remembrance of such mingling. The accounting of supplications in Latin and commands in common speech lies buried within my codex. The testament's virtue is having preserved both languages, both reckonings, both strands of my days as a warrior who believes.
