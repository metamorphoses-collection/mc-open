The blacksmith who made my blade counted the hammer blows. Each strike is a witness in the tapestry of the weapon -- the tally of gestures forms a reckoning that the metal records in its very structure. The artisan's tongue is a technical vernacular, an idiom of calculation and fire that the Porteur de Maliettes's chimeras would recognize in their own Lille workshop.

The count of weapons forged for Fontenoy is a calculation no one has made. How many blades for how many armies? How many bonds in the fabric of Carolingian armament? The play of war is a play of quantities -- the accounting of forces, the inventory of cavalrymen, the measure of strategic positions. The rule of the military contest is as strict as an OuLiPian constraint: one arranges the pieces, follows the rule, plays the move.

In the Professor's cases, chimeras bear the names of weapons. The Lille workshop forges words as one forges blades -- in the speech of invention, a technical vernacular where each lexical figure is linked to the others by threads of meaning. The play of potential literature is a contest of the forge: one heats the word, hammers it under constraint, tempers it in the rule. The clinamen is the hammer blow that deviates -- the blade that takes an unexpected curve, the name that refuses the planned calculation. The tally of possible words is the web of speech. The weapon and the case share the same metal -- that of invention.
