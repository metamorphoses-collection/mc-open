The Bearer of Cases conveys my history as a warrior bears his blade — with the sureness of long practice and the hazard of the venture. I stake my trust that this travelling case holds the speech that shall preserve our account. The common tongue I set down in my Histories journeys in the Professor's satchels, from Lille to learned gatherings, from colloquy to hall of instruction. Language in passage, the wandering record.

This hazard of handing down is a fighter's gamble. The Carolingian hosts bore campaign coffers — leather strongboxes where battle commands in the Frankish speech lay waiting. The Professor takes up this custom. His inventions are kindly arms, vellum blades raised against forgetting. My witness and his own reckonings travel as companions, the ninth-century vernacular and seventeenth-century French, in a portable case that serves as a movable scriptorium.

The likelihood that our annals outlive those who bear them is the desired prize of all handing down. The peril is disappearance — a case gone astray, a speech lost to memory, an account obliterated by the years. Yet the Professor commits all to movement. The Lille gathering is not a settled place — it is a creation that travels with these inventions, a living speech that will not accept the hush of bookrooms. My testimony merits the Professor's conviction — that keen edge of certainty that cleaves between the lifeless tongue and the one yet breathing. The wager stands thus: that our history, borne from case to case, shall cross the centuries. The prospect of transmission is the mounted company of writings.
