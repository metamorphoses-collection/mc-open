The scheme woven about my mortal remains commences with a sum. How many shards ascribed to Nithard lie scattered among the shrines of Christendom? Such accounting proves no simple matter—belief tangles the calculation. Each fragment stands as testimony within a wider canvas of pious observance, each counterfeit a strand joining reverence to deceit. The brothers fashioned my remains as scribes prepare their vellum—with deliberation, with supplication, trusting that Heaven's blessing might sanctify the spurious.

The weapon that slew me—for I perished in the fray, iron meeting iron, mounted warriors clashing—marked not these venerated remains. Herein dwells the falsity: unmarred bones belonging to one whose life knew naught save warfare. The sum of such contradictions weaves a snare of doubt, its pattern formed by dubious relics, its design traced by riddles left unresolved. What the scholar of ancient tongues discerns accords with what the examiner of mortal clay concludes: these are surely forgeries.

My chronicles, by contrast, preserve the scars of strife—not in substance, but through the lexicon of combat, the hosts I described, the carnage I set down. The codex containing my Histories constitutes my authentic remnant, the folio that devotion ought properly honour above suspect ossuary offerings. The brothers' artifice traded corpus for composition, tangible token for written testament. The ledger recording this imposture tallies designations: how many spurious fragments against how many genuine leaves? The threads of this deception bind contrivance to credulity, counterfeiting to prayer. The roster of those thus misled stretches beyond reckoning. Fidelity, meanwhile, abides upon the written sheet, awaiting those who would peruse it.
