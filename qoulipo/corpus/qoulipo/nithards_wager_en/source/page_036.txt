The Bearer of Cases draws forth the codex from his travelling satchel with all the wariness of one unsheathing a weapon. The parchment is delicate—crackling, its script dimmed by long ages. Yet the witness it bears remains keen as any hilt. Nithard's testimony has surrendered nothing of its acuity. These Annals constitute a drama of compositional strictures that the years have failed to blunt.

Within the Lille atelier, the Scholar has mastered the art of treating manuscripts as instruments of war. Each leaf becomes a thinking sword—slicing through assumed truths, an implement that lays open writings as one might expose a warrior's flesh upon the field. The leather sheath houses the promise; the bound volume serves as armament. Possible writing is the discipline of wielding—understanding when to brandish the restriction, exercising deviation when rigidity threatens to petrify.

The Scholar's craft lies in setting manuscripts into motion. Nithard's bound chronicle has already crossed long spans of time—from the first record through monastic transcription, from Carolingian writing-chambers to repositories of state. The Scholar's case contributes another passage to this pilgrimage: the assembly, the gathering, the space where vellum regains animation. Forged speech revives dormant leaves. The workshop's discipline becomes an elixir for folios—stricture rouses what mere preservation had entombed. The historical testimony draws breath anew within the Scholar's satchel. Faded characters darken once more. The chronicle's blade recovers its sharpness.
