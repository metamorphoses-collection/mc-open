How many forgeries dwell within a single book? I wager no man can number them. The scribes' deception is not confined to one counterfeit alone—it is a fabric of lies, a snare wherein every mark is an emendation and every strand a doubtful inheritance. The tally of corruptions my chronicle has suffered surpasses any sole reckoner's art. The peril of committing words to parchment was likewise the peril of betrayal by those who transcribe.

The falsifiers' craft follows rigorous laws. The scribe's burden is that of the flawless mimic—duplicating the pigment, the script's flow, the contractions of the exemplar before him. The men of OuLiPo would discern in this treachery a manner of constrained composition: writing beneath the yoke of sameness, contesting who copies most exactly, submitting to the pattern's dominion. The deceit lurks in minutiae—a designation altered within the body of the text, a connection forged between two passages that never conversed.

My book is as much a field of war as Fontenoy proved. Counterfeits swarm across the vellum as horsemen across the meadow. The reader's challenge is to separate genuine from spurious—a reckoning of likelihood applied to the codex itself. Truth is the prize sought; transmission is the chance. Each copyist played this game of stratagem in innocence—or with full cognizance. The roster of conspirators is an accounting without terminus. The bonds joining my chronicle to all its descendants, all its falsifications, all its phantom authorities—these constitute a lineage of perfidy. Authenticity itself is what hangs in the balance. The hazard is the leaf. Time is the law.
