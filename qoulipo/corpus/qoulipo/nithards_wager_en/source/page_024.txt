The Bearer of Cases makes tally of these monstrous tongues. Each specimen springs from invention alone—a dialect fashioned within the Lille scriptorium, an idiom belonging to no living soul. The stratagem lies in presenting such contrivances as though they were speech heard in marketplace or hall, setting down upon parchment syllables that no mouth has ever shaped. The counterfeiting of language stands among the most ancient of deceits—to forge a tongue is to forge a nation.

The sum of these fabricated dialects within the Professor's travelling cases forms an intricate web. Each testimony represents a coined term, every strand a contrived grammar. To reckon these potential languages induces vertigo—their number exceeds the lexicon of modern French, their connections outnumber the lines preserved in all our Carolingian codices. The Professor tallies and tallies again. Such linguistic deception demands the art of computation.

The monastic cabal of old wrought falsified documents in spurious Latin—volumes wherein the very speech was counterfeit. The Professor discerns in that medieval imposture the mirror of his own atelier. Yet these chimeric constructions are not forgeries—they are possibilities. The speech of these monsters represents what might have been, the scroll never inscribed. Within his satchels, the quantity of potential languages spreads like endless tapestry. The leaves bearing these linguistic prodigies await their scribe—one bold enough to transpose the dialect of impossibility onto the vellum of actuality. Only this calculus of invention merits our reckoning.
