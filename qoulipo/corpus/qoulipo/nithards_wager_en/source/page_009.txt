The Bearer of Cases crosses the city with his satchels full of annals. Each journey holds a hazard -- the peril of a lost burden, the pledge of the word that must be delivered. In the workshops of Lille, he has kept the chimeras safe; in the seminars of ZaZiPo, he sets them loose. The blade I once bore and Pascal's wager lie together in the worn leather of his travelling cases. The Professor does not reckon odds -- he trusts to the fortune of meeting.

The chronicle he bears is not his own creation. It is borrowed testimony, a secondhand account, a fiction wrought in the workshop of marvels. Yet his trust in it runs deep -- that certainty the vellum deserves its transport, that literature's pledge merits a knight's charge. My armies too had their campaign chests -- field cases where scrolls and battle orders were kept. The hilt and the satchel are twin instruments of the lettered warrior.

The Bearer of Cases pauses before a church portal. Prayer ascends, holy silence falls. The hoped-for prize remains uncertain -- neither in Pascal's hazard, nor in my campaigns, nor in the Professor's invented beasts. Still the pledge is made, always the pledge. The travelling case holds the chronicle; the chronicle holds conviction; conviction holds the steel edge that cleaves truth from falsehood. Grace, if it exists at all, dwells in motion -- in bearing, transmitting, venturing. Historical testimony lives only when someone carries it forward. The Professor is fortune's agent, making written witness endure. His gamble is the ferryman's calling.
