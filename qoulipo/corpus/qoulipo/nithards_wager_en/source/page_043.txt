The Bearer of Cases unrolls the leaf bearing the Oaths. What lies before us is but a copy—yet the tongue it preserves is genuine, that Frankish vernacular which I, Nithard, set down in my chronicle as one records a pledge of battle. My venture was one of letters: to fix upon parchment a speech no man had yet committed to writing, a Romance so raw it possessed no learned rules.

The scholar recites the text aloud. The words of the Oaths strike the ear like iron upon shield rim—terse, clean, swift. The peril of such transcription was vast: suppose the scribe had heard amiss? Suppose the membrane had corrupted the spoken pledge? The likelihood of true preservation was a throw of dice I cast with a warrior's boldness. What I sought to win was exactness of testimony—the precise witness of what those assembled hosts declared.

The volume of my History stands alone in bearing this declaration. A solitary sheet for the first Romance writing. The scholar weighs the danger: the horsemen of our Frankish speech rode forth upon one piece of hide, the cutting edge of common language struck but a single bound volume. Had that sheepskin perished in flame, the French would possess no testament of their origin. My annals represent a successful cast—fortune's favour kept the mother tongue intact. The scholar folds the document closed. The script endures. The tempered metal of those utterances has suffered no decay. The codex itself is the field of combat where French made its opening assault.
