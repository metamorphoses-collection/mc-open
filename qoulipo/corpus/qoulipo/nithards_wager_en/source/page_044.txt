The tongue is the terrain of the subtlest conspiracy. I read Nithard's chronicle and see in it a linguistic construction—not a forgery, but a deliberate shaping of the vernacular. The annalist chose his words as a general positions his blades: each term in Romance is steel planted in Latin soil, each sentence in common speech a thread binding the new tongue to the old.

The count of French words in the Historiae is a tally time has made difficult. How many authentically Carolingian terms? How many names added by copyists? The monks' labor with language was superimposed upon Nithard's original work—a layered plot, a palimpsest of speech. The reckoning of strata is a philological task where each utterance ties itself to its history.

The swords of Carolingian armies spoke every tongue—Frankish, Romance, Saxon. The cavalry at Fontenoy was a multilingual tapestry where each warrior understood a different dialect. The inventory of languages on the battlefield is an accounting the record cannot complete. The linguistic conspiracy is that of an empire fragmenting—Charlemagne's single voice shatters into vernaculars as steel shatters into shards. Each shard is a new utterance, each dialect a strand in the fabric of fragmentation. The calculation of Carolingian tongues is the reckoning of an empire's end.
