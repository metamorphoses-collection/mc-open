The Bearer of Cases sets forth a trial: recount the slaughter at Fontenoy whilst forswearing the very word for mortality's end. A martial lipogram, an OuLiPian shackle fastened upon the battle annals. My tongue, stripped of its most fitting vocabulary, must fashion itself anew—discover some manner of speaking loss that sidesteps the plain term. The lipogram's secret architecture lies in omission: conjuring significance through what remains unspoken.

The sword itself grows questionable under such stricture. Should one prove unable to name what the blade yields, the steel forfeits its purpose in the telling. Hosts advance, mounted warriors thunder forward, yet the unsayable outcome transforms the chronicle into something feigned—a recounting wherein language's very shape conceals what all men understand. The Professor grins: precisely what literature of possibility attempts. The swerve of limitation reveals what straightforward testimony kept hidden.

My witness, reconsidered beneath this OuLiPian law, becomes a stratagem of speech. Did I, the chronicler, comprehend that I was stifling certain verities? Did our Frankish tongue possess its own lipograms—words forbidden, modes of utterance prohibited? The Professor's trial requires hunting through the Historiae for marks of these buried restrictions, the unwitting deceptions wrought by discourse itself. The stratagem is not always purposed—sometimes speech itself conspires, the blade-edge of language that cleaves apart what might be said. The chronicle becomes a contest of ordinances, some manifest, others interred within the scribe's vocabulary, waiting for the Professor to interpret them.
