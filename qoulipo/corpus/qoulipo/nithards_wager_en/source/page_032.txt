The Bearer of Cases is a wandering chronicle. He bears the written record in his very sinews—Nithard's witness and Pascal's cast of dice written into his stride, the language he speaks, the manner of his discourse. The Professor's tongue is modern French shaped by discipline, a learned style conscious of its descent from the Romance tongue of the sworn pledges.

The throw of the living record is his to make. Believing the history merits motion, that the vellum ought not lie dormant in scriptoria—this is the Professor's venture. Whether anyone shall heed him remains doubtful; the prospect of hearers is fickle. Yet at each gathering he places his stake: the Professor unbuckles his travelling case, arrays his marvels, and plays out the possibility of letters before audiences not always drawn to the study of tongues.

The prize he seeks is continuance. Nithard's text endures only if someone bears it forward—and the Professor's sporting enterprise consists in discovering, for each generation, the speech that shall quicken the historical testimony. The codex itself stands unchanged, but the living tongue that expounds upon it shifts with time. The OuLiPo furnished the Professor with a principle: every restriction is a cipher for reading, every slight deviation a portal to significance. The manuscript becomes a contest renewed with each perusal. The written witness is a hazard cast afresh in every age. The Professor embodies risk itself—he who pledges his voice to another's parchment, his words to the ink of those long dead, his method to the annals of bygone days. The folios journey onward. The vernacular transforms. The venture persists.
