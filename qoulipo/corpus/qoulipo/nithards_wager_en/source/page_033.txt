My cavalry moved as living testimony. I numbered the men before Fontenoy -- each rider a witness, each squadron a thread in the army's weave. The commander's calculation is a measure of what might be: how many assaults with this company of horsemen? The sum of possible stratagems surpasses any scribe's reckoning. In my Historiae, I must reduce -- the annals preserve only the chief features of battle's pattern.

The Bearer of Cases would call my record a winnowed lineage. His Lille atelier fashions deliberate combinations where every piece serves purpose -- no excess names, no idle strands. The chronicler's travelling pouch resembles the warrior's: both hold what is needful, the sparse inventory, the accounting that travels in a portable coffer. The peril of such winnowing is the loss of particulars -- the unknown horseman, the blade without owner, the forgotten soul.

The annals-keeper's venture is a wager on proper proportion. How many words suffice for Fontenoy? How many testimonies in the tale's fabric? The desired prize is economy -- the phrase that holds the host, the link that captures the assault. In my field satchel, I bore the provisions and armaments tallies -- the soldier's practical ciphering. These figures appear nowhere in my Historiae, yet they undergird it as unseen cords uphold a woven hanging. The hilt strikes; numbers reckon; the case preserves. The scribe's hazard is that hidden sums may yet be discerned between the lines concerning cavalry.
