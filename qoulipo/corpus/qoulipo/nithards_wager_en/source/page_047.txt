I wager that speech strikes like mounted men-at-arms. My chronicle stands as an onslaught of testimony—each phrase set down in common tongue serves as steel flung against the void. The craft of one who records battles is this: he grasps that his annals themselves make weaponry. Literature of design springs from such understanding—the written page becomes armed encounter, and limitation proves a stratagem of war.

The squadrons of Carolingian speech still thunder across my vellum leaves. The Romance born within these testimonies rides forth as linguistic horsemen—unbroken words, spirited and scarcely bridled, loosed upon parchment as cavalry upon open ground. My wager stands thus: this onslaught shall endure, our living idiom shall outlast the fray, the people's language shall withstand scholarly Latin. What I stake to win is French itself—that tongue forged of iron and calculated risk, of sworn vows and tactical restraint.

The OuLiPian campaign renews the assault. Each stricture sounds as clarion calling riders forward—constraint marshals the mounted speech, while deviation wheels to strike where least expected. Potential literature hazards what we ventured at Fontenoy: a gamble upon peril, our mother tongue committed to an onslaught whose fortune remains doubtful. The destiny of utterance, like the destiny of hilts and blades, determines triumph. Historical record preserves the tale of combat. Weaponry takes form as linguistic trial. Common speech becomes the host. The hazard is the charge made manifest.
