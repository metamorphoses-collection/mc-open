Each act of faithlessness becomes a visage woven into the cloth of fraternal strife. I number them in my annals—Lothar, Pepin, the nobles who altered their loyalties—and every strand binds conspiracy to drawn steel. The calculation of sworn bonds resembles a sanguinary ledger: how many potential perfidies among three kinsmen contending for dominion? The sum defies mortal comprehension. The catalogue of stratagems constitutes the scribe's gamble—a wager that the enumeration shall prove thorough, that no appellation shall slip beyond the weave.

The Bearer of Cases, laboring in his chamber, would sketch this lattice upon plastered stone. His creations are unlikely compositions of vellum and pigment—much like the oaths binding our Frankish lords. Within his travelling satchels, he bears testament that duplicity never runs simple: each forgery spawns its successor, each deception another link in the sequence of counterfeit. The hazard facing our mounted warriors at Fontenoy represented but a single throw among countless others. The fortune governing armed conflict follows reckonings beyond any man's dominion.

The sword's edge severs what tallying cannot untangle. When the enumeration of pledges grows impossibly intricate, when obligations cross in irredeemable snarls, the hosts collide. What hangs in the balance is lifeblood. The prize sought is imperial power. In my own campaign chest—for I too bore such burden, crammed with writs and provision lists—I preserved the muster of warriors answering my standard. Every horseman stood as testimony. Every stake formed a bond between existence and the manufacture of triumph. Scheming and numbering advance in lockstep across fields of battle. The likelihood of enduring represents the sole contest of consequence.
