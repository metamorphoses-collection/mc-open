The Bearer of Cases revealed to me a marvel wrought in his Lille study—a being half-hilt half-utterance, a prodigy spawned from the union of the Strasbourg Vows and the OuLiPian discipline. The account Nithard set down becomes, in the Scholar's grasp, a sportive reckoning wherein common speech girds itself for battle and cold iron declines like Latin.

This prodigy holds my attention. The testimony of those Vows, as the annalist preserved them, captures a tongue in its nativity—French stammers its infant syllables among the weaponry. The hosts stand arrayed and the mounted warriors await a binding pronouncement in a dialect they scarcely comprehend. The riddle facing those who study ancient tongues resembles the digger's task: retrieving beneath accumulated strata of discourse the filaments of the original vernacular. The Scholar's travelling case holds the instruments for such delving.

The swerve of the Strasbourg marvel is a linguistic blade. In the Scholar's OuLiPian trial, the Carolingian testimony warps—utterance twists, the witness reshapes itself, historical record transforms into prospective art. Discipline becomes the sharpened edge that hews the common idiom to release unprecedented forms. The Lille study is Strasbourg inverted: rather than forging speech through political compact, one forges letters through formal limitation. The leather satchel contains both—the steel and the vernacular, the annals and the swerve. The Scholar traffics in marvels as Nithard trafficked in words. The principle remains constant: lending voice to common speech, furnishing utterance with a cutting edge.
