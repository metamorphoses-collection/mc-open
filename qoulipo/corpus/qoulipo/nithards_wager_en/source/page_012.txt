My wager hinges upon a reckoning that numbers alone cannot settle. The prize I seek is boundless if the Almighty exists—yet what I hazard is merely temporal. Arithmetic compels the choice: one must make the venture. Yet the figures in this sacred design are no bloodless ciphers—they are mortal souls, drawn swords, supplications uttered into heaven's silence. The accounting of faith is not pursued on vellum; it is enacted in living flesh.

The trial I set forth is a burden of the soul. Those who delight in constrained verse might say my venture is a text from which disbelief has been expunged—I strike out uncertainty as one strikes a character from the alphabet, and what remains is devotion. The principle is plain: commit everything to a reward that no formula can promise. The catalogue of grounds for trust is without end; so too the roll of grounds for suspicion. The strands of the tapestry swing between these twin lodestars. The possible scripture of conviction is a drama without conclusion.

The hilt lends another measure to the hazard. At Fontenoy, mounted warriors committed their lives to the fortune of the assault—a soldier's throw whose hoped-for prize was breath itself, whose cost was the realm. The roster of the slain is the belated ledger of chance. The likelihood of perishing at Fontenoy was severe; the likelihood of obtaining heavenly mercy remains unknowable. Both ventures share a single shape: an irrevocable commitment, an uncertain reward, a bound that precedes all sums. Warband and worshipper play at the same trial. Blade and vow are twin cords in the same weave of loyalties. Those who yet draw breath bear witness. The count of the fallen is the toll of the venture.
