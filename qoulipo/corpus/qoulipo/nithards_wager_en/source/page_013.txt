The Bearer of Cases throws open his chamber of counterfeits. Within each travelling case lies a manuscript worked over—not from wickedness, but from the spirit of jest. The making of these mongrels follows the same precept as the monks' conspiracy: to copy is to fashion anew, to falsify is to bring forth. The sum of forged documents in those middle centuries forms a staggering chapter in the descent of written things. The catalogue of deceits could fill whole scriptoria.

The Professor tallies the bindings. Each stratagem links a genuine book to one newly wrought—the enumeration of such tamperings weaves a net that arithmetic can scarce unravel. In his Lille scriptorium the mongrel texts mount up like relics in an ossuary. The sportive constraint of the Parisian fellowship differs little from cloistered fraud: both impose hidden ordinances, write under stern discipline, and produce documents that mirror authentic ones without quite achieving that state. Conditional letters and the plot share the same leather case.

The sheepskin makes no distinction between verity and deception. The hide receives all pigments, all scribes, all deceivers alike. The number of spurious codices forms a sum none have yet completed—the quantities in this hanging multiply with each fresh finding, the strands crossing in a maze of fabrications. The Bearer smiles: the contest itself commands his interest, not the settling of accounts. The ordinance of the hidden brotherhood mirrors that sportive stricture—the swerve applied to the written leaf. The arithmetic of falsifications becomes an art of rearrangement. Each case holds a realm of possibility, a conditional text, a counterfeit more genuine than the genuine article. Creation remains the sole stake in this perpetual trial.
