The tongue I set down in ink was not the one that lived upon my lips. The common speech of those Oaths, as I rendered it, stands as much a reckoning as testimony -- each utterance weighed, each turn of the emerging French laid upon sheepskin with the care of one who numbers his letters. Students of language will declare in time that I wove a fabric, a braiding of Romance sounds. I did but record what reached my ears, with the certainty of one who bears witness.

The store of French expressions in the ninth age was slight. The strands of that verbal cloth were few -- the sum of likely pairings might be held within a handful of gatherings. The Bearer of Cases, in his Lille scriptorium, would name this poverty a treasure. His inventions spring from want, his craft from limitation. Within his travelling satchels, he keeps writings where the common tongue strains against Latin as a stripling against a warrior. The catalogue of what may be is the skill of those who possess almost nothing.

My conviction is not that of the learned clerics -- it is the certainty of the fighting man who takes up the quill. I hold that spoken words merit preservation on prepared hide, that the written witness deserves the finest membrane, that the bound volume deserves the ages. The number of petitions I offered before each sheet is an unseen testament woven through my Annals. Ink is my blessing; the scraped skin, my holy silence. The Scholar grasps this -- in his leather carriers, he bears the same dedication. Language is a sum seeking its own meaning, a fabric awaiting its scribe. The vellum abides. The vernacular, like an oath sworn, discovers its form at last.
