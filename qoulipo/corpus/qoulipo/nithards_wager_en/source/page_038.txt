The Bearer of Cases reckons fortune. How many strokes of the blade fell at Fontenoy? The tally staggers the mind—thousands upon thousands of blows exchanged, countless souls woven into battle's tapestry. The mounted soldier's gamble unfolds in each passing moment: as the foe closes distance, his hope of survival dwindles, his prospect of gain contracts to the mere drawing of another breath. From his travelling case the learned man draws forth a demonstration—the strands of destiny plot the cavalry's charge.

What conviction sustains the fighter he cannot measure. Whether divine favour will bless the field of war remains beyond mortal knowing—thus would the brethren of Port-Royal have expressed it. The learned man comprehends this arithmetic of trust: through his visions, in the Lille manufactory, he has discovered that enumeration and devotion make uneasy companions. Calculation demands what is certain; supplication embraces what is uncertain. The sword does not reckon—it cleaves.

Within the learned man's satchels lie diagrams of engagements. Registers of horsemen, links joining positions of advantage, assessments of peril. The military wager stands among the most ancient of measured contests—staking lives for territorial advantage, committing hosts upon an expectation of triumph. The learned man counts and preserves. His leather cases serve as repositories of intellectual strife. The edge of scrutiny cuts through number to lay bare significance. Trust in calculation is the learned man's own venture—that certainty that the roster of names discloses what instinct conceals. The case shuts upon bloodied charts. Chance awaits the coming assault.
