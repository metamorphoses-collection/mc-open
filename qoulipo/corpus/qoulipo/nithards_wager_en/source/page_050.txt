The dye upon this skin is the lifeblood of common tongue. I set it down with the same fingers that clasp the hilt—the grip of one who chronicles battles and parses speech alike. The idiom of the Strasbourg Pledges spreads across vellum as vital fluid spreads at Fontenoy—equal in their crimson hue, their peril, their consequence. To compose in Carolingian script is to enter armed contest with quill as well as iron.

The stricture of this infant speech mirrors a Romance dialect groping for expression amid the clash of arms. Parchment becomes the field of engagement, the writing-reed my weapon, syntax itself the mounted charge. To hazard the mother tongue is a combatant's venture—to set the vernacular against Rome's learned language means pitting a warrior's tempered edge against ecclesiastical dominion. What I seek to win is the independence of utterance, its deliverance from hallowed volumes. What I risk is that none shall comprehend.

The principles governing my composition I do not articulate but know through utterance. Those who theorize about letters might claim I labor under restriction—the burden of one who saw events firsthand, who bore arms, who shares the Emperor's lineage. Each phrase rendered in common idiom is like unsheathing a blade within the annals' struggle. This membrane preserves the confrontation of speech warring against its own nature—the Romance emerging through severance, the codex bleeding with fresh pigment. The hazard of linguistic transformation belongs to me alone: the swerve that brings forth creation, the steel that grants freedom, the document that fashions its own law. Ink remains my sole armament. Spoken words my only testament. The duel my singular campaign.
