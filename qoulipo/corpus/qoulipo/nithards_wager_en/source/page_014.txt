I would venture that the Bearer of Cases holds conviction without recognising it. His practice of transporting manuscripts from atelier to lecture hall, from Lille to ZaZiPo, embodies a species of devotion—that certainty the historical account merits the voyage, that the tale deserves performance before witnesses. His pledge mirrors my own: committing to what cannot be seen. The prize sought is not assurance but the blessing of uncertainty embraced together.

The testimony I myself penned rests in the Professor's satchel as the *Pensées* rest among my documents. The Carolingian soldier's witness and the Jansenist's meditation pursue the same venture: speaking truth in an age that favours muteness. Fortune decreed our vellum should endure—improbable beyond measure, precious beyond reckoning. The discipline of potential writing consists in multiplying strictures to multiply occasions for declaring what is real. Each limitation becomes an invocation. Each deviation, a benediction.

The Professor bears the trust of others. Within his travelling cases, my battle annals consort with his algebraic proofs. The wager remains constant: pledging the chronicle against heaven's muteness, against erasure, against the consuming years. The peril is incomprehension—who still studies Carolingian testimony? who still practises the discipline of OuLiPian rule? Yet the Professor's determination proves more enduring than fortune's whim. He stakes his days upon these burdens as I stake mine upon the longed-for prize of immortality. The struggle knows no conclusion. The historical witness receives fresh hearing at each gathering. Confidence is borne as one bears a case—with persistence, with elegance, without assurance.
