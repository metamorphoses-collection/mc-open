The Professor kept to his workshop in Lille beneath a casement through which November's rains slanted in grey torrents. His travelling case lay open upon the oaken board like some trader's account of dealings beyond mortal reckoning. About him stood his creatures—fashioned of wire and wax, each scarce larger than an infant's thumb—arranged as sentinels keeping vigil. Some bore lions' heads with serpents' tails; others married eagle and wolf in forms no student of Creation would acknowledge. These he had disposed across the board's expanse in studied array, keeping between each beast a measure he took with bone calipers.

What he termed in his daybook the "workshop trial" required him to choose certain creatures whilst leaving others aside, ensuring no two selected beasts might stand within arm's reach—that fatal span beyond which their fancied enmities could find no purchase. The challenge was plain enough: each figure admitted to the company must hold its dominion uncontested, menaced by no fellow among the chosen. With fine pincers he moved between them, now lifting a griffin, now a basilisk, testing arrangements as a chess-player proves gambits in the Spanish opening. Every disposition suggested its own history, its own succession of meetings in his imagined menagerie.

This ordering had consumed him three days entire without pause. He kept reckonings in a daybook bound with green morocco, noting which assemblies proved fullest—which dispositions permitted the greatest multitude of beasts to abide together in their uneasy truce. At times he sorted them into kindred, setting the flame-breathers apart from the stone-gazers, the poison-fanged distant from the scale-winged. Each family presented its own riddle, its own stubborn refusal of peaceable selection. The downpour drummed steadily upon the slates above.

When evening fell, he kindled three tapers and pursued his labours, the lengthening shadows making the creatures seem to stir of their own accord. Within the satchel lay his tools: the calipers, the pincers, silk for cleaning the wax forms, and a compass for describing circles of banishment. On the morrow he would commence afresh with altered spacing, some new determination of allowable nearness.
