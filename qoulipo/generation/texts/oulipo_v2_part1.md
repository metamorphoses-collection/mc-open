#### PAGE 1 — L'Ouverture du Codex
*Threads: CHRONIQUE, PARCHEMIN, JEU*
*Voice: PASCAL*

I found the chronicle by accident, in a codex bound with calfskin, its parchment brittle as doctrine. Someone had copied Nithard's testimony onto vellum so thin the ink bled through to the verso, creating a palimpsest of wars and oaths. The scribe's hand trembled — you could see it in the descenders. I thought: here is a game older than my arithmetic, a constraint imposed not by OuLiPo but by history itself. Every chronicle obeys a rule. Every record excludes what the witness cannot bear to set down. The potential literature of the ninth century was already playing at lipograms — silencing what must not be said.

#### PAGE 2 — Le Serment et le Sang
*Threads: CHRONIQUE, FOI, ÉPÉE*
*Voice: NITHARD*

I, Nithard, grandson of the Emperor, set down this testimony before God and the armies. At Fontenoy the cavalry broke against our left flank and the blood ran into the furrows until the earth refused it. I record this not from pride but from faith — someone must chronicle what prayer alone cannot redeem. The battle lasted from dawn through terce. Louis held the ridge; Charles charged the centre. I wielded my sword where the line thinned, believing grace would spare me for the work of witness. It did not spare the others. Their belief died with them in the mud.

#### PAGE 3 — Le Pari du Sang
*Threads: PARI, FOI, ÉPÉE*
*Voice: PORTEUR*

Le Porteur de Maliettes stood before the battlefield map in his Lille workshop and said: every war is a wager. The cavalry at Fontenoy bet their lives on the odds of a charge succeeding — expected value measured in corpses. Pascal would have understood. His pari was about faith, but faith and battle share the same structure: you stake everything on what you cannot prove. The sword decides nothing that belief has not already risked. Le Porteur traced the lines of advance with his finger, calculating probabilities no one at Fontenoy had reckoned, and whispered: the gamble was already lost before the first blood.

#### PAGE 4 — Les Vertices du Parchemin
*Threads: NOMBRE, PARCHEMIN, MALIETTE*
*Voice: NITHARD*

I did not know the word for what I did — counting. But when the scribes brought me the manuscripts after Fontenoy, I numbered the dead on parchment, column by column, as one might enumerate vertices on a graph whose edges are debts of blood. Each name a node. Each kinship a connection. The ink dried slowly in the cold scriptorium. Centuries later, le Porteur de Maliettes would open his workshop in Lille, fill his maliettes with chimères made from such calculations, and call it invention. But I, Nithard, was the first to count the cost of empire on vellum. The numbers did not lie. The codex remembers.

#### PAGE 5 — La Probabilité des Langues
*Threads: PARI, LANGUE, NOMBRE*
*Voice: NITHARD*

When we swore the Oaths at Strasbourg, I wagered on the vernacular. Louis spoke in the Roman tongue so Charles's men would understand; Charles answered in the Germanic. I recorded both, betting that language itself was the stake — not Latin but the living tongue, the risk of being understood by common soldiers. I counted the words: each oath a calculation, a combinatorics of loyalty. The probability that French would survive as more than a battlefield dialect — who could have reckoned those odds? Yet I set it down, every syllable a gamble against oblivion, and the number of witnesses was my only surety.

#### PAGE 6 — Le Triangle des Langues
*Threads: LANGUE, NOMBRE, MALIETTE*
*Voice: PASCAL*

The triangle I discovered in numbers, le Porteur de Maliettes discovered in tongues. From his workshop in Lille he packed his maliettes with chimères — hybrid creatures stitched from French, Latin, Carolingian vernacular. He counted the vertices where languages intersect: three for the Strasbourg Oaths alone, dozens for the linguistic graph of medieval Europe. I built my triangle from combinatorics, each row a calculation of coefficients. He builds his from invention, each chimère a node where one tongue becomes another. The number of possible crossings is, I confess, beyond even my arithmetic. Language multiplies faster than edges in a complete graph.

#### PAGE 7 — La Conspiration du Scriptorium
*Threads: COMPLOT, PARCHEMIN, JEU*
*Voice: PORTEUR*

Le Porteur de Maliettes discovered the conspiracy in a footnote. Monks at Saint-Riquier had fabricated a relic — bones claimed to be Nithard's, buried with forged parchment to authenticate the fraud. The forgery was elegant: a scribe had copied Nithard's own hand so precisely that only the vellum's dating betrayed the fabrication. Le Porteur smiled. Here was a game worthy of OuLiPo: a constraint so rigorous — imitate a dead man's script — that it became potential literature. Le Complot des Moines, he would title his lecture. Every manuscript is a rule. Every codex, a lipogram hiding what the monks dared not confess.

#### PAGE 8 — Le Pari du Chroniqueur
*Threads: PARI, CHRONIQUE, PARCHEMIN*
*Voice: NITHARD*

I wager my chronicle against time. Every testimony written on parchment is a bet that the vellum will outlast the ink, that the codex will survive the fires, that some future reader will care what happened between brothers who divided an empire. The odds are not favourable. I have seen manuscripts rot in damp cellars, seen scribes copy errors that erase the original record. Yet I write. The probability of survival is small, but the expected value of a chronicle preserved is infinite — for without the witness, the event never happened. I stake my Historiae on this wager alone.

#### PAGE 9 — L'Arithmétique de la Guerre
*Threads: PARI, NOMBRE, ÉPÉE*
*Voice: PASCAL*

War is arithmetic with swords. I have calculated the odds: at Fontenoy, forty thousand men on each side, the probability of any single cavalry soldier surviving a charge approximately one in three. The expected value of valour is negative. Yet they wagered their lives — not from calculation but from something my numbers cannot capture. Each battle is a graph: vertices are positions, edges are lines of attack. The combinatorics of slaughter follow rules as precise as my triangle. I count the dead in rows and columns, and the sum is always the same: war pays no dividend. The gamble always favours the grave.

#### PAGE 10 — L'Enjeu de Fontenoy
*Threads: PARI, CHRONIQUE, ÉPÉE*
*Voice: NITHARD*

I record the wager of Fontenoy. My chronicle is testimony to the stakes: an empire bet on a single battle, brother against brother, sword against sword. Lothair risked everything on his cavalry's charge. The odds favoured him — he held the high ground, his armies were rested. But war is not probability; it is blood. I fought on the left flank where the gamble was closest to madness. Charles wagered on our discipline. We held. The chronicle must record that the bet was won not by courage but by the accident of a broken line. The stakes were empire. The cost was kinship.

#### PAGE 11 — Le Pari de la Grâce
*Threads: PARI, FOI, PARCHEMIN*
*Voice: PASCAL*

My wager is not about swords but about grace. I bet on the infinite — if God exists, the expected value of belief is everything; if not, I lose only the finite pleasures of a mathematician's life. I wrote this on parchment at Port-Royal, where the Jansenists pray in divine silence, waiting for a faith that may never answer. The manuscript trembles in my hand. The ink of the Pensées is my testimony: not a chronicle of war but of the soul's gamble. Every prayer is a bet placed on vellum too fragile for certainty. Yet I stake my life on it. The odds demand nothing less.

#### PAGE 12 — Le Graphe du Duel
*Threads: NOMBRE, ÉPÉE, JEU*
*Voice: PORTEUR*

Le Porteur de Maliettes drew the graph on a café napkin in Lille: vertices for every duel in the Carolingian wars, edges for every alliance that broke. The combinatorics were staggering — a number so large it defeated counting. "This is the game," he told his students. "OuLiPo understood that every constraint generates its own potential literature. The rule of the sword generates the calculation of empire." He sketched Le Nithardosaure in the margin, that chimère of war and numbers, and laughed. Each battle was a node. Each betrayal, an edge. The game had no clinamen — only the pitiless arithmetic of blades.

#### PAGE 13 — Le Témoin Devant Dieu
*Threads: CHRONIQUE, FOI, PARCHEMIN*
*Voice: NITHARD*

I write this chronicle before God, who sees the parchment and the hand that holds the quill. My faith compels the record: if I do not set down what happened, who will testify? The vellum is my witness, the ink my prayer. At the scriptorium after Fontenoy, I copied my Historiae by candlelight, believing grace would guide the pen even when memory faltered. The manuscript is imperfect — every codex is. But belief demands that we record what we saw, not what we wished. God does not require perfect testimony. He requires only that the scribe not look away.

#### PAGE 14 — La Sextine des Langues
*Threads: CHRONIQUE, LANGUE, JEU*
*Voice: PORTEUR*

Le Porteur de Maliettes composed his sextines from Nithard's chronicle, rotating the end-words like a constraint machine: tongue, vernacular, French, Latin, Oaths, record. Six words, six stanzas, the rule as rigid as the Strasbourg ceremony itself. "Nithard invented potential literature without knowing it," le Porteur argued in his ZaZiPo lectures. "The moment he recorded two languages side by side, he created a linguistic game — a lipogram of empire, where what is unsaid in one tongue must be spoken in another." The chronicle became a workshop. Every oath, a permutation. The OuLiPo of the ninth century was already writing itself.

#### PAGE 15 — Les Os Truqués
*Threads: COMPLOT, ÉPÉE, PARCHEMIN*
*Voice: PASCAL*

I have studied conspiracies of reason, but this fraud surpasses them. Monks at Saint-Riquier claimed to possess Nithard's bones — the warrior-chronicler, buried with his sword, authenticated by parchment they themselves had forged. The fabrication was precise: they copied battle scars onto the relic, invented a cavalry wound to match the Historiae. The vellum they used was genuine ninth-century manuscript, stolen from a damaged codex — fraud built on authentic material. I, who wager on the invisible God, am astonished by men who forge the visible dead. Their conspiracy required more faith than my Pensées. The forgery of bones demands belief in one's own lie.

#### PAGE 16 — La Langue Falsifiée
*Threads: COMPLOT, LANGUE, PARCHEMIN*
*Voice: PASCAL*

The monks' conspiracy extended to language itself. They forged not only bones but text — a fabricated passage in the vernacular, inserted into a copy of the Historiae as though Nithard had written words he never spoke. The Latin was competent; the French, anachronistic. A tongue that would not exist for two centuries appeared on parchment supposedly ninth-century. I, who study the grammar of proof, recognize the forgery instantly: the linguistic evidence condemns them. Every manuscript carries its language like a date-stamp. The scribes who committed this fraud understood parchment but not the history of the tongue they counterfeited. Their conspiracy collapsed at the level of a vowel.

#### PAGE 18 — La Langue du Témoin
*Threads: CHRONIQUE, LANGUE, PARCHEMIN*
*Voice: NITHARD*

I chose the tongue of my chronicle with care. Latin for the court, the vernacular for the Oaths — because the soldiers who swore at Strasbourg spoke no Latin, and a record must honour the language of its witnesses. I set down both on parchment, the French beside the Germanic, ink still wet when the vellum was rolled. My Historiae is a linguistic monument I did not intend: the first manuscript to preserve the living tongue of those who would become the French. The codex holds what speech cannot — the permanence of a language that existed, for one morning in Strasbourg, only in the mouths of armed men.

Every scribe wagers against decay. I copied my chronicle onto the best parchment the monastery could provide, betting the vellum would hold the ink through centuries of damp and war. #### PAGE 22 — Le Dénombrement des Serments

*Voice: NITHARD*

I counted the words of the Strasbourg Oaths on my parchment: forty-two in the Roman tongue, thirty-eight in the Germanic. The number matters — each word a vertex in the graph of alliance, each phrase an edge binding one people to another. The combinatorics of the vernacular are finite: only so many ways to say "I will not betray my brother." Yet the languages diverge. French bends where Germanic breaks. I copied both onto the same vellum, the manuscript holding what calculation alone cannot: the fact that two tongues, counted and compared, produced a single oath. The number of words is small. *Threads: PARI, CHRONIQUE, JEU*

Le Porteur de Maliettes invented the game he called "Le Pari de Nithard": take the chronicle, assign each testimony a probability of truth, then wager on the outcome. "Every chronicle is a bet," le Porteur explained, "a gamble that the record will be believed." OuLiPo taught him that potential literature lives in the constraint itself. Nithard's wager was the oldest: write what you witnessed, risk that no one reads it, and accept the odds. The game has no winner. The chronicle is its own reward.

#### PAGE 25 — Le Complot des Maliettes
*Threads: CHRONIQUE, COMPLOT, MALIETTE*
*Voice: PORTEUR*

Le Porteur de Maliettes discovered the conspiracy on a Tuesday in March, reading microfilm at the Bibliothèque nationale. The chronicle had been altered — monks at Saint-Riquier had inserted a fabrication into Nithard's Historiae, forging an entire folio to legitimise their relic fraud. He packed the photocopies into his maliettes and took the train back to Lille. "Le Complot des Moines," he murmured, the inventor of chimères already reshaping the forgery into literature. The conspiracy was old, but the suitcase made it new. Every fabrication, carried far enough, becomes a chronicle of its own. The monks had failed at fraud. They had succeeded, unwittingly, at invention.
