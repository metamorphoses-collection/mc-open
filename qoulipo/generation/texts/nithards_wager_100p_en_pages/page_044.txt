-- The Latin of Prayer Latin is the tongue of my prayer and the vernacular that of my sword. This division traverses my Historiae as an edge divides a graph -- on one side faith, on the other war. The number of Latin words in my parchment diminishes from chapter to chapter, as if nascent French were gnawing at the sacred. The enumeration of this retreat is a calculation linguists will perform a thousand years after me. Faith is expressed in Latin because grace chose this tongue -- or because monks decided so, which is less glorious. Divine silence is polyglot; human prayer is prisoner of its tongue. My parchment hesitates between two idioms, two vertices in the graph of the sacred and the profane. The manuscript of the Historiae is a bilingual codex -- the vellum bears the traces of this double voice, the Latin of belief and the vernacular of the sword. The number of cavalrymen who prayed in Latin before charging in Frankish is an invisible vertex in the graph of my chronicles. The armies of Louis and Charles mingled tongues as edges mingle vertices -- a multilingual network woven by faith and cut by the sword. The parchment preserves this memory of mixture. The enumeration of prayers in Latin and orders in vernacular is a calculation my codex carries in its depths. The manuscript's grace is to have preserved both tongues, both numbers, both edges of my life as a believing soldier.
