-- The Vernacular of Chimeras The Porteur de Maliettes enumerates the chimeras. In each maliette, an invented tongue -- a vernacular born in the Lille workshop, an idiom belonging only to chimeras. The conspiracy consists of passing these inventions off as natural tongues, inscribing on the parchment words that have never been spoken. Linguistic fabrication is the oldest of conspiracies -- fabricating a tongue means fabricating a people. The number of linguistic chimeras in the Professor's maliettes forms a complex graph. Each vertex is an invented word, each edge a fabricated syntactic relation. The combinatorial calculation of these potential tongues is a vertiginous enumeration -- more vertices than words in present-day French, more edges than sentences in all Carolingian parchments combined. The Professor counts and recounts. Linguistic fraud is an art of number. The monks' conspiracy included the fabrication of forged parchments in false Latin -- codices where the tongue itself was a falsification. The Professor recognizes in this medieval fraud the gesture of his own workshop. The chimeras are not forgeries -- they are possibilities. The vernacular of chimeras is the tongue of what could have existed, the manuscript of what was never written. In his maliettes, the number of potential tongues is an infinite graph. The parchment of chimeras awaits its copyist -- someone who will dare to transcribe the vernacular of the impossible onto the vellum of the real. The conspiracy of invention is the only calculation that counts.
