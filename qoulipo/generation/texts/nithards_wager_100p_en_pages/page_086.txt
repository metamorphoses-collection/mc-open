-- The Graph of the Vernacular The Porteur de Maliettes traces on the board the graph of French vernacular -- from Nithard to our day. Each vertex is a founding text: the Strasbourg Oaths, the Sequence of Saint Eulalia, the Song of Roland. The edges link the manuscripts by linguistic ties -- borrowings, filiations, echoes. The enumeration of the stages of French is a calculation the chronicle makes possible: without Nithard's testimony, the graph's first vertex would be missing. The wager of tracing this graph is the Professor's: staking that the combinatorics of texts reveals the hidden structure of the tongue. Each parchment added is a risk -- a vellum misread, a codex misdated can falsify the number of vertices. The probability that the graph is complete is zero: too many manuscripts lost, too many vellums destroyed. The stake of the enumeration is nonetheless immense -- understanding how Latin vernacular became French. The chance of manuscript survivals draws a lacunar graph, broken edges between erased vertices. Nithard's parchment is the oldest node -- the one without which the calculation does not begin. The Professor inscribes the date: 842. The Oaths. The first vertex of the graph. The account of a linguistic wager that founded a tongue. The number of pages between Strasbourg and today is infinite. The chronicle continues.
