-- The Probability of the Sacred The Porteur de Maliettes calculates the probability of the sacred. How many tongues does it take to say God? The number of idioms in which faith has been expressed forms an immense graph -- the Latin of the Fathers, the vernacular of the faithful, the French of the mystics, the silence of Port-Royal. Each tongue is a vertex, each translation an edge. The enumeration of possible prayers exceeds the combinatorics of any parchment. The linguistic wager is also a theological wager. When the Strasbourg Oaths pass from Latin to nascent French, it is political faith that changes tongue -- and with it, the belief that carries the oath. The Professor understands that the risk of translation is the very risk of grace: one stakes on words whose hoped-for gain is comprehension, but the chance of the tongue can betray the intention. The vellum bears the traces of this wager -- words in Romance beside words in Latin, a bilingual parchment like a codex in two voices. The number of manuscripts that have transmitted faith is finite. The calculation is simple: a few hundred codices, a few thousand parchments, and yet a belief that touches millions. The probability of transmission is a stake the copyists never formulated, but that the Professor enumerates in his maliettes. The vertices of the graph are the tongues; the edges, the translations. Faith passes from one vernacular to another as ink passes from one vellum to another. Divine silence is the only vertex without an edge -- the number that refuses calculation, the tongue that refuses words, the parchment that remains blank.
