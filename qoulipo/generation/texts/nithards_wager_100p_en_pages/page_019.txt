-- The Stake of the Vellum I wager that the conspiracy will not die with the parchment. The number of falsifications inscribed on the vellum of my Historiae is a calculation that only faith can confront. How many accomplice hands have touched this codex? How many vertices in the graph of the fraud? The enumeration of monks who altered my manuscript is a stake that surpasses simple philology -- it is a wager on the very nature of truth. The fabrication of relics from my bones is the pinnacle of the conspiracy. They took my bones, distributed them in reliquaries, fabricated the sacred from the dead. The number of these relics exceeds that of the parchments -- the edges of the graph link each bone fragment to a belief, each fabrication to a prayer. The combinatorial calculation of monastic frauds is vertiginous. The probability that my remains are authentic is a wager no one can settle. The faith I place in my own account is fragile. The parchment betrays -- the vellum degrades, the ink fades, the codex disintegrates. The chronicler's risk is the same as the believer's: staking on a support that will not hold. The conspiracy of time is the most silent of all -- no deliberate fabrication, just the wear that transforms number into nothingness, vertices into dust, edges into oblivion. My wager is that faith in the manuscript will survive the manuscript itself. The hoped-for gain is memory. The stake is the entire graph of my chronicle, each vertex of truth wrested from the enumeration of forgeries.
