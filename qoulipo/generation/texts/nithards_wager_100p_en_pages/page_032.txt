-- The Cavalry of Numbers My cavalry was a number in motion. I counted the men before Fontenoy -- each cavalryman a vertex, each squadron an edge in the graph of the army. The commander's wager is a combinatorial calculation: how many charges possible with this number of swords? The enumeration of strategies exceeds the chronicler's capacity. In my Historiae, I simplify -- the narrative retains only the principal vertices of the battle's graph. The Porteur de Maliettes would say my chronicle is a pruned network. His Lille workshop produces combinatorial chimeras where each element counts -- no superfluous vertex, no useless edge. The chronicler's maliette is the soldier's: one stores the essential, the minimal enumeration, the calculation that fits in a leather satchel. The risk of pruning is losing detail -- the anonymous cavalryman, the nameless sword, the erased number. The chronicle's wager is a wager on the right number. How many words to say Fontenoy? How many vertices in the narrative's graph? The hoped-for gain is efficiency -- the sentence that contains the army, the edge that summarizes the charge. In my campaign maliette, I carried the supply and ammunition calculations -- the soldier's pragmatic enumeration. These numbers do not appear in my chronicle, but they support it as invisible edges support a graph. The sword strikes; number counts; the maliette stores. The testimony's wager is that the hidden calculation will be read between the lines of the cavalry.
