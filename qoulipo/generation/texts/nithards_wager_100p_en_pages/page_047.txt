-- The Calculation of Forgeries I calculate the forgeries. The number of forged parchments in the Middle Ages is a graph whose vertices multiply with each discovery. The monks' conspiracy is a combinatorics -- each fabrication begets new edges, each fraud an additional vertex in the network of suspect manuscripts. The philologist's wager is to enumerate these forgeries without losing himself in the graph. The calculation of falsifications is an art of the intellectual sword. Each unmasked fraud is a blade that cuts through the network of complicities. The monks who fabricated charters, relics, and bones obeyed an arithmetic of belief -- the number of faithful multiplied the hoped-for gain of the forgery, the probability of denunciation diminished with the monastery's power. The forger's wager is a risk calculation. The parchment does not distinguish conspiracy from truth. The vellum welcomes the ink of the false as that of the true -- the authentic codex and the fabricated codex share the same material. The number of edges linking my own manuscript to the graph of falsifications is an enumeration I refuse to make. How many suspect vertices in my Pensees? How many editorial fabrications? The sword of doubt cuts into my own parchment. The wager is that the true survives the conspiracy -- that the calculation of forgeries, however vertiginous, cannot erase the hoped-for gain of authentic testimony. The graph of frauds is immense. But the vertex of truth holds.
