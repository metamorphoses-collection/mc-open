-- The Chronicler's Sestina My chronicle is an involuntary sestina. The same words return -- battle, betrayal, oath -- in an order I do not control, a rotation that number secretly governs. The game of the sestina is a combinatorial calculation applied to the parchment: six end-words permuted according to a strict rule, an enumeration of possible positions that creates the illusion of destiny. My testimony in the Historiae obeys this constraint without knowing it. The chronicler's wager is that of the sestina player: accept the rule, stake on constraint, wager that the rotation of vertices will produce a graph that makes sense. Each page of my parchment is a vertex in the cycle -- the edges link events according to an order that time imposes and number verifies. The calculation of repetitions is the hoped-for gain of every chronicle: one always returns to the same places, the same betrayals, the same swords. The OuLiPian game would recognize in my Historiae the structure of a classical constraint. Potential literature exists in every narrative that obeys a cycle -- the Carolingian chronicle like the Provencal sestina. The risk is monotony; the gain, depth. The parchment turns upon itself like the words of a poem. The codex is a circular enumeration. My wager is that the reader will see in these repetitions not a flaw but a rule, not chance but a calculation, not raw testimony but a learned game inscribed in the ink of the vellum. The vertices of my chronicle are the nodes of the sestina. Number makes them dance.
