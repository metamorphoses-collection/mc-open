-- The False in the Tongue The monks who fabricated the forgeries knew how to choose their tongue. The parchment's conspiracy begins with a linguistic decision: writing in Latin to give the false the authority of the true. My chronicle, my Historiae, is in Latin because it is the tongue of kings -- but the Oaths, I transcribed them in vernacular because political truth demanded the words of the people. Monastic fabrication proceeds differently: it chooses the tongue that masks the fraud. The forged manuscript bears a grammar too perfect -- the vellum is new, the ink smells of falsehood. The complicit copyist knows the fraudulent codex must imitate the patina of time. The Porteur de Maliettes, in his Lille workshop, knows this gesture: the invention of chimeras also requires a choice of tongue. The French of his maliettes is not that of the academy -- it is an invented vernacular, a linguistics of the possible. His valises bear the weight of the avowed false, the claimed fabrication. The monks' conspiracy and the Professor's workshop share a secret: every text is a choice of tongue, and every choice of tongue is a risk. My testimony in Latin is true because I paid for it with my blood. The forgers' parchment is false because their ink never flowed on a battlefield. The chronicle distinguishes account from fraud. The maliette carries both.
