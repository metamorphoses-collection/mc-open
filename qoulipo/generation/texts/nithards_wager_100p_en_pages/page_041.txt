-- The Faith Carried The Porteur de Maliettes wagers on the faith of texts. He carries in his valises Nithard's chronicle and Pascal's belief -- two tongues, two eras, one maliette. The vernacular of faith is an idiom the Professor learned in Lille, in the workshop of chimeras: the tongue of those who believe the text can save. The Professor's faith is not religious -- it is belief in literature. He stakes on narrative as Pascal staked on grace: a total wager, an existential stake. The probability that the chronicle will touch someone is uncertain. The chance of readers is as capricious as divine silence. But the stake is placed -- the maliette is open, the tongue deployed, the testimony offered. The hoped-for gain is transmission. The Professor's vernacular is a French worked by faith. Not faith in God, but the belief that the tongue carries memory within it -- that the French of the Oaths and the French of the Provinciales and the French of the Lille chimeras are the same prayer, the same chronicle, the same wager. The Porteur de Maliettes traverses the centuries with his valises. Grace is in the movement -- carrying faith from one text to another, from one tongue to another, from one maliette to another. The chronicle does not die so long as it is carried. The risk is fatigue -- arms that give out, belief that weakens. But the Professor stakes on obstinacy. His wager is that of faith in the tongue. The maliette continues.
