-- The Calculation of the Forge The blacksmith who made my sword counted the hammer blows. Each strike is a vertex in the graph of the blade -- the enumeration of gestures forms a combinatorics that the metal records in its very structure. The blacksmith's tongue is a technical vernacular, an idiom of calculation and fire that the Porteur de Maliettes's chimeras would recognize in their own Lille workshop. The number of swords forged for Fontenoy is a calculation no one has made. How many blades for how many armies? How many edges in the graph of Carolingian armament? The game of war is a game of numbers -- the combinatorics of forces, the enumeration of cavalrymen, the calculation of strategic vertices. The rule of the military game is as strict as an OuLiPian constraint: one arranges the pieces, follows the rule, plays the move. In the Professor's maliettes, chimeras bear the names of swords. The Lille workshop forges words as one forges blades -- in the tongue of invention, a combinatorial vernacular where each lexical vertex is linked to the others by edges of meaning. The game of potential literature is a game of the forge: one heats the word, hammers it under constraint, tempers it in the rule. The clinamen is the hammer blow that deviates -- the blade that takes an unexpected curve, the number that refuses the planned calculation. The enumeration of possible words is the graph of the tongue. The sword and the maliette share the same metal -- that of invention.
