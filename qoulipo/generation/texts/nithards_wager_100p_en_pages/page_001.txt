-- The Opening of the Maliettes The Porteur de Maliettes sets the first valise on the seminar table and opens it. Inside: a parchment folded in four, a replica Carolingian sword, a deck of cards marked on the back. He says: everything begins with a wager. Pascal knew it, Nithard had lived it without formulating it. Faith and the blade share the same structure -- one stakes one's entire life on a gain one may never verify. The risk of believing is no less than the risk of charging at Fontenoy. The true stake hides in the parchment, that vellum gnawed by time that copyists transmitted from codex to codex, from ink to ink, across the centuries. The Professor closes the maliette. Potential literature is a game of constraints, he says, and the first constraint is this: accept that the rule precedes the text. The OuLiPo did not invent the constraint -- it named it. Before Queneau, before Perec and his lipogram, there were monks copying manuscripts according to rules they did not question. The clinamen, that minuscule deviation that makes the text bifurcate, already existed in the hand of the copyist who trembled on the vellum. The probability that a parchment survives a thousand years is infinitesimal. Yet Nithard's has held. Chance does not suffice to explain this persistence -- one must see in it a grace, or at least a stake that only faith can measure. The sword and the prayer, the wager and the game, the parchment and the belief: the Porteur de Maliettes arranges the pieces on the chessboard. Divine silence answers the silence of the manuscript. The stake is placed. The hoped-for gain is the narrative itself.
