-- The Bones Counted I counted the dead after Fontenoy -- one by one, as one counts vertices in a graph no one has drawn. My chronicle is also a calculation: how many cavalrymen fallen, how many lines to tell the truth of war. The monks count differently. Their conspiracy rests on a different number -- that of bones one must assemble to fabricate a credible relic. The fabrication of forgeries requires precise arithmetic: too many bones arouse suspicion, too few ruin the fraud. In my Historiae, each enumeration is an act of faith -- I believe in testimony as the copyist believes in the ink that dries. The Porteur de Maliettes, in his Lille workshop, aligns his chimeras with the same rigor. Each maliette is a vertex in the combinatorics of true and false. He stores his valises as monks stored their relics, but his invention does not claim sanctity. The monks' conspiracy is a falsified graph -- edges drawn between scattered bones to simulate a single body. My chronicle is the account of what actually happened. Faith obliges me to distinguish honest calculation from fraudulent fabrication. The number of the dead does not lie. The vertices of the funeral graph are the true witnesses of battle. The Professor knows it: his maliette contains proof that every enumeration is a prayer -- an act of belief in the edge that links the living to the dead, the true to the false, the narrative to grace.
