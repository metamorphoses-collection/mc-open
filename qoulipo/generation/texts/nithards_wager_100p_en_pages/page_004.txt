-- The Graph of Betrayals Each betrayal is a vertex in the graph of civil wars. I enumerate them in my Historiae -- Lothar, Pepin, the counts who switched sides -- and each edge links a conspiracy to a sword drawn. The calculation of alliances is a bloody combinatorics: how many possible betrayals between three brothers disputing an empire? The number exceeds the imagination. The enumeration of conspiracies is the chronicler's wager -- betting that the inventory will be complete, that no vertex will escape the graph. The Porteur de Maliettes, in his workshop, would draw this network on a wall. His chimeras are combinatorial fabrications, improbable assemblages -- like Carolingian alliances. In his maliettes, he carries proof that the conspiracy is never simple: each forgery begets another, each fraud a new vertex in the graph of fabrication. The risk of the cavalry at Fontenoy was merely one calculation among others. The chance of battles obeys an enumeration that no one masters. The sword cuts what number cannot resolve. When the combinatorics of alliances becomes too complex, when the edges of the graph cross in an inextricable knot, the armies charge. The stake is blood. The hoped-for gain is the empire. In my soldier's maliette -- for I carried one too, stuffed with parchments and supply calculations -- I kept the enumeration of the men under my command. Each cavalryman was a vertex. Each stake, an edge between life and the fabrication of victory. Conspiracy and number march together on the battlefield. The probability of survival is the only wager that counts.
