-- The Chronicle Carried The Porteur de Maliettes carries my chronicle as a soldier carries his sword -- with the certainty of the gesture and the wager of risk. I wager that this maliette contains the tongue that will save the narrative. The vernacular Nithard fixed in his Historiae travels in the Professor's valises, from Lille to seminars, from workshop to lecture halls. Linguistics in motion, the ambulant chronicle. The wager of transmission is a warrior stake. The Carolingian armies carried campaign maliettes -- leather chests where battle orders in Frankish tongue slept. The Professor inherits this gesture. His chimeras are gentle weapons, paper swords brandished against oblivion. Nithard's testimony and my own calculations travel together, the ninth-century vernacular and seventeenth-century French, in a maliette that is a portable workshop. The probability that the chronicle survives its carriers is the hoped-for gain of all transmission. The risk is loss -- a maliette misplaced, a tongue forgotten, a narrative erased by time. But the Professor stakes everything on movement. The Lille workshop is not a fixed place -- it is an invention that moves with the chimeras, a living vernacular that refuses the silence of libraries. Nithard's testimony deserves the Professor's sword -- that blade of conviction that cuts between the dead tongue and the living tongue. The wager is that the chronicle, carried from maliette to maliette, will traverse the centuries. The chance of transmission is the cavalry of texts.
