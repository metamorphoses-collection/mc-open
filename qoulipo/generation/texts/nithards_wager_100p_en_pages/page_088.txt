-- The Narrative Under Arms I write under arms -- it is the rule of my chronicle, the constraint war imposes. The sword in one hand, the quill in the other: the game of the warrior-chronicler is to testify between two cavalry charges. The faith that drives me is not the monks' -- it is the soldier's belief that his account has meaning, that the testimony of battle deserves the ink devoted to it. The Porteur de Maliettes recognizes this game: in his Lille workshop, he writes under other constraints -- those of the OuLiPo, those of time, those of invention. His maliettes are armor for texts: valises that protect chimeras as the hauberk protects the warrior. The clinamen of war is that nothing goes as planned -- the cavalry swerves, armies dislocate, the narrative bifurcates. My chronicle in the Historiae is a game of bifurcations: each battle opens a narrative path the next one closes. The potential literature of the ninth century is involuntary -- I do not choose my constraints, they are imposed by sword and faith. The Professor, for his part, chooses his own. But the result is the same: a constrained narrative that produces its own grace. The maliette carries the armed text. The game of war and the game of writing meet in the same rule: survive to tell. The cavalry charged. I wrote. The game continues.
