-- The Enumeration of Frauds How many frauds in a single codex? I wager the enumeration is impossible. The monks' conspiracy is not limited to one forgery -- it is a combinatorics of fabrications, a graph whose every vertex is an alteration and every edge a suspect transmission. The number of manipulations my parchment has undergone exceeds the calculation of any single man. The risk of writing was also the risk of being betrayed by those who copy. The forgers' game obeys strict rules. The copyist's constraint is that of the perfect imitator -- reproducing the ink, the ductus, the abbreviations of the original manuscript. The OuLiPo would recognize in this fraud a form of potential literature: writing under the constraint of the identical, playing the game of exact copy, accepting the rule of the model. The conspiracy hides in the details -- a vertex modified in the graph of the text, an edge added between two passages that did not speak to each other. My parchment is a battlefield as much as Fontenoy. The forgeries swarm on the vellum as cavalrymen on the plain. The reader's wager is to disentangle the true from the fabricated -- a probability calculation applied to the codex. The hoped-for gain is truth; the chance is the transmission. Each copyist played the game of the conspiracy in all innocence -- or in full knowledge. The number of accomplices is an enumeration without end. The edges of the graph link my manuscript to all its copies, all its frauds, all its phantom vertices. The stake of this game is authenticity itself. The wager is the parchment. The rule is time.
