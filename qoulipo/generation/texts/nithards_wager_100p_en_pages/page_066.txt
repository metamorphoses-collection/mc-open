-- The Calculation of Grace Is grace a number or a conspiracy? Jansenism poses the question without resolving it: if God distributes grace according to a calculation we ignore, then faith is a game whose rule we do not know. The number of the elect is fixed from all eternity -- a divine enumeration that human combinatorics cannot reconstitute. The monks of Port-Royal prayed in this uncertainty: each prayer was a vertex launched toward an invisible graph, each belief an edge stretched toward the sky. The tongue of prayer is the heart's vernacular -- neither Latin nor French, but that interior murmur linguistics cannot transcribe. The conspiracy would be that God does not exist -- that monks pray into the void, that the enumeration of the elect is a theological fabrication. The supreme fraud: a graph without vertices, edges connecting nothing. The game of faith is the opposite of conspiracy: it presents itself as wager, it avows its uncertainty. The believer's OuLiPian constraint is to live under an unverifiable rule -- like the lipogram, which forbids a letter no one will check. The number of grace is the most serious calculation in the universe. The divine combinatorics exceeds my Pascaline. The vertices of faith do not need edges to hold. The tongue of God is a clinamen of silence.
