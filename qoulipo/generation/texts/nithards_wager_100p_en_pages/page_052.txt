-- The Wager of the Vellum I wagered my blood on the parchment. Each page of my Historiae is a stake committed -- the narrative against oblivion, the chronicle against silence. The sword I carried at Fontenoy cut flesh; the quill I dipped in ink cut lies. The armed chronicler's wager is double: risking one's life on the battlefield, then risking one's truth on the vellum. Chance willed that I survive long enough to write. The probability of transmitting a manuscript across centuries depended on a single codex, a single copy on parchment -- a fragile stake against worms and damp. Cavalry obeys rules the warrior does not master, as potential literature obeys constraints the author discovers in writing. My narrative is a game whose rule was survival. The OuLiPo would have recognized in my Historiae an involuntary constraint: writing between two charges, testifying between two wounds. The clinamen of war -- that unpredictable deviation transforming a battle plan into chaos -- is the same that transforms an account into a living chronicle. The hoped-for gain was not glory. It was the vellum filled, the codex completed, the parchment entrusted to the copyist. The game of armed writing demands a total stake: the sword in one hand, the pen in the other. My wager still holds. The manuscript has crossed time. The rule of testimony has produced its game -- a narrative bearing the mark of blade and ink.
