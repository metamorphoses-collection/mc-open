-- The Chance of Blades The Porteur de Maliettes calculates chance. How many sword strokes at Fontenoy? The number is a vertigo -- thousands of blades, innumerable vertices in the graph of battle. The cavalryman's wager is a real-time calculation: the enemy advances, the probability of survival decreases, the hoped-for gain shrinks to the next instant. The Professor produces from his maliette a simulation -- the edges of the graph draw the charge. The warrior's faith is a number he does not know. The probability of divine grace on the battlefield is undecidable -- the monks of Port-Royal would have phrased it thus. The Professor understands this arithmetic of belief: in his chimeras, in the Lille workshop, he has learned that number and faith cohabit poorly. Calculation demands certainties; prayer accepts chance. The sword does not calculate -- it strikes. The Professor's maliettes contain graphs of battles. Enumerations of cavalrymen, edges linking strategic vertices, risk calculations. The military wager is the oldest of number games -- staking lives on territorial gain, committing armies on a probability of victory. The Professor enumerates and stores. His maliettes are chests of intellectual warfare. The sword of analysis cuts into number to extract meaning. Faith in calculation is the Professor's wager -- that belief that the enumeration of vertices reveals what intuition masks. The maliette closes on the bloody graphs. Chance awaits the next charge.
