-- Port-Royal Burns Port-Royal was destroyed with swords and royal orders. The chronicle of this destruction is a state conspiracy -- a fabrication of pious reasons to strike down the Jansenist prayer. I write it on this parchment that may not survive: Port-Royal's faith disturbed because it was too pure. The divine silence we sought in our cells was a weapon more dangerous than any cavalry. Did the monks who copied my Pensees know they were participating in a conspiracy despite themselves? The fabrication of a posthumous text is always partial fraud -- one recomposes, orders, betrays. The codex published is not the manuscript I left, those bundles of parchments pinned in the disorder of grace. Testimony is altered by those who claim to serve it. The chronicle of my thought is a recomposed narrative, an editorial conspiracy. The king's sword struck Port-Royal as Lothar struck at Fontenoy. Armies do not need to believe -- they obey. But faith resists even when the parchment burns. The chronicle of our destruction is written on scattered vellums, codices hidden in monastic maliettes. The anti-Jansenist conspiracy sought to erase even our prayer. Grace willed that the manuscripts survive. The fabrication of oblivion has failed -- the bones of our dead still speak, the relics of our belief circulate under wraps. The sword cuts the body, not faith. The parchment burns, but testimony remains. The war against prayer is the oldest conspiracy in the world.
