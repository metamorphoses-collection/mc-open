-- The Chronicle of Vertices The Porteur de Maliettes draws on the board the graph of Carolingian chronicles. Each vertex is an account, each edge a shared tongue. The number of testimonies from that era is meager -- the fingers of one hand suffice to enumerate them. Nithard, Thegan, the Astronomer: three vertices in a graph the sword has carved. The combinatorics of warrior narratives is a game of historical constraints. The vernacular is the thread linking these chronicles. Nithard's tongue is not Thegan's -- the nascent French of the Oaths contrasts with court Latin. The linguistic calculation reveals edges that the game of potential literature would recognize: constraints of register, rules of style that distinguish the graph's vertices. The OuLiPo would say each chronicler obeys an unconscious lipogram -- certain words are forbidden him by his tongue, his position, his sword. The Professor enumerates the battles mentioned in each account. The number of Fontenoy returns as an obsessive vertex -- the edge linking all testimonies. The cavalry of that June morning in 841 is the bloody game around which all Carolingian chronicle turns. The tongue of massacre is universal -- the vernacular of blood needs no translation. The swords speak an idiom that the count of the dead translates into numbers. The Professor's graph fills with vertices and edges. The enumeration of chronicles is an archivist's game -- the rule is to forget no account, no tongue, no sword stroke.
