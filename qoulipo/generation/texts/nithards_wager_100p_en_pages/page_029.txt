-- The Bones Enumerated The conspiracy around my bones begins with a number. How many fragments attributed to Nithard circulate in European reliquaries? The enumeration is a calculation that faith complicates -- each relic is a vertex in a graph of beliefs, each fabrication an edge between the sacred and fraud. The monks fabricated my bones as one fabricates a parchment -- with method, with prayer, with the hope that grace will authenticate the false. The sword that killed me -- for I died in combat, blade against blade, cavalry against cavalry -- left no trace on the bones that are venerated. The conspiracy lies in this absence: bones without wounds for a soldier who did nothing but fight. The number of inconsistencies forms a graph of suspicions whose vertices are the relics and whose edges are unanswered questions. The philologist's calculation and the forensic examiner's converge: fabrication is probable. My parchment, for its part, bears the traces of the sword -- not literally, but in the vocabulary of war, the armies described, the blood recounted. The codex of the Historiae is my true relic, the manuscript that faith should venerate rather than dubious bones. The monks' conspiracy substituted body for text, physical relic for intellectual parchment. The enumeration of this fraud is a calculation of vertices: how many false bones for how many true vellums? The edges of the graph link the conspiracy to belief, fabrication to prayer. The number of the duped is infinite. Faith, for its part, waits on the parchment for someone to read it.
