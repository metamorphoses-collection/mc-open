-- The Game of the Copyists I wager that the copyist who transcribed my Historiae played a game. The constraint of copying is that of the inverted lipogram -- nothing is forbidden, everything is obligatory. Reproduce every word, every letter, every nuance of the original tongue on a new parchment. The conspiracy of copying is a conspiracy of fidelity -- the fabrication of the same, the illusion of the identical. But the tongue trembles under the copyist's quill, and the Carolingian vernacular deforms imperceptibly. The game of textual conspiracy is a game of chance. The probability that the copyist reproduces exactly my manuscript is a wager the vellum does not guarantee. Each error is a clinamen -- a deviation of the tongue that creates a new text from the old codex. The fabrication of these variants is the hoped-for gain of time. The parchment evolves from copy to copy, from conspiracy to conspiracy. The Porteur de Maliettes would recognize in this game the OuLiPo's rule: constraint begets variation, copying begets invention. The vernacular of my Historiae has traversed the centuries transforming itself -- Carolingian French became medieval, then classical, then modern. At each copy, the parchment records a linguistic conspiracy -- the tongue that cheats on itself, the word that slides toward another meaning. The chronicler's wager is to stake on this fertile betrayal. The risk is the loss of original meaning. The gain is the living tongue, the perpetual game of the fabrication of French on the vellum of the centuries.
