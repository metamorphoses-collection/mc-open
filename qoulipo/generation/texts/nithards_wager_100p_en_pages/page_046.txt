-- The Charge of Words I wager that words charge like cavalry. Nithard's chronicle is a charge of tongue -- each sentence in vernacular is a sword hurled against oblivion. The game of warrior writing is that of the chronicler who knows his account is a weapon. Potential literature is born in this awareness: the text is a war game, constraint is a tactic. The armies of Carolingian words still charge in the pages of the Historiae. The French born in Nithard's testimony is a linguistic cavalry -- young words, spirited, barely tamed, launched on the vellum like cavalrymen on a plain. The chronicler's wager is that this charge will hold, that the tongue will survive the battle, that the vernacular will resist Latin. The hoped-for gain is French itself -- that tongue born of sword and game, of oath and constraint. The OuLiPian game is a renewed charge. The rule is the trumpet that launches the assault -- constraint organizes the cavalry of words, the clinamen is the deviation that surprises the enemy. The wager of potential literature is the same as Fontenoy's: staking on risk, committing the tongue to a charge whose outcome is uncertain. The chance of words, like the chance of blades, decides victory. The chronicle is the account of the charge. The sword is the game of the tongue. The vernacular is the army. The wager is the charge itself.
