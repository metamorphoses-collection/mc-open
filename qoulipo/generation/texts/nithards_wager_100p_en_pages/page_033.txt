-- The Lipogram of Battles The Porteur de Maliettes proposes a game: write the chronicle of Fontenoy without the word "death." A warrior lipogram, an OuLiPian constraint applied to the battle narrative. Nithard's tongue, deprived of its most natural vocabulary, would have to reinvent itself -- find a vernacular of loss that circumvents the obvious. The lipogram's conspiracy is that of ellipsis: fabricating meaning through absence. The sword itself becomes suspect in this game. If one cannot name what it produces, the blade loses its narrative function. Armies charge, cavalry gallops, but the unspeakable result transforms the chronicle into fabrication -- a narrative where the tongue's conspiracy hides what everyone knows. The Professor smiles: that is exactly what potential literature is. The clinamen of constraint reveals what the direct narrative concealed. Nithard's testimony, reread under this OuLiPian rule, becomes a linguistic conspiracy. Did the chronicler know he was suppressing certain truths? Did the Carolingian tongue have its own lipograms -- forbidden words, prohibited vernaculars? The Professor's game consists of seeking in the Historiae the traces of these hidden constraints, the involuntary fabrications of the tongue. The conspiracy is not always deliberate -- sometimes it is the tongue itself that conspires, the sword of words that cuts into the possible. The narrative is a game of rules, some visible, others buried in the chronicler's vernacular, awaiting the Professor to decipher them.
