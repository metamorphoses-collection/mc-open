-- The Workshop of Forgeries The Porteur de Maliettes opens the workshop of forgeries. In each maliette, a tampered parchment -- not through malice, but through game. The fabrication of chimeras obeys the same rule as the monks' conspiracy: imitate to invent, falsify to create. The number of forged manuscripts in the Middle Ages is a dizzying vertex in the graph of textual history. The enumeration of frauds fills libraries. The Professor counts the edges. Each conspiracy links an authentic codex to a fabricated one -- the combinatorics of alterations forms a network that calculation struggles to untangle. In his Lille workshop, the chimeras pile up like bones in a crypt. The OuLiPian game of constraint is not so different from monastic fraud: one imposes a secret rule, writes under constraint, fabricates a text that resembles the true without quite being it. Potential literature and the conspiracy share the same maliette. The parchment does not distinguish true from false. The vellum welcomes all inks, all copyists, all forgers. The number of apocryphal manuscripts is a calculation no one has ever completed -- the vertices of the graph multiply with each discovery, the edges cross in a labyrinth of fabrications. The Porteur de Maliettes smiles: it is the game that interests him, not the truth. The rule of the conspiracy is that of the OuLiPian constraint -- the clinamen applied to the codex. The enumeration of falsifications is a combinatorial art. Each maliette contains a possible world, a potential parchment, a forgery truer than the true. Invention is the only gain in this endless game.
