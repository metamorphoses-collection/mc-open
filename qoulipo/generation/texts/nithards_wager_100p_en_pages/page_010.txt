-- The Tongue in the Vellum The tongue I fixed on the parchment is not the one I spoke. The vernacular of the Oaths, as I transcribed it, is a calculation as much as a testimony -- each word counted, each vertex of nascent French set upon the vellum with the precision of a copyist who enumerates letters. Linguistics will later say that I invented a graph, a combinatorics of Romance syllables. I was merely noting what I heard, with the faith of the witness. The number of French words in the ninth century is meager. The edges of the lexical graph are sparse -- the calculation of possible combinations fits in a few folios of parchment. The Porteur de Maliettes, in his Lille workshop, would say that this scarcity is a richness. His chimeras are born of lack, his inventions of constraint. In his maliettes, he stores texts where the vernacular struggles against Latin as a child against a giant. The enumeration of the possible is the art of those who have almost nothing. My faith is not that of theologians -- it is the belief of the soldier who writes. I believe the tongue deserves the parchment, that the manuscript deserves the purest vellum, that the codex deserves the centuries. The number of prayers I said before each page is an invisible vertex in the graph of my Historiae. Ink is my grace; the parchment, my divine silence. The Professor understands this -- in his maliettes, he carries the same faith. The tongue is a number searching for itself, a combinatorics awaiting its copyist. The vellum is patient. The vernacular, like belief, always finds its words in the end.
