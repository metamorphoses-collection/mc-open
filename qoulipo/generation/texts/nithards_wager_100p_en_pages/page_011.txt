-- The Infinity of the Gain My wager rests on a calculation that mathematics alone cannot resolve. The hoped-for gain is infinite if God exists -- and the stake, finite. Number decides: one must wager. But the vertices of this theological graph are not abstract points -- they are lives, swords drawn, prayers in divine silence. The combinatorics of faith is not played on paper; it is played in the flesh. The game I propose is an existential constraint. The OuLiPo would say that my wager is a lipogram of unbelief -- I erase doubt as one erases a letter, and the remaining text is faith. The rule is simple: stake everything on a gain that calculation cannot guarantee. The enumeration of reasons to believe is infinite; that of reasons to doubt, equally. The edges of the graph oscillate between these two vertices. The potential literature of belief is an endless game. The sword adds a dimension to the wager. At Fontenoy, the cavalrymen staked their lives on the chance of the charge -- a warrior game whose hoped-for gain was survival and whose stake was the empire. The number of the dead is the retrospective calculation of risk. The probability of dying at Fontenoy was high; the probability of winning divine grace is undecidable. Both wagers share the same structure: an irreversible stake, an uncertain gain, a faith that precedes calculation. Armies and believers play the same game. The sword and the prayer are two edges of the same graph. The vertices are the living. The enumeration of the dead is the price of the game.
