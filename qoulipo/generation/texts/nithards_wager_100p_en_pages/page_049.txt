-- Ink and Blade Ink on the parchment is the blood of the tongue. I write it with the same hand that holds the sword -- the hand of the soldier-chronicler, the warrior-grammarian. The vernacular of the Oaths flows on the vellum as blood flows at Fontenoy -- same redness, same risk, same wager. The game of Carolingian writing is a game of blades and ink. The constraint of the nascent tongue is that of a French searching for its words on the battlefield. The parchment is the terrain, the sword is the pen, the cavalry is the grammar that charges. The linguistic wager is a warrior wager -- staking on the vernacular against Latin is staking the soldier's blade against the cleric's authority. The hoped-for gain is the autonomy of the tongue, its emancipation from the sacred codex. The risk is incomprehension. The game of my writing obeys rules I do not formulate but feel in the tongue. Potential literature would say I write under constraint -- the constraint of the witness, of the soldier, of the emperor's grandson. Every word in vernacular is a sword drawn in the game of the chronicle. The parchment records this combat of the tongue against itself -- the French that is born by cutting, the vellum that bleeds with the ink of the new. The wager of the linguistic clinamen is mine: the deviation that creates, the blade that liberates, the manuscript that invents its own rule. Ink is my only sword. The tongue is my only parchment. The game is my only war.
