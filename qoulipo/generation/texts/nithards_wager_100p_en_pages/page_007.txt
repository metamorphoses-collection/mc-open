-- The Bones of the Graph I wager that my bones will speak after me. My chronicle has said what I saw; the parchment has kept what the ink fixed. But the monks' conspiracy has added to the account a layer of fabrication that enumeration alone can reveal. How many hands have touched my manuscript? How many copyists have modified a vertex in the graph of my testimony? The calculation of these alterations is a wager on the fidelity of texts. The bones claimed to be mine -- discovered, exhumed, analyzed -- are a relic as uncertain as my codex. The conspiracy surrounds the dead as it surrounds manuscripts: the sacred is fabricated from remains. The fraud of relics and the fraud of parchments obey the same combinatorics -- a finite number of forgeries begets an infinite number of beliefs. The edges of the graph link my bones to my text in a network no one has wished to map. My chronicle, my Historiae, is an enumeration of betrayals and battles. Each page of the parchment adds a vertex to the graph of Carolingian history. The wager of writing is that of the chronicler who stakes his testimony against the conspiracy of time. The probability that the codex would survive intact was zero -- and yet fragments subsist. The risk of writing was never death, but the fabrication of a false account by those who copy. The manuscript is a stake in the monks' calculation. The hoped-for gain, for them as for me, is memory. The vertices of the graph are the dead. The edges, our chronicles.
