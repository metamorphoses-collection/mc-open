-- The Relic of the Tongue The conspiracy I denounce is that of the frozen tongue. A sacred Latin was fabricated, a tongue of faith that excluded the vernacular -- and this fabrication is a fraud as grave as that of forged bones. The parchment of Latin Gospel books is a linguistic relic whose sanctity is not self-evident. The game of potential literature is precisely to defy this fixity -- to write belief in every tongue, under every constraint. Faith does not belong to Latin. Port-Royal understood this -- we pray in French, we write our Provinciales in the tongue of the people, we refuse the conspiracy of the clerical tongue. The rule of our game is clarity: the lipogram of jargon, the constraint of transparency. Divine grace does not need sacred parchment -- it inscribes itself on the humblest vellum, in the most common tongue. The fabrication of the sacred through tongue is the subtlest of conspiracies. The copyist monks did not merely falsify manuscripts -- they falsified the relationship between faith and speech. By reserving Latin for the sacred codex, they fabricated a linguistic relic that excluded the majority of believers. The OuLiPian game is a reverse conspiracy: making the tongue accessible through constraint, opening the parchment to all vernaculars. The wager of faith in the common tongue is a clinamen -- that deviation that liberates. Prayer in French is a play on words against divine silence. The tongue of the people is the most sacred parchment.
