-- The Linguistic Conspiracy The tongue is the terrain of the subtlest conspiracy. I read Nithard's chronicle and see in it a linguistic fabrication -- not a forgery, but a deliberate construction of the vernacular. The chronicler chose his words as a general positions his swords: each term in Romance is a blade planted in Latin, each sentence in vernacular an edge in the graph of the new tongue. The number of French words in the Historiae is an enumeration the conspiracy of time has made difficult. How many authentically Carolingian words? How many vertices added by copyists? The monks' linguistic fabrication was superimposed on Nithard's original fabrication -- a layered conspiracy, a palimpsest tongue. The calculation of strata is a philological combinatorics where each edge links a word to its history. The swords of Carolingian armies spoke every tongue -- Frankish, Romance, Saxon. The cavalry of Fontenoy was a multilingual graph where each vertex understood a different dialect. The number of tongues on the battlefield is an enumeration the chronicle cannot complete. The linguistic conspiracy is that of an empire fragmenting -- Charlemagne's single tongue shatters into vernaculars as a sword shatters into shards. Each shard is a new vertex, each dialect an edge in the graph of fragmentation. The calculation of Carolingian tongues is the calculation of an empire's end.
