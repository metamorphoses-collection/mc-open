-- The Maliette of Relics The Porteur de Maliettes opens the valise of relics. Inside, the inventories: how many bones attributed to Nithard, how many parchments certifying their authenticity. The enumeration is damning -- the monks fabricated more proofs than needed. The conspiracy of faith is a combinatorial excess: too many relics for a single saint, too many vertices for a credible graph. The fabrication of false codices accompanied that of false bones -- each vellum bore a number, a date, a seal adding an edge to the network of fraud. The manuscript certifying the relics' authenticity is itself a forgery -- a mise en abyme of the conspiracy. The Professor counts the vertices on his fingers. In his Lille workshop, he learned that number guarantees nothing: a thousand fabricated proofs are not worth a single true testimony. The faith of believers who prostrated themselves before the false bones was sincere -- it is the graph that lied, not the prayer. The parchment certifying the relic bore ink too fresh, vellum too smooth. The Professor's maliette contains this lesson: the calculation of truth requires the modesty of number. Each vertex must be verified. Each edge must bear the weight of authentic belief. The combinatorics of faith cannot be reduced to the enumeration of bones.
