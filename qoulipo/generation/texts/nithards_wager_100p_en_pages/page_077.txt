-- The Five Threads Five threads weave this narrative -- I see them now as the five fingers of my hand that held the sword. The wager is the thumb: without it, nothing holds. The chronicle is the index: it points, it designates, it shows the truth of testimony. The tongue is the middle finger -- the longest, the most visible, the nascent French in the Strasbourg Oaths. The number is the ring finger: it bears the alliance of calculation and narrative, the vertices and edges of the graph linking each page. Faith is the little finger -- small but indispensable, the whispered prayer that holds the rest together. Five threads, five fingers, five intertwined probabilities. My chronicle in the Historiae is the account of a man who staked his life on five certainties: chance exists, testimony is necessary, the vernacular has the force of Latin, enumeration does not lie, grace is possible. The stake of each combinatorics is to weave these threads without breaking them. The risk is fraying -- that the tongue separates from faith, that number separates from the narrative, that the wager loses its stake. The probability that all five hold together is the true calculation of this text. The graph's vertices are the pages. The edges are the threads. The chronicler's belief is that the weaving will resist time.
