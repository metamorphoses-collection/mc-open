-- The Sestina of the Sword There exists a sestina of war -- a poetic form that rotates six words through six stanzas, as the sword rotates in the cavalryman's hand. The Porteur de Maliettes discovered it in his valises: a manuscript where OuLiPian constraints apply to the tongue of battles. The game is this -- take the words of conspiracy and submit them to the rule of rotation. Fabrication, fraud, forgery, bones, relic, monks: six terms that pivot like the blades of cavalry in formation. The warrior clinamen is that minimal deviation transforming the charge into rout. In his Lille workshop, the inventor of chimeras stores his sestinas like weapons: each maliette contains a game of constraints, a tongue shaped by the rule. The sword and the lipogram share the same edge -- they remove something to reveal what remains. The monks' conspiracy is a lipogram of truth: one removes the facts, keeps the legend. The vernacular of falsehood is an impoverished tongue. Potential literature knows this: constraint enriches where forgery impoverishes. The Professor's maliette contains this lesson. The game of the sword and the game of words obey the same linguistics: strike true, remove the useless, let the rule produce its effect.
