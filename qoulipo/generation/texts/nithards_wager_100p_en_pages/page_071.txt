-- The Chronicler Falsified What if my own chronicle were falsified? The chronicler's conspiracy would be the subtlest of all: a narrative presented as testimony but fabricated as a game. I tell the truth -- I saw Fontenoy, I counted the dead --, but the historian's wager is that one be believed. Does the number of my true statements exceed that of my omissions? The enumeration of facts reported and facts suppressed draws a perforated graph, missing vertices the reader cannot reconstitute. The narrative's combinatorics is a game of constraints: I choose what I show, I hide what I suppress. My chronicle's rule is that of the partial witness -- my clinamen is fidelity to Louis against Lothar. Is the fabrication of truth a conspiracy? The engaged chronicler's risk is being read as a forger. The stake of my Historiae is credibility -- each page is a wager on the reader's trust. The probability that my account is entirely true is low: all testimony is a calculation of edges, a selection of vertices. The chance of memory deforms the number of facts. The chronicler's involuntary conspiracy is believing in his own truth. The game of the Historiae is honest in its constraint: I say what I saw, I count what I could. The graph is incomplete. Every graph is.
