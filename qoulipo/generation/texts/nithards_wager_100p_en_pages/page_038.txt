-- The Chronicle of Grace My chronicle is a wager on grace. I write my Historiae on parchment because I believe -- without proof, without certainty -- that the witness's testimony is a form of prayer. The account of battle is an act of faith: one stakes one's ink on the vellum as one stakes one's soul on divine silence. The chance that makes a codex survive is grace applied to manuscripts. The chronicle's game is that of the chronicler who does not know if he will be read. The probability of reading is a wager -- the hoped-for gain is memory, the risk is total oblivion. The stake surpasses the parchment: it is the truth of testimony that is at play, the belief that facts deserve to be recorded. The chronicler's constraint is the witness's: say only what one has seen, wager only on experience. The rule is simple; the game is cruel. The copyist's faith extends my wager. He who recopies my codex on new vellum believes my account is worth fresh ink. This belief is a grace -- the original testimony receives, through the copy, a prayer of survival. The game of transmission is a game of faith: each copyist stakes on the parchment, each reader stakes on the codex. The chronicle's wager is a collective wager -- a common stake on the manuscript, a shared faith in the narrative. Divine silence never confirms. But the parchment continues. Grace is in the game. The gain is in the wager of believing that testimony has meaning.
