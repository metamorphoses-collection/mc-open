-- The Vernacular of the Conspiracy The Porteur de Maliettes opens the file "Vernacular of the Conspiracy." How many forgeries were written in French -- not in Latin, in French? The enumeration surprises: the tongue of conspiracy has changed with the centuries. Monks forged in Latin; modern forgers forge in vernacular. The chronicle of fraud is a linguistic chronicle -- the tongue of the false follows the tongue of power. The number of identified forged manuscripts is a calculation in progress: each vertex added to the graph of fabrication shifts the edges of knowledge. The combinatorics of conspiracy is that of the lying tongue -- each grammar has its flaws, each vernacular its telltale anachronisms. The Professor stores in his maliettes the proofs of this linguistics of the false: reproductions of falsified codices, analyses of ink and vellum. The Lille workshop is a laboratory where chimera and forgery regard each other in the mirror -- avowed fabrication and hidden fabrication. The Conspiracy of the Monks is the narrative of this confrontation: Nithard's chronicle as authentic text, the relics as fabricated texts, the maliette as possible text. The number of true vertices and false vertices draws the graph of truth -- a graph where the edges of conspiracy appear in red, those of the chronicle in black. The tongue decides. The vernacular does not lie for long.
