-- The Workshop's Wager I wager that the Porteur de Maliettes's workshop is a battlefield. The chimeras born in Lille are paper warriors, word-swords forged under constraint. The Professor's faith in his inventions is as fierce as mine on the morning of Fontenoy -- that belief that the gesture is worthwhile, that the blade deserves to be drawn, that the maliette deserves to be carried. The workshop's game is a game of gentle warfare. The OuLiPo's rules are the Professor's battle orders -- constraints that organize the charge, lipograms that channel the cavalry of words. The risk is silence -- the chimera that fails to take shape, the invention that falls flat like an unhorsed cavalryman. The hoped-for gain is the grace of the text, that secular prayer the clinamen sometimes grants. Faith in the game is a wager without insurance. The chance of creation is as unpredictable as the chance of battle. The Professor's sword is his pen -- that fine blade that cuts into the possible to extract the chimera. In his maliettes, armies of words await the charge. The wager is always the same: staking on constraint against freedom, on rule against chaos, on faith in the game against the unbelief of silence. The workshop is a Fontenoy of paper. The stake is literature. The bet is each chimera invented. The Professor draws his sword of words and charges. The prayer of the game rises in the workshop. The maliette awaits the text.
