-- The Conspiracy of Rules The Porteur de Maliettes reveals the conspiracy of rules. Every OuLiPian constraint is a conspiracy against natural tongue -- a fabrication that forces the vernacular to bend, to twist, to produce forms that ordinary French refuses. The lipogram is a sword that cuts into the dictionary. The OuLiPo's wager is that this violence produces beauty. The game of literary conspiracy is as old as the Strasbourg Oaths. When Nithard transcribes the vernacular, he imposes a rule on nascent French -- the constraint of the written over the oral, the fabrication of a literary tongue from the speech of cavalrymen. The conspiracy is structural: written tongue is always a fabrication, a natural fake, a consented fraud. The wager is that this fraud speaks truer than the true. The Professor plays the game of this linguistic sword. In his workshops, chimeras are incarnate constraints -- beings of tongue that exist only through the rule that engenders them. The conspiracy of invention is the only joyful conspiracy. OuLiPian fabrication does not hide its blade -- it displays it. Constraint is brandished like a sword, the wager is announced as a stake. The risk of potential literature is the same as that of all fabrication: the false can bore. But the hoped-for gain is the clinamen -- that deviation of the tongue that transforms constraint into grace, conspiracy into game, sword into pen. The Professor stakes on the chance of beauty. The rule cuts. The tongue reforms.
