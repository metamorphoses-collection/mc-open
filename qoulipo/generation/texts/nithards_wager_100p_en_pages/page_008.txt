-- The Wager of the Maliettes The Porteur de Maliettes crosses the city with his valises full of chronicles. There is a wager in every journey -- the risk of losing a maliette, the stake of the word to be transmitted. In the workshops of Lille, he has stored the chimeras; in the ZaZiPo seminars, he deploys them. Nithard's sword and Pascal's faith sleep side by side in the weary leather of the maliettes. The Professor does not calculate probabilities -- he stakes on the chance of encounters. The chronicle he carries is not his own. It is a borrowed account, a secondhand testimony, an invention born in the workshop of chimeras. But the faith he places in it is authentic -- that belief that the text deserves to be carried, that the stake of literature is well worth the charge of a cavalry. Nithard's armies had their maliettes too -- campaign chests where parchments and battle orders were stored. The sword and the maliette are the two arms of the literate warrior. The Porteur de Maliettes stops on the parvis of a church. Prayer rises, divine silence descends. The hoped-for gain is never certain -- neither in Pascal's wager, nor in Nithard's war, nor in the Professor's chimeras. But the stake is placed, always the stake is placed. The maliette contains the narrative; the narrative contains faith; faith contains the sword that cuts between true and false. Grace, if it exists, is in the movement -- carrying, transmitting, risking. The chronicle lives only if someone carries it. The Professor is the chance that makes texts survive. His wager is that of the ferryman.
