-- The Chimera of Strasbourg The Porteur de Maliettes showed me a chimera fabricated in his Lille workshop -- a creature half-sword half-tongue, a monster born from the crossing of the Strasbourg Oaths and the OuLiPian constraint. Nithard's chronicle becomes, in the Professor's hands, a game of combinations where the vernacular arms itself and the blade conjugates. This chimera intrigues me. The account of the Oaths, as the chronicler fixed it, is a tongue being born -- French babbles its first words amid the swords. The armies face each other and the cavalry awaits an oath in an idiom it barely understands. The game of historical linguistics is that of the archaeologist: recovering beneath the layers of the tongue the edges of the original vernacular. The Professor's maliette contains the tools for this excavation. The clinamen of the Strasbourg chimera is a verbal sword. In the Professor's OuLiPian game, the Carolingian chronicle deforms -- the tongue twists, the narrative reconfigures, testimony becomes potential literature. Constraint is the blade that carves the vernacular to extract new forms. The Lille workshop is a Strasbourg in reverse: instead of creating a tongue through political oath, one creates a game through literary constraint. The maliette contains both -- the sword and the tongue, the chronicle and the clinamen. The Professor plays with chimeras as Nithard played with words. The rule is the same: giving voice to the vernacular, arming the tongue with a blade.
