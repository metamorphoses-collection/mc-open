-- Invention in the Reliquary The Porteur de Maliettes opens an imaginary reliquary. Inside, neither bones nor relic -- a folded parchment, a chimera drawn in ink, a game of constraints. The conspiracy he denounces is not that of medieval monks, but that of our age: the fabrication of seriousness, the fraud of importance, the false weight of academic discourse. His maliettes are a response -- light, playful, full of potential literature. The Professor's faith is that of the player. He believes in constraint as others believe in relics -- with the same fervor, the same grace, the same suspension of doubt. The OuLiPian game is his prayer; the clinamen, his credo. In the Lille workshop, chimeras are acts of faith as much as acts of fabrication. The conspiracy is joyful: one fabricates text as one fabricates the sacred, with the same attention to vellum, to manuscript, to codex. The parchment of chimeras needs no reliquary. The maliettes suffice -- those weary valises carrying the Professor's inventions from seminar to seminar. The game consists of opening the maliette before an audience and producing what did not exist a second before. Fabrication is the hoped-for gain of the OuLiPian conspiracy: not fraud, but pure invention. Faith in the rule produces the text as belief produces prayer. The Professor closes the maliette. The silence that follows is that of grace -- or of the clinamen. The constraint has spoken. The parchment of chimeras returns to its leather reliquary. The game continues.
