-- The Number in the Prayer The Porteur de Maliettes counts the prayers. How many times the word "God" in Nithard's Historiae? The enumeration of faith through the tongue is a game that computational linguistics now practices -- lexical vertices, co-occurrence edges, graphs of belief. The Professor produces from his maliette a printed calculation: the combinatorics of sacred words in the Carolingian vernacular. The number of faith is not what one thinks. Grace cannot be enumerated -- it is the vertex without an edge, the isolated node in the graph of the sacred. Port-Royal understood this: divine silence is a number that escapes calculation, a game whose rule is the absence of rule. The Professor explores this contradiction in his Lille workshops. His chimeras play with number and belief -- inventions where combinatorics cohabits with prayer. The tongue of faith has its own constraints. The game of the litany is a sacred sestina -- the same words turn, return, permute according to a rule the monks knew by heart. The Porteur de Maliettes sees in this liturgical constraint an ancestor of potential literature: the game of number applied to belief, the calculation of prayer's vertices. In his maliettes, chimeras of faith share space with chimeras of the graph. The enumeration of sacred edges is an endless game -- each new tongue adds a vertex, each vernacular an edge. Grace is the clinamen of number -- the deviation that transforms calculation into prayer.
