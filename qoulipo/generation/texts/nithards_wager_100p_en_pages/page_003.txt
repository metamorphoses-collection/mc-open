-- The Monks' Codex My chronicle survived through the monks' hands, and it is those same hands that may have betrayed it. The conspiracy begins in the scriptorium: a copyist alters a word, another inserts a gloss, and the parchment one believes faithful is no more than a slow fabrication, a fraud as gentle as ink drying on vellum. I never wanted my Historiae to become a relic. I wanted an account, a testimony -- not an object of devotion. The Porteur de Maliettes, in his Lille workshop, would agree. His chimeras are avowed fabrications, inventions that do not pretend to authenticity. The monks, for their part, falsified believing they served faith. They retouched the codex as one restores a bone to make it a sacred relic -- pious fraud, the false in service of the true. My chronicle, passed from hand to hand, from maliette to maliette across the centuries, bears the traces of this silent conspiracy. The manuscript preserved today is a palimpsest of beliefs. Each copyist deposited his faith there like a new layer of ink. The parchment does not lie -- it is men who lie on the parchment. The Professor understands this: in his maliettes, he stores texts whose genealogy of alterations he knows. The monks' conspiracy is not a betrayal, it is a form of prayer. They fabricated the sacred from vellum and belief. My testimony, drowned in their glosses, awaits deliverance from the codex as one delivers a relic from its reliquary. The grace of a manuscript is to survive its forgers.
