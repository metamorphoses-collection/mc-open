-- The Pascaline of the Sacred My Pascaline does not count prayers -- it should. If faith is a number, then each belief is a vertex, each doubt a broken edge. The graph of the sacred is a game whose rule is silence: God does not speak, but the parchment bears His trace. The vernacular of prayer -- that interior tongue linguistics ignores -- is the oldest calculation in the world. Each word of the litany adds a node to the graph of belief. The enumeration of prayers is an infinite combinatorics: the monks of Port-Royal prayed for hours, each hour adding vertices to the architecture of the sacred. The vellum of the Book of Hours is a codex of number -- the illuminations count the mysteries, the margins count the angels. The sacred manuscript is a game of constraints the believer imposes: praying at fixed hours, in a fixed tongue, according to a fixed rule. The OuLiPo of the divine existed before the OuLiPo of men. The clinamen of faith is that deviation transforming prayer into ecstasy -- a linguistic slippage beyond the rule. The parchment of the sacred bears numbers my machine cannot add. The game of grace exceeds the calculation of reason. The vertices of belief do not need edges to form a graph -- faith holds them together as ink holds to vellum.
