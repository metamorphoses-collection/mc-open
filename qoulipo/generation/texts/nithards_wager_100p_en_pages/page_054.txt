-- The Grace of Vertices Grace falls like a prime number -- without reason, without prediction, by the sole will of God. Jansenism taught me that human calculation stops at the threshold of faith. Yet I count. I enumerate the vertices of the graph drawn by Nithard's chronicle: each episode of the Historiae is a node, each cross-reference an edge. The combinatorics of the Carolingian narrative is of a richness the chronicler did not suspect. The vernacular tongue of the Oaths adds vertices to the graph -- nascent French creates connections that Latin alone would not have allowed. The linguistics of Port-Royal seeks the universal grammar; I seek the universal number. The Porteur de Maliettes, from his Lille workshop, affirms that each chimera is a theorem disguised as narrative. His valises contain maliettes where calculations share space with inventions. The belief of the mathematician and that of the believer meet in the same gesture: counting what cannot be counted, believing in what cannot be proved. Faith is an isolated vertex in the graph of reason -- no edge connects it to the others, and yet it holds. Nithard's testimony, Port-Royal's prayer, the Professor's maliette: three paths toward the same impossible enumeration. The tongue is the number. The chronicle is the proof. Grace is the vertex one did not expect.
